## CUPA Agency

The **City of Roseville Fire Department** operates Roseville's own Certified Unified Program Agency (CUPA) — the only municipality in Placer County that does so. Facilities within Roseville city limits are regulated by the city CUPA; facilities outside city limits (unincorporated Placer County and other cities) fall under **Placer County Environmental Health**.

## Programs Administered

The Roseville Fire Department CUPA administers all five elements of California's Unified Hazardous Waste and Hazardous Materials Management Regulatory Program:

| Program | Authority |
|---------|-----------|
| Hazardous Materials Business Plans (HMBP) / Hazardous Materials Management Plans (HMMP) | Health & Safety Code §§ 25500–25545 |
| Hazardous Waste Generators & Tiered Permitting | H&SC §§ 25100 et seq. |
| Aboveground Storage Tanks (APSA) | H&SC §§ 25270 et seq. |
| Underground Storage Tanks (UST) | H&SC §§ 25280 et seq. |
| California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP) | H&SC §§ 25531 et seq. |

## Reporting Portal

All HMBP/HMMP submissions are made through the **California Environmental Reporting System (CERS)**: https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/

## Compliance Incentive Program

Businesses with no Class I violations (major deviation from Title 22 or hazardous waste generator requirements) in the prior 3 years qualify for a **25% discount on annual CUPA permit fees**.

## CalEPA Oversight

CalEPA's Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment (OEHHA) and DTSC oversee the statewide program. Roseville CUPA was formally evaluated by CalEPA in 2022 (findings published December 2022).
