## Adopted Code Edition

**2022 California Fire Code (CFC)** — based on the International Fire Code (IFC) 2021.

Adopted by **St. Helena Ordinance No. 22-12**, codified as **SHMC Chapter 15.36** ("California Fire Code Adopted"). The chapter is current through Ordinance 26-3 (February 24, 2026).

> NOTE (2026): The statewide 2025 California Building Standards Code (Title 24) became effective January 1, 2026. The City Attorney has raised the question whether SHMC 15.36 language referencing the "2022 CFC" requires re-adoption to track into the 2025 CFC. Verify current adopted edition with the SHFD Fire Inspector before advising on permit conditions.

## Findings Basis for Local Amendments (SHMC 15.36.030)

Local amendments are justified by St. Helena's specific climatic, geological, and topographic conditions:
- Narrow valley floor surrounded by **steep hill terrain** with areas very susceptible to wildland fires.
- Wildland fire is considered a significant threat to the community.
- Amendments affect fire apparatus access roads, residential development access requirements, and fire protection system requirements.

## Local Amendments to the 2022 CFC

### Fire Apparatus Access Roads (SHMC 15.36.100 / CFC Appendix D)

**CFC Section D103.2 — Maximum grade:** Amended. Fire apparatus access roads shall not exceed **16% grade**. Exception: lots of record established before January 1, 2014 that cannot feasibly meet this limit may exceed 16% with Fire Code Official approval, but shall not exceed 20%.

**CFC Section D103.5 — Gated entrances:** Amended. Minimum gate width shall be **16 feet for residential** occupancies or **20 feet for commercial** occupancies.

**CFC Section D103, Figure D103.1:** Amended to incorporate the City of St. Helena street, storm drain, and sidewalk standards drawings.

**CFC Section D107.1 — Residential developments with more than 30 dwelling units:** Amended to require **two separate and approved fire apparatus access roads** for residential developments with more than 30 dwelling units. [NOTE: An ordinance adopted in early 2026 removed this two-road requirement; a citizen referendum petition was filed but did not proceed to ballot. As of May 2026 the City Council voted 4-0 to reinstate the two-road requirement. Verify current SHMC 15.36.100 text before advising on site design — active litigation as of mid-2026.]

### Fire Protection Systems (SHMC 15.36.060 / CFC Chapter 9)

**CFC Section 901.1 / 903.2:** Amended. Approved automatic fire sprinkler systems are required in new buildings and in **qualified additions and remodels** at the following trigger thresholds:
- Any existing commercial or residential building where the **floor area of an addition (within any 3-year period) exceeds 50%** of the existing floor area (excluding attached garages or unconditioned spaces).
- When **an additional story is added**.
- Any **change of occupancy** to a more hazardous classification (per fire and life safety evaluation by the Fire Code Official and Building Code Official), including conversion to single-family residences, ADUs, bed-and-breakfast inns, lodging houses, or congregate residences.

**Exemptions from sprinkler requirement (SHMC 15.36.060):**
- Detached Group U private garages and carports < 1,000 sq ft and > 10 ft from adjacent structures.
- Detached pool houses and similar structures < 1,000 sq ft.

## Hazmat Permit Amounts / High-Piled Storage

SHMC Chapter 15.36 does not contain separate local quantity thresholds for hazmat storage or high-piled storage beyond those in the 2022 CFC itself. Zoning code SHMC **17.28.080** separately requires that hazardous and extremely hazardous materials comply with California Hazardous Materials Regulations and that an annual HMBP be filed with **Napa County DEH** (the countywide CUPA). CUPA threshold quantities triggering permitting are the state defaults: ≥ 55 gallons liquid, ≥ 500 lbs solid, ≥ 200 cu ft compressed gas at any time.
