## CUP Process (SHMC 17.05.020 and Common Procedures Chapter 17.04)

### Who Decides

| Permit Type | Decision Authority | Public Hearing |
|-------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Conditional Use Permit (CUP) | Planning Commission | Yes |
| Minor Use Permit (MUP) | Planning Director (staff level) | No (public notice only) |
| General Plan Amendment | City Council (final action) | Yes |

### Application Steps

1. **Pre-application meeting** — Recommended (contact Planning Department: 707-968-2792).
2. **Application submittal** — Submit to Planning Department at 1480 Main Street. Application prepared in compliance with SHMC 17.04.020 (Application process) / 17.05.020.
3. **Completeness review** — Staff issues completeness letter or incompleteness notice.
4. **Agency referrals** — 30-day review period; referrals to Building, Public Works, Fire, Police, applicable state and federal agencies (RWQCB, CalTrans, Fish & Wildlife, Army Corps), and community advisory committees.
5. **CEQA review** — Determine appropriate environmental review level (Exempt / ND / MND / EIR); prepare initial study as appropriate.
6. **Staff report and hearing notice** — Prepared and noticed for Planning Commission public hearing.
7. **Planning Commission public hearing** — Commission may approve, approve with conditions, or deny.
8. **Appeal period** — Decisions may be appealed within **14 days** of action to Planning Commission (for staff decisions) or to City Council (for Commission decisions).

### Processing Timelines

- Simple use permits (e.g., standard CUP): **3–5 months** average.
- Most discretionary permits: **4–6 months** once application is deemed complete.
- Complex projects (General Plan Amendment): **2+ years**.

## Hazardous Materials in Zoning (SHMC 17.28.080)

All industrial uses handling hazardous or extremely hazardous materials must comply with SHMC **17.28.080**, which requires:
1. Compliance with California Hazardous Materials Regulations, California Fire and Building Codes, and DTSC regulations.
2. Annual HMBP filing with **Napa County DEH** (the CUPA).
3. Submission of county CUPA approval copy to the Community Development Director.

This creates a zoning-level hook: industrial project approvals in St. Helena should confirm CUPA compliance as a condition.
