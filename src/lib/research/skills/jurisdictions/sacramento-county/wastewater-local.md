## POTW and Collection System Structure

Sacramento County's sewer system is a **two-district structure**:

| District | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sacramento Area Sewer District (SacSewer / SASD)** | Collection system — owns/operates trunk and lateral sewers throughout most of the county; issues sewer impact permits and wastewater discharge permits for the collection system |
| **Sacramento Regional County Sanitation District (Regional San)** | Treatment — owns/operates the **EchoWater Resource Recovery Facility** (NPDES No. CA0077682); sets and enforces all industrial pretreatment/local limits via the Regional San Consolidated Ordinance |

GOTCHA: SacSewer's Sewer Ordinance SDI-0079 explicitly states (§ General Provisions): "The District collects wastewater and diverts it to the conveyance and treatment systems of the Sacramento Regional County Sanitation District (Regional San) and therefore must enforce **all the provisions of the Regional San Consolidated Ordinance** in addition to the provisions of the SASD Sewer Ordinance." Industrial discharge limits live in the **Regional San Consolidated Ordinance**, not in the SASD ordinance.

## Permitted vs. Prohibited Discharges (SASD SDI-0079 § 2.2–2.4)

**Permissible:** Wastewater that complies with SDI-0079, the Regional San Consolidated Ordinance, and any permit conditions; all applicable impact fees and rates must be paid.

**Prohibited activities (§ 2.3):**
- Discharge into manholes, cleanouts, or other openings without specific authorization
- Damaging or tampering with the collection system
- Obstructing flow
- Creating pressurization of laterals
- Conditions endangering health/safety

**Prohibited discharges (§ 2.4) — SASD-specific additions:**
- Flows exceeding capacity or customary quantities for the permitted use
- Stormwater, surface water, pool water, groundwater, roof runoff, or subsurface drainage
- Fats, oils, and grease (FOG) causing build-up in any collection system portion
- Trucked and hauled wastes
- Substances causing excessive foaming
- Liquids or vapors with temperature > **150°F**
- Materials obstructing flow or impairing collection system performance
- Cementitious materials
- Corrosive materials causing structural damage
- Chemicals creating contamination in pipe, soil, or groundwater
- Garbage > **¼-inch** in any dimension

Note from compliance report context: **hazardous waste and wastewater with pH < 5 or ≥ 12.5 cannot be discharged** (per SacSewer enforcement practice referencing SDI-0079 and Regional San ordinance).

## Industrial Wastewater Discharge Permit (SASD § 3.3)

A **Wastewater Discharge Permit (WDP)** is required when discharge volume, rate, or characteristics—as determined by the District Engineer—warrant it. The WDP is issued by SacSewer (which acts as enforcement arm for Regional San pretreatment rules).

**WDP Application requirements (§ 3.3):**
- File application on District-provided form before commencing discharge

**Temporary WDP (§ 3.3.1):** Application at least 30 days before discharge; renewal 30 days before expiration.

## Local Discharge Limits

Local limits (technology-based and water quality-based) are set by **Regional San** in the Regional San Consolidated Ordinance (effective June 9, 2023). SacSewer enforces these limits on all users discharging to the SASD collection system.

The Regional San consolidated ordinance is the controlling authority for all concentration-based discharge limits (pH, BOD, TSS, metals, organics). The SASD SDI-0079 adds collection-system-specific prohibitions (listed above) on top of those limits.

**Key reference documents:**
- Regional San Consolidated Ordinance (effective June 9, 2023): https://www.regionalsan.com/ordinance (redirects to sacsewer.com/ordinance — site intermittently down; call SacSewer Wastewater Source Control Section for current PDF)
- EchoWater NPDES permit (Order R5-2021-0019 / NPDES No. CA0077682) — sets the plant effluent limits that back-calculate local limits. Final effluent limits effective May 9, 2023: BOD5 10 mg/L monthly avg / 15 mg/L weekly avg; TSS 10/15 mg/L; Copper Total 7.4 µg/L monthly; Cyanide Total 11 µg/L monthly; Ammonia-N 2.1 mg/L monthly (May–Oct) / 2.4 mg/L (Nov–Apr); Nitrate+Nitrite 10 mg/L monthly.

NOTE: Specific local limits tables (concentrations at the discharge point) were not publicly retrievable from the SacSewer website during this fetch (site intermittently offline). Per the SacSewer July 2024 semi-annual pretreatment report, no changes to local limits occurred during Jan–Jun 2024. Contact Wastewater Source Control Section at (916) 876-5287 for current local limits table.

## Grease / FOG Interceptors

Grease interceptors are required for food service establishments and any facility capable of discharging significant FOG amounts, including restaurants, cafes, fast food, pizza, delis, sandwich shops, schools, churches, nursing homes, day care centers with kitchens. All grease interceptors must be designed per the California Plumbing Code; maintenance is at sole expense of owner. (SDI-0079 § 2.6–2.7)

## Dental Amalgam (40 CFR Part 441)

SacSewer enforces EPA's Dental Amalgam Rule (40 CFR Part 441). Dental practices must submit a One-Time Compliance Report to SacSewer Wastewater Source Control Section, 10060 Goethe Rd., Sacramento CA 95827 (fax: 916-854-9286). pH constraints for cleaning products: must not have pH < 6 or > 8.

## Sewer Impact Permits (SDI-0079 § 4)

A **Sewer Impact Permit** is required before any new connection or change triggering additional flow capacity. Categories: Single-Family, Multi-Family, Commercial, Industrial, Groundwater Remediation, Non-Defined Commercial. Fees based on category and location within District service area (Relief Area vs. Expansion Area). Impact fees payable prior to building permit issuance for residential; prior to connection for commercial/industrial.
