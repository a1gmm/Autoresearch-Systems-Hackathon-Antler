## Governing Code

**Nevada County Code, Title 12 — Zoning Regulations** (formerly called the Land Use and Development Code/LUDC). Title 12 governs land use in the unincorporated county; the incorporated cities (Grass Valley, Nevada City, Truckee) have their own zoning codes.

## Industrial Base Zoning Districts

Nevada County's zoning code establishes 21 base zoning districts. Three are industrial:

| Abbreviation | District Name | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **BP** | Business Park | Light-industrial mixed: manufacturing, distribution, processing, service, and R&D uses normally associated with light industries; a Development Permit (DP) is required for light industrial uses in BP. |
| **M1** | Light Industrial | Light assembly and production; all activities conducted within an enclosed building; must not generate dust, fumes, odors, or other emissions detectable from outside the building; warehousing only if related to a primary commercial use on site. |
| **M2** | Heavy Industrial | More intensive than M1; allowed uses may generate greater impacts on adjacent land uses, public facilities, and the environment. |

Allowable uses and permit requirements for industrial districts are found in **Table 12.02.050.D — Industrial Districts Allowable Uses and Permit Requirements** (Nevada County Code § 12.02.050).

## Permit Tiers

Nevada County's Title 12 uses three permit tiers for uses within zoning districts:

| Code | Permit Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Allowed | Subject to zoning compliance and building permit issuance only |
| **DP** | Development Permit | Administrative discretionary review; per § 12.05.050 |
| **UP** | Use Permit (CUP) | Conditional Use Permit; per § 12.05.060 |
| **NP** | Not Permitted | — |

Most light-industrial uses in BP require a DP; more intensive uses in M1/M2, uses with external emissions, outdoor operations, or large commercial projects go to a UP before the Zoning Administrator or Planning Commission.

## Use Permit (CUP) Process

The Use Permit process is governed by **Title 12, § 12.05.060**. Key findings required for approval:

1. The property is adequate in size and shape for the proposed use.
2. Roads are adequate for the proposed use.
3. The proposed use will not have a significant adverse effect on surrounding properties or their permitted uses.
4. Adequate services (water, sewer, fire) are available to support the use.
5. The proposed use is consistent with the General Plan.

**Small commercial/industrial projects** are reviewed by the **Zoning Administrator**; campgrounds, large commercial projects, mining operations, and subdivisions go to the **Planning Commission**.

Applications are submitted to the Planning Department. Filing fees must be paid at submittal; additional costs may include road impact fees, environmental study preparation, and Fire District review fees.

## Notes for Industrial EHS Siting

- Nevada County is predominantly rural; industrial-zoned land (BP/M1/M2) is limited in extent and concentrated around community region boundaries (e.g., Grass Valley/Nevada City area, Penn Valley).
- The Loma Rica area has a specific Industrial Area Plan: https://www.nevadacountyca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/14841/Loma-Rica-Industrial-Area-Plan-PDF
- Any use generating noise detectable outside the building that exceeds General Plan noise standards for the Commercial designation is prohibited, even in industrial zones.
- The county's WUI overlay (wildland-urban interface) affects site development standards for any industrial project in or near SRA; fire clearance review is a standard condition of approval in fire districts.
