> Nevada City operates its own POTW (Publicly Owned Treatment Works). The City maintains the main sewer collection system; the Nevada City Waste Water Treatment Plant (NCWWTP) is a small municipal facility discharging to Deer Creek. Industrial pretreatment program status is unconfirmed online — see VERIFY note below.

## Nevada City Waste Water Treatment Plant (NCWWTP)

- Constructed 1949 in Deer Creek canyon; capacity 0.699 mgd, average flow approximately 0.460 mgd, peak flood capacity 1.200 mgd
- Treatment train: grit removal → sequencing batch reactors (biological) → sand filtration → pH adjustment → chlorination/dechlorination
- Discharge: treated effluent to **Deer Creek** (tributary to Yuba River below Engelbright Dam)
- NPDES permit: issued by the **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (Region 5)**; Order No. R5-2002-0050 was the permit on record as of 2002 [VERIFY current permit order number and expiration with the Regional Water Board at https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/centralvalley/]

## Industrial Pretreatment Program

- **No industrial pretreatment program was confirmed online for Nevada City.** The NCWWTP is a small (~0.7 mgd) municipal POTW serving a rural city of approximately 3,150 residents. At this scale, EPA's pretreatment regulations (40 CFR Part 403) may not require a locally-administered Industrial Pretreatment Program if the facility does not receive significant industrial flows.
- The city's public-facing wastewater page does not mention an industrial pretreatment program, IWD permits, or categorical industrial user requirements.
- [VERIFY — confirm with Brandon Pierce at (530) 265-8668 or brandon.pierce@nevadacityca.gov whether the NCWWTP operates under an approved pretreatment program. If industrial discharges are proposed, verify directly with the City and the Central Valley Regional Water Board whether a pretreatment program applies.]
- For context: if no local pretreatment program exists, industrial dischargers may still be subject to federal categorical pretreatment standards (40 CFR Parts 405–471) directly, and would need to coordinate with the Regional Water Board.

## Sewer Lateral Responsibility

- City maintains main sewer lines
- Property owner is responsible for the sewer lateral (the line connecting the property to the City's main), even where the lateral crosses other properties or runs within the street right-of-way
