## Wastewater Authorities

Nevada County is predominantly rural; the vast majority of the unincorporated area is served by individual septic/OWTS systems, not public sewers. Public sewer service exists only in specific suburban districts.

### County Sanitation Districts — Nevada County Department of Public Works

The **Nevada County Department of Public Works** operates **Nevada County Sanitation District No. 1**, which serves a collection of smaller suburban communities in western Nevada County (not the entire county). Known service zones include: Penn Valley, North San Juan, Cascade Shores, Lake Wildwood, Lake of the Pines, Gold Creek, Mountain Lakes Estates, Eden Ranch, Dark Horse, Higgins Village, and Valley Oak Court. Total combined permitted treatment capacity across all zones is approximately **1.245 MGD** (well below the 5 MGD federal pretreatment program threshold).

### City of Grass Valley — Municipal POTW

The City of Grass Valley owns and operates its own **Grass Valley Wastewater Treatment Plant**, which discharges treated effluent to Wolf Creek (tributary to Bear River). Permitted average dry weather capacity is approximately **2.78 MGD** (based on 1998 expansion to 1.84 MGD + subsequent capacity). The facility is regulated by the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board under NPDES General Order R5-2017-0085-019.

**Contact — Grass Valley Public Works:**
https://www.grassvalleyca.gov/public-works

### Town of Truckee and Nevada City

The Town of Truckee and City of Nevada City each have separate wastewater/sewer systems; see city-level sub-jurisdiction files for those.

## Sewer Use Ordinance / Sanitation District Code

The county's wastewater rules are codified in the **Nevada County Sanitation District Code** (a separate code from the Nevada County Code/Title 12), administered by the Department of Public Works. The document is available on the county's DocumentCenter but was not directly accessible online at time of research.

**Sanitation District Code (DocumentCenter link — authentication required):**
https://nevadacountyca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/53358/Sanitation-District-Code

Key provisions confirmed through search extracts:

- **Industrial waste discharge prohibition:** Industrial wastewaters shall not be discharged into District sewerage facilities without authorization. Specific prohibitions include:
  - Gasoline, benzene, naphtha, solvents, fuel oil, or any flammable/explosive liquid, solid, or gas
  - Toxic or poisonous solids, liquids, or gases at quantities that may harm humans, animals, or the environment, interfere with treatment processes, or cause a public nuisance
  - Waste with pH below 6.0 or with corrosive characteristics that may injure operations personnel
- **Connection permits:** Sewer Connection Permits are required to connect to any Nevada County Sanitation zone; application must include a detailed site plan with sewer line location, connection point, cleanouts, and backflow devices.

**Ordinances & Sewage Disposal Technical Advisory Group page:**
https://nevadacountyca.gov/2486/Ordinances-Sewage-Disposal-Technical-Adv

## Industrial Pretreatment Program

**No formal federal pretreatment program (40 CFR Part 403) is required or evident for Nevada County Sanitation District No. 1** because the combined treatment capacity is ~1.245 MGD, substantially below the 5 MGD federal threshold. The Grass Valley WWTP (~2.78 MGD) is also below the 5 MGD threshold. Neither facility appears to operate a federally-required Industrial Pretreatment Program.

The Lake of the Pines WWTP NPDES permit (Order R5-2015-XXXX, NPDES No. CA0081612, issued by Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board) contains a standard Provision requiring compliance with any future federal pretreatment standards (CWA § 307), but does not establish an active pretreatment program.

[VERIFY — No active IWP/pretreatment program found online. Industrial dischargers should contact Nevada County Public Works or Grass Valley Public Works directly before discharging to confirm current requirements.]

## Individual Sewage Disposal (Septic)

For properties not served by a public sewer, on-site sewage disposal is governed by:
- Nevada County Code, **Chapter VI (Sewage Disposal)** of the Land Use and Development Code — sets minimum design, construction, installation, operation, and maintenance standards for OWTS within the unincorporated county.
- Administered by the **Nevada County Department of Environmental Health** (within the Community Development Agency).
