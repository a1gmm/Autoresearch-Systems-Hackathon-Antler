## CUPA Agency

**Nevada County Environmental Health Department** (within the Nevada County Community Development Agency) is the **sole Certified Unified Program Agency (CUPA)** for the entire county. No city-run CUPA and no Participating Agency (PA) were identified; the county appears to administer the Unified Program countywide, covering all incorporated jurisdictions (City of Grass Valley, City of Nevada City, Town of Truckee) as well as the unincorporated area.

> This is typical for rural/small counties. Businesses in Grass Valley, Nevada City, and Truckee all submit to the county CUPA — not to a city agency.

## Unified Program Elements Administered

Nevada County Environmental Health administers all six Unified Program elements:

1. **Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP)** — hazmat inventory and emergency response plans; reported via **CERS** (California Environmental Reporting System) at https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/
2. **Hazardous Waste Generator Program** — oversight of conditionally exempt, small-, and large-quantity generators
3. **Underground Storage Tanks (UST)** — permits, inspections, and leak prevention
4. **Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA)** — spill prevention plans for qualifying aboveground tanks
5. **Hazardous Materials Release Response Plans and Inventories** (part of HMBP above)
6. **Household Hazardous Waste (HHW)** — county HHW collection program

## Local Hazmat Ordinance

No separate local hazardous materials ordinance beyond the CUPA program was found online. The CUPA operates under California Health & Safety Code Division 20, Chapter 6.95 (Hazardous Materials Release Response Plans and Inventory) and the unified program chapters (HSC § 25404 et seq.).

## Notes

- CERS is the single reporting portal for HMBP; all regulated businesses in Nevada County must submit through CERS regardless of city vs. unincorporated location.
- The county does not appear to have a separate Participating Agency arrangement for any of its incorporated cities, so EHS practitioners dealing with Grass Valley or Nevada City businesses should contact Nevada County Environmental Health, not the cities.
