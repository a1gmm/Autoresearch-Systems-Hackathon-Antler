> Long Beach is a **full CUPA** — NOT under LA County CUPA. The program is split between two city departments: Health (HazWaste/HMBP/UST/APSA) and Fire (CalARP). Facilities may need to coordinate with BOTH. CalEPA CUPA ID: 1030.

## CUPA Identity

- **CUPA type:** City-level full CUPA, certified by CalEPA (NOT LA County CUPA)
- **CalEPA CUPA directory ID:** 1030
- **CalEPA directory URL:** https://cersapps.calepa.ca.gov/Public/Directory/RegulatorDetails/1030

## HMBP Program Details

- **Submission system:** California Environmental Reporting System (CERS) — https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/
- **Statutory authority:** California Health and Safety Code Chapter 6.95, Sections 25500–25520; CCR Title 19, Division 2, Chapter 4.
- **LBMC reference:** Title 8 (Health and Safety); also Title 18, Chapter 18.48 (Fire Code) — the HMBP program established 1986 per CUPA programs.
- **Who must comply:** Any business storing, using, or handling hazardous materials at or above threshold quantities:
  - Liquids: ≥ 55 gallons
  - Solids: ≥ 500 pounds
  - Compressed gases: ≥ 200 cubic feet
  - Extremely hazardous substances above threshold planning quantities
- **Annual obligation:** Review, update, and recertify the HMBP Plan annually via CERS.
- **Update triggers:** Material quantities increase ≥100%; new undisclosed materials; address/ownership changes; significant operational changes — each triggers 30-day filing requirement.
- **Required CERS content:** Hazardous materials inventory; emergency response/contingency plan; updated site map; employee training records.
- **Release reporting:** Report to LBCUPA@longbeach.gov and California OES Warning Center; call 911 for emergencies.

## Hazardous Waste Generator Program

- All HazWaste submissions via CERS.
- Program covers EPA identification numbers, manifest compliance, transporter requirements, generator consolidation, used oil/filter management, universal waste, spent lead-acid batteries.
- Contact: Environmental Health Bureau at (562) 570-4132.

## Relationship to LA County CUPA

Long Beach operates its own independent CUPA — the LA County CUPA (Fire Department, Hazardous Materials Division) has **no jurisdiction** inside Long Beach city limits. Long Beach facilities file with LBCUPA, not LA County.

## Relationship to Fire Prevention Bureau

Facilities requiring CalARP Risk Management Plans submit to USEPA, and the Long Beach Fire Prevention Bureau serves as the Local Implementing Agency (LIA) for CalARP. Facilities above CalARP thresholds must coordinate with Fire (562-570-2563) separately from the Health-administered HMBP.

**GOTCHA:** A single industrial facility may require:
1. HMBP filed in CERS → reviewed by Health Environmental Health Bureau
2. Hazardous Waste Generator registration in CERS → Health
3. UST permit → Health
4. CalARP Risk Management Plan → Fire Prevention Bureau
5. CFC Hazmat Operational Permit → Fire Prevention Bureau (see fire-code-local.md)
