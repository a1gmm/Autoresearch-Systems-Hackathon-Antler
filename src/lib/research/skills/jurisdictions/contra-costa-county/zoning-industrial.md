## Zoning Authority

**Agency:** Contra Costa County Department of Conservation and Development (DCD), Application & Permit Center.
**Applicable area:** Unincorporated Contra Costa County only. Each incorporated city has its own zoning code.
**Zoning code:** County Ordinance Code, **Title 8 — Zoning**.
**GIS/parcel zoning lookup:** https://gis.cccounty.us/Html5//index.html?viewer=CCMAP

## Industrial Zone Districts — Overview

Contra Costa County uses four manufacturing/industrial base zoning districts (County Ordinance Code, Division 84):

| District | Chapter | Description |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| C-M | Ch. 84-56 | Controlled Manufacturing District — light manufacturing, service industrial, some retail |
| L-I | Ch. 84-58 | Light Industrial District — warehousing, light manufacturing, research/lab |
| W-3 | Ch. 84-60 | Controlled Heavy Industrial District — moderate heavy industrial; 2024 Zoning Update Report recommends merging into H-I |
| H-I | Ch. 84-62 | Heavy Industrial District — heavy manufacturing, chemical processing, refinery-related |

Additionally, **Chapter 84-63** governs Land Use Permits for Development Projects Involving Hazardous Waste or Hazardous Materials — a cross-cutting layer applicable within any zoning district.

**Note:** Contra Costa County does not use M-1/M-2 designations. The local industrial designations are L-I and H-I (plus C-M and W-3).

## Chapter 84-56 — C-M (Controlled Manufacturing District)

General commercial-manufacturing zone permitting light industrial, light manufacturing, and service-commercial uses. Used in transitional areas between commercial and industrial uses. Development standards and use lists in Ch. 84-56.

## Chapter 84-58 — L-I (Light Industrial District)

**Ordinance Code chapter:** Ch. 84-58, Division 84, Title 8.
**Purpose (§ 84-58.202):** Accommodate warehousing, light manufacturing, research and laboratory uses, and other light industrial activities while protecting nearby sensitive land uses.

**Article structure:**
- Art. 84-58.2: General Provisions (§ 84-58.202 — general purpose and standards)
- Art. 84-58.4: Uses (§ 84-58.402 — permitted by right; § 84-58.404 — requiring Land Use Permit)
- Art. 84-58.6: (Development standards — setbacks, lot coverage, etc.)
- Art. 84-58.8: Building Height (§ 84-58.802 — maximum height)

**Uses:**
- **Permitted by right (§ 84-58.402):** Light manufacturing, warehousing, distribution, research and development, laboratory uses, contractor's yards, automotive repair, and similar light industrial activities.
- **Requiring Land Use Permit (§ 84-58.404):** Uses with potentially greater impact on surroundings — e.g., certain hazardous materials storage/use above specified thresholds (see also Ch. 84-63), large-scale truck terminals, and other uses identified in the section. Land Use Permit (LUP) required per Ch. 82-6 procedures.

The 2024 Zoning Update Report (June 2024) notes that the current Code presents L-I uses as lists (permitted vs. LUP-requiring) rather than use tables, and recommends transitioning to a use-table format in a future code update.

## Chapter 84-62 — H-I (Heavy Industrial District)

**Ordinance Code chapter:** Ch. 84-62, Division 84, Title 8.
**Purpose:** Accommodate heavy manufacturing, chemical processing, petroleum refining, and other intensive industrial uses.

**Article structure:**
- Art. 84-62.2: General Provisions (§ 84-62.202 — general purpose and standards)
- Art. 84-62.4: Uses (permitted by right and requiring Land Use Permit)

**Uses (§ 84-62.4):**
- **Permitted by right:** Heavy manufacturing, chemical processing, petroleum refining, refineries and chemical plants (subject to ISO requirements under Ch. 450-8 if RMP-obligated), tank farms, bulk storage of flammable/combustible liquids (in conforming zones per fire code § 5704.2.9.6.1), and similar intensive industrial uses.
- **Requiring Land Use Permit:** Uses with heightened environmental or community impact — specific uses identified in § 84-62.4 include activities requiring HMBP or CalARP filings above certain thresholds, and facilities potentially subject to the Industrial Safety Ordinance (Ch. 450-8). See also Ch. 84-63 for LUP requirements triggered by hazardous materials/waste.

The 2024 Zoning Update Report (Figure 7) shows H-I use regulations are presented as a list format; the report recommends a future conversion to use tables for clarity.

## Chapter 84-60 — W-3 (Controlled Heavy Industrial District)

**Ordinance Code chapter:** Ch. 84-60, Division 84, Title 8.
**Status:** The 2024 Zoning Update Report recommends **eliminating W-3** as a separate district (it is rarely applied) and incorporating relevant W-3 regulations into the H-I district. Until such update is adopted, W-3 remains a live zoning designation.
**General provisions (§ 84-60.202):** Controlled heavy industrial uses; more restrictive environmental controls than H-I; specific use list in the chapter.

Source: http://contracostaco-ca.elaws.us/code/oc_title8_div84_ch84-60_art84-60.2

## Chapter 84-63 — LUP for Hazardous Waste/Materials Projects

Any development project in any zoning district involving **hazardous waste or hazardous materials** above specified thresholds requires a Land Use Permit under **Chapter 84-63**. This is a cross-cutting overlay — even uses permitted by right in L-I or H-I may need a Ch. 84-63 LUP if they trigger the hazardous materials thresholds.

## Land Use Permit (LUP) — Chapter 82-6

**Name:** "Land Use Permit" (LUP) — Contra Costa County's equivalent of a Conditional Use Permit (CUP). Do not use the term CUP; the local term is LUP.

**Governing chapter:** County Ordinance Code, **Chapter 82-6** (Division 82, General Regulations, Title 8).

**Decision authority:** Zoning Administrator hears and decides LUP applications. Appeals go to the Board of Zoning Adjustments (BZA) and then to the Board of Supervisors.

**When required in industrial districts:**
- Uses listed in § 84-58.404 (L-I LUP uses) or § 84-62.4 (H-I LUP uses).
- Any development project involving hazardous materials/waste above thresholds (Ch. 84-63).
- Certain modifications or expansions of existing non-conforming industrial uses.
- Specific activities in L-I or H-I that involve HMBP or CalARP obligations (cross-reference with CUPA requirements).

**Application portal:** ePermits Center — accessible at https://www.contracosta.ca.gov/7947 (Application & Permit Center online portal).

## Development Standards — Key Parameters

Development standards by district are in the respective articles (Art. 84-58.6 for L-I, Art. 84-62.X for H-I). Specific standards from publicly accessible text:
- **L-I maximum building height (§ 84-58.802):** Published in Ch. 84-58, Art. 84-58.8. Check current code for numeric value; standard industrial height limits in CA typically 45–65 ft absent special provisions.
- **Setbacks:** Addressed in Ch. 82-12 and Ch. 82-14 (Yards) for general county-wide minimums; district-specific adjustments in each chapter.

## 2024 Zoning Code Update Status

Contra Costa County published a Zoning Update Report in June 2024 (Envision Contra Costa 2040 process) recommending substantial code modernization, including:
- Replace use lists with use tables for all districts.
- Eliminate W-3 district; merge into H-I.
- Split the existing Land Use Permit into "Major Use Permit" and a second tier (minor/ministerial) permit.
- Update industrial use lists for contemporary land use types.

As of the fetched date, the existing Chapter 84-58 (L-I) and Chapter 84-62 (H-I) remain the operative code. Monitor DCD for adoption of updated zoning code per the General Plan 2045 update.
