## Adopted Code Edition

**Current adopted code:** 2025 California Fire Code (California Code of Regulations, Title 24, Part 9), based on the 2024 International Fire Code.

- **Adopting ordinance:** Ordinance No. 2025-14 (County of Contra Costa / Crockett-Carquinez FPD / Contra Costa County FPD)
- **Superseded:** Ordinance No. 2022-34 (which adopted the 2022 CFC) — expressly repealed by Ordinance 2025-14.
- **Effective:** 30 days after passage, operative no earlier than January 1, 2026.
- **Codified at:** County Ordinance Code **Division 722, Chapter 722-2** (Fire Code).
- **AHJ:** Contra Costa County Fire Protection District (Con Fire) — also covers Crockett-Carquinez FPD. Con Fire absorbed the former East Contra Costa FPD on 2022-07-01.
- **Adopted appendices:** B, C, D, F, H, I, J, K, and P (new in 2025 edition).

## Key Local Amendments (Ord. 2025-14, § 2)

### Scope and Permits

- **§ 101.1 amended:** Title clause updated to name all three jurisdictions.
- **§ 102.1 amended (item 5 added):** Provisions apply to vessels and boats permanently affixed to a location.
- **§ 105.5 amended:** Operational permits issued under Sections 105.5.1 through **105.5.60** (expanded range).
  - **New § 105.5.60:** Operational permit required for any business conducting the five-year test of an automatic fire sprinkler system or standpipe (NFPA 25, 2013 CA Edition).
- **§ 105.6 amended:** Construction permits issued under Sections 105.6.1 through **105.6.25**, plus new local sections:
  - **§ 105.6.26:** Permit for fire apparatus access road installation/modification.
  - **§ 105.6.27:** Plans required for all construction/substantial alteration/additions needing a building permit (except non-sprinklered R-3 without substantial addition/alteration).
  - **§ 105.6.28:** Plans required for all land subdivisions.
  - **§ 105.6.29:** Plans required for water supply adequacy determination (fire hydrants, § 507).
  - **§ 105.6.30:** Plans required for all land developments.

### Definitions Added (§ 202)

New locally defined terms include: Administrator (= Fire Chief), Aerial Pre-Plans, All-weather driving surface, Board of Directors, Board of Fire Commissioners, Combustible Material, Driveway (serving ≤2 SFDs), Fire Trail, Hazardous Vegetation, Knox Box, Nuisance Fire Alarm, Public Nuisance, Response Time, Rural Area (>10 acres), Rural Residential Area (3–10 acres), SASS, Streets, Substantial Addition (>50% gross floor area AND combined ≥5,000 sq ft), Substantial Alteration (>50% interior/exterior wall linear length AND >50% roof area within one year), Temporary Fire Department Access Road.

### Hazardous Materials (Chapter 50)

- **§ 5001.5.1 amended (items 10–11 added):** For bulk transfer/process/storage facilities, the following are required in addition to state requirements:
  - A **Site Fire/Explosion/Hazardous Material Release Analysis Assessment**.
  - A **Fire Protection Engineer (FPE)-stamped risk assessment** for all fire, explosion, smoke, and toxicity hazard risks (per NFPA 550 & 551).
- **New § 5001.5.3:** HMMP, HMIS, floor plans, and MSDSs must be stored at a readily accessible on-site location (or outside cabinet); may be required in specific electronic media format for CAD dispatching.
- **New § 5003.9.1.2:** On-site documentation of compliance with state and federal hazardous material regulations must be maintained and available for fire department inspection.

### Flammable/Combustible Liquids (Chapter 57)

- **§ 5703.3 amended:** FPE-stamped risk assessment required for bulk transfer/process/storage facilities (same trigger as § 5001.5.1).
- **§ 5704.2.9.6.1 amended:** Storage of Class I and II liquids in aboveground tanks outside buildings is **prohibited in all zoning districts except commercial, industrial, or agricultural**. Exception for emergency power generator installations in commercial, industrial, agricultural, business, rural, or rural residential zones, on a case-by-case basis.
- **§ 5706.2.4.4 amended:** Same aboveground tank location prohibition for bulk delivery/dispensing operations.

### Explosives and Fireworks (Chapter 56)

- **§ 5601.1.3 amended:** Fireworks possession, manufacture, storage, sale, handling, and use are **prohibited** within the jurisdiction. Exceptions for licensed fireworks displays, pyrotechnic special effects, and Health & Safety Code Division 11 manufacturing/testing.
- **New §§ 5601.1.3.1–5601.1.3.3:** Responsible-party liability for fireworks violations; any Fire Official or Peace Officer may issue infraction citations.
- **§ 5601.2.2 amended:** No retail fireworks displays on highways, sidewalks, public property, or in Group A/E occupancies.
- **§ 5601.2.4 amended:** Before a pyrotechnic permit is issued, applicant must file a **$2,000,000 corporate surety bond or public liability policy** (official may adjust if site conditions require greater or lesser amount; government entities exempt).

### Sprinkler Requirements — Local Thresholds (Chapter 9)

The ordinance amends multiple sprinkler threshold sections. Key industrial-relevant provisions:
- **§ 903.2.4 (Group F-1):** Sprinkler required throughout all F-1 buildings (manufacturing/industrial).
- **§ 903.2.9 (Group S-1):** Sprinkler required throughout all S-1 buildings (moderate-hazard storage).
- **§ 903.2.10.3 (Group S-2 low hazard storage):** Sprinkler required per 2025 CFC and 2025 CBC without local amendment — no local carve-out.
- **§ 903.3.5.3:** Non-permissible water supply for fire sprinklers — specifics in code.
- **High-piled storage rack systems:** Where rack height exceeds 14 feet, sprinkler design shall conform to Extra Hazard Group I; otherwise Ordinary Group II.

### Fire Apparatus Access Roads (Appendix D)

- **D102.1 amended:** Access roads must support fire apparatus up to **75,000 lbs** (asphalt, concrete, or approved surface).
- **D103.2 amended:** Grade 16–20% requires grooved concrete (½" × ½" grooves, 1½" o.c., 30–45° angle); no grade shall exceed **20%**; cross slope ≤8%.
- **D103.3 amended:** Minimum turning radius — 25 ft inside, **45 ft outside** (based on 20-ft unobstructed width).
- **D103.4 Table amended:** Dead-end roads 151–750 ft require 100-ft hammerhead, 50-ft Y, 75-ft shunt, or 90-ft cul-de-sac. Over 750 ft requires special approval.
- **D103.5 amended:** Minimum clear width 20 ft (16 ft for ≤2 SFDs); all gates set ≥30 ft off the street.
- **D106.1 amended:** Multi-family projects with >100 DUs require two separate approved fire apparatus access roads.

### Violations and Penalties

- **§ 112.4 amended:** Every violation is a misdemeanor or infraction under Health & Safety Code § 13871 and Government Code § 53069.4. Each 10-day period of continued non-compliance is a separate offense.

### Nuisance Alarms and Aerial Pre-Plans

- **New § 401.5.1:** A fee may be charged for false or nuisance fire alarms per Board-adopted fee schedule.
- **New § 401.10:** For new construction, fire official may require an aerial pre-plan prepared by an approved vendor at the developer's cost.

### Exterior Fire Hazard Control (New § 324)

- Peat fires and subsurface vegetative combustion must be extinguished by the property owner. If owner fails within reasonable time, the Fire District may act and charge costs against the property (Health & Safety Code § 13009).

## Coverage Area

Con Fire serves unincorporated Contra Costa County plus the cities of Antioch, Bay Point, Bethel Island, Brentwood, Byron, Clayton, Concord, Discovery Bay, Hercules, Knightsen, Lafayette, Martinez, Oakley, Pittsburg, Pleasant Hill, San Pablo, and Walnut Creek. Where a city adopts more restrictive requirements, the more restrictive standard applies only within that city's limits.
