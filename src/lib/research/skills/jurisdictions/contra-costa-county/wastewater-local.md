## Primary POTW

**Agency:** Central Contra Costa Sanitary District (Central San)
**Service area:** Central county — Concord, Walnut Creek, Pleasant Hill, Lafayette, Danville, Alamo, Moraga, Orinda, and surrounding unincorporated areas.
**Plant:** Main treatment plant in Martinez; discharges to Suisun Bay (San Francisco Bay watershed) under NPDES permit administered by the SF Bay Regional Water Quality Control Board.

**GOTCHA — East County:** Brentwood, Antioch, Pittsburg, and the East County area are served by **Delta Diablo Sanitation District**, not Central San. Always verify the correct POTW by facility street address before applying Central San's rules.

## Industrial User Classification (§ 10.04.020)

The District classifies non-domestic users into four classes that drive permit tier and oversight level:

- **Class I:** Users requiring significantly greater administrative services/oversight than Class II, due to unusual wastewater character (volume, strength, composition, hazardous character, or variability).
- **Class II (Significant Industrial Users — SIUs):** Users who meet one or more of the following:
  1. Discharge flow ≥25,000 gallons/average work day; OR
  2. Contribute process wastewater making up ≥5% of the plant's average dry-weather hydraulic or organic capacity; OR
  3. Have hazardous pollutants in wastes; OR
  4. Are subject to national pretreatment standards (federal categorical standards); OR
  5. Have untreated wastewater pollutants exceeding any pretreatment standard or local limit; OR
  6. Have a reasonable potential to adversely impact the treatment plant, singularly or in combination.
- **Class III:** Users with lesser impact potential than Class II; includes any user generating hazardous waste (whether or not normally discharged to sewer) if there is a potential for accidental sewer discharge; includes users with county hazardous materials response plan/inventory requirements or RCRA hazardous waste notification triggers.
- **Class IV:** All other non-domestic users not meeting Class I–III criteria.

Classes I–III require an Industrial User (IU) Permit or Permit Contract. Class IV users are subject to general prohibitions only.

## Permit Types

### 1. Industrial User (IU) Permit / Permit Contract
Required for Class I, II, and III users. Permits specify:
- Discharge flow limits and concentration limits (local limits + applicable federal categorical standards).
- O&M recordkeeping requirements.
- Spill response plan.
- Compliance certification reports.
- Discharge monitoring requirements (sampling frequency, parameters, analytical methods).
- Slug discharge prevention and contingency plan (SDPC) for certain users.
- Prohibition of dilution as substitute for treatment (§ 10.08.100).

The Board has determined that permit issuance is a ministerial act; entering a permit contract is non-ministerial (§ 10.04.040).

### 2. Special Discharge Permit (SDP)
For temporary, non-standard, or emergency discharges not covered by a standard IU Permit. May require pretreatment system and sampling requirements.

Contact Environmental Compliance Section to apply: https://www.centralsan.org/post/special-discharge-permit

### 3. Waste Hauler Discharge Permit (Chapter 10.28)
Required for any person transporting liquid wastes (septic, grease, industrial) to District facilities. Permits include Class III septage and grease interceptor waste haulers.

### 4. BMP Permit
For users generating hazardous materials with a potential for accidental discharge — requires a Best Management Practices plan approved by the District.
Application: https://www.centralsan.org/sites/main/files/file-attachments/bmp_permit_application.pdf

## Key Discharge Prohibitions (Chapter 10.08)

### General Prohibitions (§ 10.08.020)
Unlawful to discharge to District facilities without authorization. Applies to all users.

### Prohibited Effects (§ 10.08.030)
Discharges causing interference, pass-through, or other adverse effects on the treatment system are prohibited.

### Prohibited Substances or Characteristics (§ 10.08.040)
No user shall discharge:
- **(A)** Flammable liquids/materials (closed cup flash point <140°F / 60°C); explosives or materials exceeding 5% (successive) or 10% (single reading) of LEL at point of discharge. Includes gasoline, kerosene, benzene, toluene, xylene, ethers, alcohols, ketones, aldehydes, sulfides, and similar compounds.
- **(B)** Solid or viscous substances causing sewer obstruction (feathers, ashes, sand, metal, wood, plastic, tar, petroleum residues, grease with particles >½ inch, animal tissues, blood, etc.).
- **(C)** Wastewater with **pH < 5.5 or > 11.5**, or with corrosive properties capable of damaging structures, equipment, or persons.
- **(D)** Hazardous pollutants in quantities sufficient to injure the treatment process, create health hazards, or cause NPDES violations.
- **(E)** Wastewater exceeding **65°C (150°F)** at the collection system entry point, or causing the treatment plant influent to exceed **40°C (104°F)**.
- **(F)** Oxygen-demanding pollutants released at a flow rate/concentration that may cause interference or pass-through; slug loads regardless of interference potential.
- **(G)** Discharges causing toxic gases, vapors, or fumes in quantities causing acute worker health hazards in any District facility.
- **(H)** Noxious or malodorous liquids, gases, or solids.
- **(I)** Radioactive wastes exceeding limits in 10 CFR Part 20.2003.
- **(J)** Stormwater, groundwater, or unpolluted water without a specific permit.
- **(K)** Cooling water or process blowdown without prior permit.
- **(L)** Septic tank waste, grease interceptor waste, portable toilet waste without a waste hauler permit.
- **(M)** Any waste defined as hazardous under federal or state law unless delisted with approved variance.
- **(N)** Any substance specifically prohibited or prohibited by concentration level in local limits adopted by District Board resolution (limits kept on file at the District office).
- **(O)** Any substance that alone or in combination may cause the District to violate NPDES permit toxicity conditions.
- **(P) Discharge prohibition substances (no numeric limit — presence is a violation):**
  1. Cyanide (for unpermitted users)
  2. Dioxin compounds
  3. Polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs)
  4. Tributyltin (TBT)
  5. Dieldrin
  6. 4,4'-DDE
  7. Perchloroethylene wastes from dry cleaner operations

(Ord. 253, 2008; Ord. No. 323, § 4, 6-2-22)

### National Pretreatment Standards (§ 10.08.060)
Federal categorical pretreatment standards (40 CFR Parts 405–471) apply directly and are enforced by the District. Most stringent of federal categorical or local limits applies.

### Specific Pollutant Limitations (§ 10.08.070)
Numeric discharge limits (daily maximum concentrations and/or mass limits) for specific pollutants are adopted separately by **District Board resolution** — they are not embedded in Title 10 itself. The resolution-adopted limits are maintained on file at the District office and on the District's Environmental Compliance website.

The District's Local Discharge Limits document (posted at centralsan.org/environmental-compliance) sets numeric limits for metals (including copper, nickel, and others), Total Toxic Organics (TTO, limit: 2.10 mg/L), and other parameters. Limits are daily-maximum unless otherwise specified.

Contact the Environmental Compliance Section for the current Local Discharge Limits schedule.

## Monitoring and Compliance

- Sampling of industrial discharges for pH, cyanide, sulfide, phenols, oil and grease, and volatile organics uses grab samples (§ 10.12 administration provisions).
- Significant Non-Compliance (SNC) determinations follow 40 CFR 403.8(f)(2)(vii):
  - Chronic violations: ≥66% of measurements exceeding a numeric limit in a 6-month period.
  - Technical Review Criteria (TRC): ≥33% of measurements ≥ (limit × 1.4 for BOD/TSS/FOG; × 1.2 for all other pollutants except pH).
  - SNC-listed users are published annually in the newspaper with the largest circulation (historically the Contra Costa Times).
