## CUPA Administrator

**Agency:** Contra Costa Health, Hazardous Materials Programs Division (CCHHMP)
**Type:** Own-CUPA — Contra Costa County is a Certified Unified Program Agency (CUPA) under CalEPA, operating its own countywide program. Covers all unincorporated areas plus participating cities.
**Address:** 4005 Port Chicago Hwy, Suite 120, Concord, CA 94520
**Phone:** 925-941-3300
**Email:** ccchazmat@cchealth.org
**Online payments:** hsdmobile.cchealth.org
**Regulated facilities:** Approximately 3,800 countywide.

## Programs Administered

The Contra Costa CUPA administers all six Unified Program elements:

1. **Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP)** — Community Right-to-Know / Chapter 6.95 H&SC; required for any business storing/using hazardous materials above threshold quantities. Reported via CalEPA's CERS portal.
2. **Hazardous Waste Generator (HWG)** — Annual permit covering on-site generation, accumulation, and disposal of hazardous waste.
3. **Underground Storage Tank (UST)** — Operational permits for UST facilities; inspection and leak detection oversight.
4. **California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP)** — Federal RMP counterpart; required for facilities handling regulated substances above threshold quantities.
5. **Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA)** — Required for facilities with aggregate aboveground petroleum storage ≥1,320 gallons (or any single tank >660 gallons).
6. **Industrial Safety Ordinance (ISO)** — Local CalARP overlay; see below.

## Industrial Safety Ordinance (ISO) — Local Layer

- **Codified at:** County Ordinance Code **Chapter 450-8** (Division 450, Hazardous Materials and Wastes).
- **Purpose:** Stricter than state CalARP; applies to petroleum refineries and chemical plants that submit a federal RMP (Risk Management Plan) to US EPA and are located in unincorporated Contra Costa County.
- **Scope:** Requires a Safety Plan and Safety Plan Summary (public document) in addition to the CalARP RMP. Requires an independent third-party audit, public participation in the audit process, and annual safety performance reports.
- **Richmond ISO:** A separate and parallel ISO applies to facilities within the City of Richmond — administered by the City of Richmond, not the County CUPA. Research both if a facility straddles or is near the Richmond city limit.
- **Research rule:** For any major chemical/refinery facility in unincorporated Contra Costa County, always investigate Chapter 450-8 in addition to the state CalARP program.

## Fee Schedule — Resolution No. 2024-293 (Effective August 13, 2024)

**Adopting action:** Board of Supervisors Resolution No. 2024-293, adopted August 13, 2024 (5:0 vote). Replaces prior fee schedule under Resolution No. 2012/184 (which had not been updated since 2012).

**Fee structure overview (per Resolution No. 2024-293):**

- **HMBP Annual Permit Fee:** Calculated based on (a) number of chemicals at the regulated facility and (b) number of pounds in the HMBP inventory, as reported via CERS or observed on-site by inspector. The annual fee is assessed per fiscal year (July 1 – June 30) using the calendar year beginning January 1 prior to the fiscal year.
- **HWG Annual Permit Fee:** Based on CERS-reported data and hazardous waste generation amounts; assessed annually.
- **UST, CalARP, APSA fees:** Per program; vary by facility size/complexity.
- **Initial permit processing fee:** **$96** — charged to any business newly subject to CUPA regulation or changing ownership.
- **Regulatory hourly rate (regular business hours):** **$365/hour per staff member** — charged for regulatory activities provided to regulated businesses; also applies to Community Warning System (CWS) access not already included in the annual permit fee.
- **Regulatory hourly rate (non-business hours):** **$404/hour per staff member**.
- **Hazardous Materials Incident Response (non-CUPA regulated entities):** $365/hour per staff member during regular hours; $404/hour non-business hours — authorized under Health & Safety Code § 101325.
- **Remote facility exemption:** Businesses with unstaffed remote facilities in isolated areas qualifying under H&SC § 25505.5 are exempt from the HMBP Annual Permit Fee but pay a fee for exemption application review.

**Fee basis:** Fee is primarily driven by the time associated with the regulated activity and the frequency of required inspections. Main driver = staff time × inspection frequency per program element.

**Fiscal impact:** Fee increase effective FY 2025-26 to fully recover CUPA program costs. Community Warning System fees increased ~67% to reflect actual cost increases since 2012.

## Legal Authorities

- Health & Safety Code § 25404.5 — authority to set CUPA fees.
- Health & Safety Code §§ 25501–25501.4 — HMBP definitions and thresholds.
- Health & Safety Code § 25505.5 — remote facility exemption.
- Health & Safety Code § 101325 — hazmat incident response fee authority.
- County Ordinance Code Chapter 450-8 — Industrial Safety Ordinance.
