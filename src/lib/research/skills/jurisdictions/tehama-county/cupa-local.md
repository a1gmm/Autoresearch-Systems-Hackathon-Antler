> CUPA reference for all Tehama County facilities, incorporated and unincorporated.
> CRITICAL: No city in Tehama County operates its own CUPA. Tehama County
> Environmental Health is the single countywide CUPA — it covers Red Bluff, Corning,
> the City of Tehama, and all unincorporated areas.

## Programs Administered

Tehama County Environmental Health administers all six CalEPA unified programs
countywide:

| # | Program | Acronym | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hazardous Materials Business Plan | HMBP | Submitted via CERS; Tehama EH reviews and inspects |
| 2 | Hazardous Waste Generator Program | HWG | Generator ID, manifest compliance, inspection |
| 3 | Underground Storage Tanks | UST | Installation, operation, modification, closure |
| 4 | Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act | APSA | Spill prevention for above-threshold petroleum storage (gas stations, vehicle repair shops, commercial petroleum users) |
| 5 | California Accidental Release Prevention Program | CalARP | Local implementing agency for RMP-regulated facilities |
| 6 | Unified Fire Code Hazardous Materials Operational Permits | CFC HazMat | Coordinated with fire AHJ |

## HMBP Program Details

- **Submission system:** California Environmental Reporting System (CERS) —
  https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/business
- **Who must comply:** Businesses storing, using, or handling hazardous materials at
  or above threshold quantities (Health & Safety Code §25507 — generally 55 gallons
  liquid, 500 lbs solid, 200 cubic feet compressed gas).
- **Annual update required:** Yes; CERS resubmission each year or upon material changes.
- **County inspection program:** Tehama EH conducts inspections for HMBP accuracy,
  hazardous waste generator compliance, UST, and APSA. The department's stated scope
  covers "hazardous materials and hazardous waste generators, ASTs, USTs, gas stations,
  vehicle repair shops, other commercial facilities storing or disposing of oil, gasoline, and
  diesel."

## No City-Level CUPA

- The Cities of Red Bluff, Corning, and Tehama do not have their own CUPAs.
- All CUPA filings, inspections, and enforcement for facilities within those city limits go
  through Tehama County Environmental Health at the address above.
- This differs from larger California counties where major cities (e.g., Oxnard in Ventura
  County) operate independent CUPAs.

## CalARP / RMP

- Tehama County Environmental Health is the Local Implementing Agency (LIA) for
  CalARP. Facilities with regulated substances above threshold quantities must submit
  an RMP to US EPA and undergo local CalARP review by Tehama EH.
