## Pretreatment Program

**Formal EPA-Approved IWP: NO**

The 2022 NPDES tentative permit fact sheet explicitly states "Pretreatment Program: **No**" — the City of Corning does not have a formally approved industrial pretreatment program under 40 CFR Part 403 at the time of the 2022 permit reissuance.

However, the NPDES permit **requires** the City to implement pretreatment functions (40 CFR Part 403.8(f)), including:
- Maintaining legal authority to control nondomestic discharges
- Enforcing categorical and local limits (40 CFR §§ 403.5 and 403.6)
- Programmatic functions per 40 CFR § 403.8(f)(2)
- Adequate funding and staffing per 40 CFR § 403.8(f)(3)

The city also operates a **Sewer Pollution Prevention and Pre-Treatment Program** (per city website) focused on preventing accidental or illegal discharge of hazardous pollutants, including a Fats, Oils & Grease (FOG) program for food service establishments.

## Notable Industrial User

**Bell-Carter Foods (formerly Bell-Carter Olive Company)** — olive processing plant — is the significant industrial discharger in Corning. Bell-Carter operates its own industrial WWTP with a separate NPDES permit (CA0083721), also regulated by the Central Valley RWQCB. The city WWTP and Bell-Carter share a diffuser outfall to the Sacramento River. Bell-Carter's treated effluent enters the shared outfall; it is not subject to the city's sewer use ordinance for the bulk of its discharge.

## Notes for EHS Research
- Facilities planning to discharge industrial wastewater to the city sewer must contact Public Works at (530) 824-7025 before connecting.
- Because no formal IWP is in place, categorical pretreatment standards under 40 CFR Parts 405–471 still apply directly to industrial users as pass-through requirements.
- FOG control requirements are in effect; food service establishments must provide interceptors and submit monthly pumping reports.
