## CUPA Agency

**Sierra County Environmental Health** is the single countywide CUPA, designated by CalEPA in 2002. There is no participating-agency (PA) split — the county Environmental Health office administers all six unified program elements directly, including for the sole incorporated city (Loyalton).

## Programs Administered

Sierra County Environmental Health administers all five CUPA program elements:

1. **HMBP** — Hazardous Materials Business Plan (business-level hazmat inventory and emergency response planning)
2. **Hazardous Waste Management** — generator compliance (large and small quantity generators)
3. **UST** — Underground Storage Tank program
4. **APSA** — Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure)
5. **CalARP** — California Accidental Release Prevention Program (Risk Management Plans)

## Permit / Reporting Process

- All regulated businesses submit Unified Program information electronically through **CERS** (California Environmental Reporting System), as required since January 1, 2009 (Health & Safety Code).
- After electronic submission, staff conduct facility walkthroughs to assess permitting needs.
- Facilities receive an "annual single system fee" invoice; upon payment, a **consolidated CUPA permit** is issued.

## Coverage Notes

- Countywide CUPA with no PA split — both unincorporated Sierra County and the City of Loyalton are served by this single county CUPA.
- Expect very low facility count given population (~3,200 residents); most regulated sites will be small fuel/petroleum storage, agricultural operations, and any legacy mining/timber operations.
- Federal lands (Tahoe and Plumas National Forests, Humboldt-Toiyabe NF) are not subject to county CUPA jurisdiction — USFS/federal facilities report to state CalEPA programs directly.
