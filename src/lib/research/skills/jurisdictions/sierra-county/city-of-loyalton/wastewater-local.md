## POTW and operating authority

The City of Loyalton owns and operates its own community sanitary wastewater system (POTW).
The system was originally constructed with Municipal Improvement Bond proceeds (1957) and
has been extended since. The City is responsible for sewer mains; property owners own and
maintain service laterals.

## Governing ordinances

Two sewer chapters exist in the Loyalton Municipal Code (July 2024 edition):

| Chapter | Title | Notes |
|---------|-------|-------|
| **6.24** | Sanitation ("City of Loyalton Sewer Regulations Chapter") | Older chapter adopted under H&SC Article 4, Ch. 6, Part 8, Div. 5; references 1955 Western Plumbing Officials Uniform Plumbing Code for definitions |
| **11.08** | Sewer (active, more recent) | Adopted under H&SC Article IV, Div. 5, Part 3, Ch. 6; supersedes previous sewer ordinance (§ 11.08.020 rescission clause); includes FOG program, discharge limits, EDU-based fee structure |

Chapter **11.08** is the operative sewer-use ordinance for current compliance purposes.

## Discharge prohibitions (§ 6.24.070 and § 11.08.150)

### Absolute prohibitions (§ 6.24.070(B) — applies to all users):

- Liquids/vapors > **150 °F**
- FOG > **100 mg/L** (fat, oil, or grease by weight) without pretreatment
- Gasoline, benzene, naphtha, fuel oil, or other flammable/explosive liquids, solids, or gases
- Un-shredded garbage; ashes, cinders, sand, mud, metals, glass, tar, plastics, wood, or other solid/viscous substances
- pH < **5.5** or > **9.0**, or any waste with corrosive properties causing damage to sewer structures or personnel
- Toxic or poisonous substances in quantities that injure sewage treatment processes, or create hazards to humans, animals, or receiving waters
- Suspended solids of character or quantity requiring unusual treatment-plant attention
- Noxious or malodorous gases capable of creating a public nuisance
- Septic tank sludge
- Roof leaders and surface/storm water into sanitary sewers (§ 6.24.070(A))

### FOG (Fats, Oils, and Grease) Control Program (§ 11.08.140)

**Food service establishments (FSEs)** discharging to the Loyalton wastewater system must:

1. Install, operate, and maintain an approved grease interceptor (below-ground, baffled tank) meeting Uniform Plumbing Code and California Plumbing Code requirements.
   - Sizing: **20 gallons per bar/restaurant seat**, minimum **500 gallons**.
2. New FSEs must obtain City approval for grease interceptor sizing **before** submitting building permit plans.
3. Existing FSEs with plumbing modifications ≥ **$20,000 building permit valuation** must upgrade to compliance.
4. Wastewater discharge from grease interceptor/trap must not exceed **600 mg/L FOG** (§ 11.08.140(B)(1)).
5. Variance from grease interceptor to indoor grease trap available only on documented feasibility grounds; City must approve alternative pretreatment technology.

### Preliminary treatment threshold (§ 6.24.070(F))

Industrial dischargers exceeding any of the following must obtain City Engineer review and, where required, install preliminary treatment at owner's expense:

| Parameter | Threshold |
|-----------|-----------|
| 5-day BOD | > 300 mg/L |
| Suspended Solids | > 350 mg/L |
| Daily flow | > 2% of city's average daily sewage flow |

Industrial dischargers with unusual-strength waste may enter special agreements with the City Engineer (§ 6.24.070, agreement clause).

### Grease/oil/sand interceptors (§ 6.24.070(C)–(D))

Required whenever the City Engineer determines liquid wastes contain excessive grease, flammable wastes, or sand. Must be accessible for cleaning and maintained continuously by the owner.

## Inspection authority (§ 11.08.010(C))

City officers, inspectors, and authorized employees may enter any building or industrial facility for inspection, observation, measurement, sampling, and testing to enforce sewer ordinance provisions.
