## Overview

Sierra County is predominantly a rural, on-site sewage county. There is no countywide centralized sewer system or POTW (Publicly Owned Treatment Works) operated by the county. Most unincorporated parcels are served by individual onsite sewage treatment and disposal systems (septic systems).

## County Onsite Sewage Program (Unincorporated Areas)

**Sierra County Environmental Health** administers the county's Liquid Waste Program under authority of the **Local Agency Management Program (LAMP)** (2018 edition on file with county).

### Program Coverage

- Permitting and inspection of onsite sewage treatment and disposal systems
- Greywater systems oversight
- Registration and oversight of septage/liquid waste haulers under California Health & Safety Code § 117400 et seq.
- Oversight of qualified professionals licensed by the State of California

## Loyalton Municipal Sewer System

The **City of Loyalton** (sole incorporated city) operates a separate municipal sewer collection and treatment system. Loyalton's POTW is regulated by the **Lahontan Regional Water Quality Control Board (Region 6)**, which covers the eastern Sierra Nevada. ## Industrial Pretreatment

Because Loyalton's POTW is small (population ~800) and there are no known significant industrial users, it is unlikely that a formal Industrial Pretreatment Program (40 CFR Part 403) applies. Industrial dischargers to the Loyalton sewer system should contact the City of Loyalton Public Works and the Lahontan RWQCB to confirm applicable limits. ## Sewer Use Ordinance

A municipal sewer use ordinance (if any) would be in the **City of Loyalton Municipal Code** — not in Sierra County Code, which does not publish a county-level sewer use ordinance for incorporated areas. For unincorporated areas served by on-site systems, the governing standards are found in **SCC Chapter 12.04** (local agency management program for sanitation) and state Title 27 (Onsite Sewage).
