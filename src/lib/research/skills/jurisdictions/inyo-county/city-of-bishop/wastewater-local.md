## POTW Operator

**Bishop Area Wastewater Authority (BAWA)**
A Joint Powers Authority formed June 30, 2020, by the City of Bishop and the Eastern Sierra Community Services District (ESCSD).

- Mailing address: 377 West Line Street, Bishop, CA 93514
- Website: https://bawa.ca.gov/
- City Public Works (sewer inquiries): (760) 873-5863

The City previously operated its own WWTP independently; BAWA now manages both the City plant and the ESCSD plant, with consolidation of the two plants under study (BAWA Preliminary Engineering Report, June 2025).

## Collection System

- City of Bishop collection system: 16 miles of pipe, 300+ manholes, one lift station.
- Flow averages ~800,000 gpd (half of the 1.6 MGD permitted plant capacity).
- Separate ESCSD system (~0.85 MGD capacity) managed under interagency agreement.
- Total combined BAWA infrastructure: ~50 miles of collection system.

## Treatment Process

Both plants use lagoon-based treatment:
- Headworks + primary clarification → lagoon secondary treatment → secondary clarified effluent discharged to land application.
- City plant: 1 aerated pond + 2 facultative ponds + 3 excess storage ponds.
- Effluent disposed by flood irrigation on ~40 acres (City-leased) + ~85 acres (Los Angeles-leased pasture land).

## Discharge Permits (LRWQCB)

Both plants are regulated by the **Lahontan Regional Water Quality Control Board (LRWQCB)** under Waste Discharge Requirements:

| Entity | WDID |
|---|---|
| City of Bishop WWTP | WDID-6B140101001 |
| Eastern Sierra CSD WWTP | WDID-6B140108001 |

These are land discharge WDRs (groundwater receiving body), not NPDES surface water permits.

## Current Effluent Permit Limits

Based on BAWA PER Table 2.3 (Lumos & Associates, June 2025), limits set by LRWQCB:

| Constituent | Existing Permit Limit | Projected Future (Consolidated) Limit |
|---|---|---|
| BOD₅ | 50 mg/L | 30 mg/L |
| TSS | Not regulated (not monitored) | 30 mg/L |
| Total Nitrogen (TN) | Not regulated (monitored monthly) | 10 mg/L |

Note: Nitrogen is not currently regulated at either BAWA plant, though there is historical LRWQCB interest in nitrogen management. Groundwater nitrate monitoring is ongoing at both sites.

## Industrial Pretreatment Program

No published local industrial pretreatment ordinance or IWP program was found for the City of Bishop or BAWA. The small-city context and land-discharge WDR (rather than NPDES surface discharge) means federal categorical pretreatment standards trigger differently. ## Capital Investment Context

The BAWA PER (June 2025) recommends construction of a new extended aeration mechanical plant (consolidated) to meet projected future LRWQCB permit limits (BOD₅ 30 mg/L, TSS 30 mg/L, TN 10 mg/L). The existing lagoon-based system operates near its limit at present BOD₅ 50 mg/L. Industrial dischargers should account for likely tightening of local limits as the new plant comes online.
