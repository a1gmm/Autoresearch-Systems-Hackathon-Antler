## EHS Local Rules by Family

### Air

- **Air district: Great Basin Unified Air Pollution Control District (GBUAPCD)** — formed 1974 via joint powers agreement among Alpine, Mono, and Inyo Counties. The sole air permitting authority for all stationary sources in unincorporated Inyo County and the City of Bishop.
- **Address:** 157 Short Street, Suite 6, Bishop, CA 93514-3537; (760) 872-8211; www.gbuapcd.org
- **PM10 nonattainment:** The Owens Valley planning area (southern Owens Valley, Inyo County) remains a **Serious nonattainment** area for the 1987 24-hour PM10 NAAQS. Owens Lake is the dominant dust source. The Mono Basin area is also PM10 nonattainment. All other Inyo County areas are attainment/unclassifiable.
- **Permit rules:** Rule 200 (Permits Required), Rule 222 (NSR for major sources in nonattainment areas, eff. 01/06/22), Rule 221 (PSD for attainment/unclassifiable areas), Rule 217 (Title V operating permits), Rule 218 (Limiting Potential to Emit), Rule 219 (Synthetic Minor status).
- Source: https://www.gbuapcd.org/PermittingAndRules/RulesAndRegulations/ | https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/great-basin-unified-air-pollution-control-district

### CUPA

- **CUPA agency:** Inyo County Department of Environmental Health Services (ICDEHS), CalEPA-certified. Single countywide CUPA — no participating agencies and no city-run CUPA. Bishop is served by the county CUPA.
- **Programs administered:** HMBP, Hazardous Waste Generator, Underground Storage Tanks (UST), Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA), California Accidental Release Program (CalARP).
- **HMBP threshold:** businesses storing/using ≥55 gallons, ≥500 lbs, or ≥200 cu ft (compressed gas) of hazardous materials must file via CERS (cers.calepa.ca.gov).
- **2026 annual facility fees** (effective Jan 1, 2026):
  - Base fee, CUPA facility (no UST/LQG/treatment): **$139/facility/year**
  - CUPA facility with UST(s): **$417/facility/year**
  - CUPA facility large quantity generator: **$256/facility/year**
  - CUPA facility with treatment: **$256/facility/year**
  - Combination of any two (UST, LQG, or treatment): **$387/facility/year**
  - All three activities: **$525/facility/year**
  - CalARP facility: **$740/facility/year**
- **Contact:** 168 N. Edwards St, Independence, CA 93526; (760) 878-0238
- Source: https://www.inyocounty.us/services/environmental-health/environmental-health-programs/hazardous-materials-cupa | Fee schedule PDF: https://www.inyocounty.us/sites/default/files/2025-10/Environmental%20Health%202026%20Fee%20Schedule.pdf

### Fire

- **No single countywide fire AHJ.** Local structural protection is fragmented across independent fire protection districts and volunteer departments.
- Key FPDs: Southern Inyo FPD (~1,250 sq mi, southeastern Inyo; towns include Shoshone, Tecopa, Death Valley Junction; 760-852-4130); Lone Pine FPD; Paradise FPD; Independence FD; Olancha-Cartago FD; Bishop Fire Department (joint city/rural district operation).

### Wastewater

- **No countywide POTW or sewer use ordinance.** Inyo County is largely unincorporated and served by individual water/sewer districts and the City of Bishop.
- **City of Bishop POTW:** City of Bishop operates a domestic wastewater collection and treatment facility serving Bishop proper (shares campus with Eastern Sierra Community Services District plant). Design capacity: 1.6 MGD; current average flow ~800,000 GPD (half capacity). Treated effluent disposed via land application on ~125 acres of leased pasture (City of Bishop + City of Los Angeles parcels). No surface discharge. The City of Bishop Sewer Service is regulated by the Lahontan Regional Water Quality Control Board. [VERIFY NPDES/WDR permit number from Lahontan RWQCB.]
- **Big Pine CSD POTW:** Big Pine Community Services District operates a 0.150 MGD secondary treatment facility serving Big Pine (oxidation ditch + percolation basins). Regulated under Lahontan RWQCB Waste Discharge Requirements, WDID No. 6B140100001, Board Order revised 2025. No surface discharge; percolation to groundwater. No industrial pretreatment program required at this scale.
- **Industrial pretreatment:** No federally-required pretreatment program identified for any Inyo County POTW (all design flows are well below the 5 MGD threshold). [VERIFY with Lahontan RWQCB if any significant industrial discharger exists.]
- **Regulator:** Lahontan Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB Region 6), 15095 Amargosa Rd., Bldg. 2, Suite 210, Victorville, CA 92394; (760) 241-6583.

### Zoning

- **Zoning code:** Inyo County Zoning Ordinance, Title 18 of the Inyo County Code (originally Ordinance No. 182, adopted 1970; now codified in eCode360). Covers all unincorporated Inyo County. Bishop is excluded (has its own municipal code).
- **Industrial district:** Only one industrial zone designation — **M-1 (General Industrial & Extractive)** — codified at Chapter 18.57. No M-2 or separate light-industrial zone.
- **M-1 purpose:** Space for all types of manufacturing, warehousing, processing, mining, ore reduction, and mineral development, provided no pollution of human or natural resources.
- **M-1 key requirement:** All uses must be planned/operated so smoke, fumes, dust, odors, liquids, and waste are confined/purified to meet Planning Commission standards and provide no threat to public health, welfare, or aesthetic value of the county.
- **FAR:** Up to 0.5 by-right; up to 1.2 with CUP (for light industrial or general industrial GP designation).
- **Cannabis in M-1:** Indoor commercial cannabis cultivation, non-volatile and volatile manufacturing, transportation/distribution, and microbusinesses are allowed in M-1 with a CUP.
- **Zone map:** "Zoning Map of the County of Inyo" on file with County Clerk / Planning Department.
- **Zoning update underway:** Active General Plan and Zoning Code update in progress as of 2025–2026.
- Source: https://ecode360.com/44465949 | https://www.inyocounty.us/services/planning-department/zoning-ordinance-title-18

## CUPA (Unified Program)

- Inyo County Department of Environmental Health Services (ICDEHS) — the single countywide CUPA / Administering Agency, CalEPA-certified for the Unified Program. Administers HMBP, Hazardous Waste Generator, Underground Storage Tanks (UST), Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA), and California Accidental Release Program (CalARP). No participating-agency split and no city-run CUPA; Bishop (the county's only incorporated city) is served by the county. Farms/ranches are excluded and regulated separately by the Inyo County Agricultural Commissioner.
- https://www.inyocounty.us/services/environmental-health/environmental-health-programs/hazardous-materials-cupa

## Fire authority (AHJ)

- No single countywide structural fire department. Local structural protection is provided by independent fire protection districts and community volunteer departments (e.g., Southern Inyo FPD, Lone Pine FPD, Paradise FPD, Independence FD, Olancha-Cartago FD, and the Bishop Fire Department). Wildland State Responsibility Area (SRA) protection falls to CAL FIRE's San Bernardino Unit (BDU), which covers San Bernardino, Inyo, and Mono counties; Inyo/Mono SRA was consolidated under the San Bernardino unit in the late 1980s.
- https://www.fire.ca.gov/who-we-are/contact-us/find-your-local-unit (San Bernardino/BDU unit) — also https://www.sifpd.org/

## Notes

- GOTCHA (single incorporated city): Bishop is the **only** incorporated city in Inyo County; everything else is unincorporated. There are no municipal CUPAs and no fully independent municipal fire department — the county EHS is the sole CUPA.
- GOTCHA (federal-land carve-outs): very large shares of the county are federal/tribal land (Death Valley National Park, Inyo National Forest, BLM, and LADWP-owned Owens Valley lands plus the Bishop Paiute Reservation). Expect carve-outs where the federal land manager or tribe — not the county — is the authority. Southern Inyo FPD covers ~1,250 sq mi including Death Valley NP areas (Furnace Creek, Shoshone, Tecopa).
- GOTCHA (Bishop fire is a joint operation): the Bishop Fire Department is a cooperation between the City of Bishop and the Bishop (Rural) Fire Protection District, governed by both the City Council and the District Board, and also serves the Bishop Paiute Reservation under contract — it is not a standalone municipal fire department.
