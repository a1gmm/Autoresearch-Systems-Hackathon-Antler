## Industrial Zone Districts

The San Diego County Zoning Ordinance contains five industrial use regulation zones for
unincorporated areas:

| Zone | Short Name | Ordinance Sections | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M50** | Basic Industrial | §§ 2500–2509 | Basic manufacturing, low nuisance, all uses in enclosed buildings; high-quality industrial park setting |
| **M52** | Limited Impact Industrial | §§ 2520–2529 | Manufacturing + industrial-support commercial; no/very low nuisance; all uses enclosed |
| **M54** | General Impact Industrial | §§ 2540–2549 | General industrial |
| **M56+** | Mixed Industrial | §§ 2560–2569 | Mixed industrial uses |
| **M58** | High Impact Industrial | §§ 2580–2589 | High-impact industrial |

The compatibility matrix (§ 2050) shows which zones are consistent (●) or special
circumstances (o) with each General Plan land use designation.

## M50 — Basic Industrial Use Regulations (§§ 2500–2509)

### Intent (§ 2500)
Create and preserve areas for basic manufacturing operations which evidence no or very
low nuisance characteristics. Non-manufacturing uses restricted to essential support for
manufacturing plants and their personnel. All uses (with limited exceptions) conducted
entirely within enclosed buildings.

### Permitted Uses by Right (§ 2502)
**Civic:** Ambulance Services, Community Recreation, Emergency Shelters (§ 6911), Essential
Services, Fire Protection Services (§ 6905), Law Enforcement Services, Parking Services,
Postal Services

**Commercial:** Automotive and Equipment: Parking; Recycling Processing Facility (Wood
and Green Materials); Research Services

**Industrial:** Custom Manufacturing (§ 6300)

**Agricultural:** Horticulture (all types), Tree Crops, Row and Field Crops, Packing and
Processing (Limited, Winery, General)

### Permitted with Limitations (§ 2503 — subject to § 2980 limitations)
**Commercial:** Adult Entertainment Establishments ("19"), Administrative and Professional
Services ("5"), Recycling Collection Facility Small or Large ("2"), Recycling Processing
Facility Light or Heavy ("3"), Wholesaling/Storage/Distribution: Light ("8") (§ 6300)

**Industrial:** General Industrial ("14")

### Minor Use Permit Required (§ 2504)
**Civic:** Minor Impact Utilities, Small Schools

**Commercial:** Convenience Sales and Personal Services, Eating and Drinking
Establishments, Food and Beverage Retail Sales

### Major Use Permit Required (§ 2505)
**Civic:** Administrative Services, Child Care Center, Civic/Fraternal/Religious Assembly,
Clinic Services, Cultural Exhibits and Library Services, Major Impact Services and Utilities

**Commercial:** Wholesaling, Storage and Distribution: Heavy (§ 6300)

**Extractive:** Site Preparation

## M52 — Limited Impact Industrial Use Regulations (§§ 2520–2529)

### Intent (§ 2520)
Create and preserve areas where manufacturing and industrial uses with no or very low
nuisance characteristics may locate. Non-industrial uses that support or are adjuncts to
industrial uses are permitted, particularly administrative, sales, and service uses. All uses
conducted entirely within enclosed buildings.

### Permitted Uses by Right (§ 2522)
**Civic:** Ambulance Services, Community Recreation, Emergency Shelters (§ 6911), Essential
Services, Fire Protection Services (§ 6905), Law Enforcement Services, Parking Services,
Postal Services

**Commercial:** Administrative and Professional Services, Automotive and Equipment:
Parking, Business Equipment Sales and Services, Business Support Services,
Communications Services, Laundry Services, Participant Sports and Recreation: Indoor,
Recycling Processing Facility (Wood and Green Materials), Research Services,
Wholesaling/Storage/Distribution: Mini-Warehouses (§§ 6300, 6909)

**Industrial:** Custom Manufacturing (§ 6300)

**Agricultural:** Horticulture (all types), Tree Crops, Row and Field Crops, Packing and
Processing (Limited, Winery, General)

### Permitted with Limitations (§ 2523 — subject to § 2980 limitations)
**Commercial:** Adult Entertainment Establishments ("19"), Agricultural and Horticultural
Sales ("8"), Automotive Cleaning ("8"), Automotive Repairs Heavy ("8"), Automotive Repairs
Light ("8"), Automotive Sales/Rentals Farm/Heavy/Light Equipment ("9"), Building
Maintenance Services ("8"), Construction Sales and Services ("8"), Eating and Drinking
Establishments ("21"), Food and Beverage Retail Sales ("10"), Gasoline Sales ("12"),
Recycling Collection Facility Small or Large ("2"), Recycling Processing Facility Light or
Heavy ("3"), Wholesaling/Storage/Distribution: Light ("8")

**Industrial:** General Industrial ("14")

### Minor Use Permit Required (§ 2524)
**Civic:** Minor Impact Utilities, Small Schools

**Commercial:** Convenience Sales and Personal Services

## Compatibility Matrix (§ 2050 Summary)

Industrial zones (M50–M58) in the San Diego County Zoning Ordinance are coded in the
compatibility matrix as consistent (●) or special circumstances (o) against General Plan
land use designations. The matrix legend:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ● | Consistent Use Regulation |
| o | Special Circumstances (existing prior use, or meets findings in § 2072) |
| A | Permitted by Administrative Permit |
| S | Permitted by Site Plan |
| m | Permitted by Minor Use Permit |
| M | Permitted by Major Use Permit |
| P | Permitted only within Planned Developments ≥ 20 acres |

## Discretionary Permit Process

### Minor Use Permit
- Required for uses listed in § 2504 (M50) or § 2524 (M52).
- Administrative permit procedure governed by Zoning Ordinance §§ 7050–7099.

### Major Use Permit (MUP)
- Required for uses listed in § 2505 (M50) or the equivalent M52 section.
- Also required for structures over 60 ft in height.
- MUP process involves public hearing, findings of compatibility.
- Application form (PDS-313): https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/pds/zoning/formfields/PDS-313.pdf
- Discretionary permit portal: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/pds/zoning/ZoningPermits.html

### Special Circumstances (§ 2072 Findings)
A use regulation that is "special circumstances" (o in matrix) requires finding that:
(a) it is consistent with the applicable community plan;
(b) it is consistent with or applied under special circumstances in the majority of bordering
    land use designations;
(c) it is compatible with surrounding land uses considering density, public facilities,
    neighborhood character, and other relevant factors.
