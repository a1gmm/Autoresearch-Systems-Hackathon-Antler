## CUPA Agency

**County of San Diego, Department of Environmental Health and Quality (DEHQ) —
Hazardous Materials Division (HMD)**

HMD has operated as the Certified Unified Program Agency (CUPA) for San Diego County
since 1996. "HMD" and "CUPA" are used interchangeably by the county. HMD is an
**own-CUPA** (not a sub-CUPA under another agency).

- **CUPA coverage:** Unincorporated county + many contract cities.
- **Separate CUPA — City of San Diego:** San Diego Fire-Rescue Department (SDFD)
  serves as the CUPA for the incorporated City of San Diego.

## Local Regulatory Authority / Municipal Code

Hazardous materials authority is codified in the **San Diego County Code of Regulatory
Ordinances (SDCCO)**:

- **Title 6, Division 8, Chapter 11** — Certified Unified Program Agency, Hazardous
  Materials Inventory and Response Plans (§§ 68.1101–68.1111). This chapter establishes
  the local HMBP program, inventory thresholds, and response plan requirements.
- **Title 6, Division 8, Chapter 10** — Underground Storage Tanks (UST permit
  requirements).
- **Title 6, Division 8, Chapter 9** — Additional hazardous materials provisions.
- **Title 6, Division 8, Chapter 2 (§ 68.201 et seq.)** — General sewer/industrial discharge
  provisions cross-referenced by the Sanitation District.

The state enabling authority is **California Health and Safety Code, Chapter 6.95
(§§ 25500–25543)**, implementing AB 2185 and AB 2189.

## HMBP Reporting Thresholds

A business must submit an HMBP through CERS if it uses, handles, or stores a hazardous
material (or hazardous waste) in quantities at or above:

| Quantity trigger | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Solid substance | ≥ 500 pounds |
| Liquid | ≥ 55 gallons |
| Compressed gas | ≥ 200 cubic feet |
| Highly toxic compressed gas (TLV ≤ 10 ppm) | Any quantity |
| Extremely hazardous substances (EHS) | Threshold Planning Quantity (40 CFR Part 355) |

Source: HM-952 (04-2021), DEHQ Hazardous Materials Division.

## Programs Administered by HMD (CUPA)

All reported via the **California Environmental Reporting System (CERS)**:
https://cers.calepa.ca.gov

1. **Unified Program Facility Permit (UPFP)** — required for any facility handling hazardous
   materials in HMBP-reportable quantities.
2. **Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP)** — chemical inventory, site map, emergency
   response/employee training plan.
3. **California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP)** — for regulated substances above
   Program 1/2/3 thresholds.
4. **Underground Storage Tank (UST)** — permit, inspection, removal.
5. **Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA/SPCC)** — for aboveground petroleum
   storage ≥ 1,320 gallons.
6. **Hazardous Waste Generator** — small, large, and very small quantity generators.
7. **Hazardous Waste Onsite Treatment** — permit by rule and tiered permits.
8. **Medical Waste** — generators, transport, treatment.
9. **Remote Waste Consolidation** — multi-site generators.

## Permit Mechanism

**Unified Program Facility Permit (UPFP)** — a single consolidated permit covering all
programs listed above. UPFP is required before a facility may store or handle hazardous
materials in HMBP-reportable quantities.

All permit data submitted and maintained through **CERS**. Fees calculated annually based
on most recently "accepted" CERS data prior to invoice generation.

## Inspection Authority

HMD conducts periodic compliance inspections to verify HMBP requirements are met,
identify safety hazards, and confirm emergency response plans are current. Violations
subject to administrative penalties under SDCCO Title 6, Division 8.
