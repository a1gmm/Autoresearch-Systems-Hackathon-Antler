## Local Discharge Limits Table (§ 14.16.090)

All limits apply at the point of discharge to the sanitary sewer, as **instantaneous maximum concentrations**. All metal concentrations are **total metals** unless noted. The District Manager may impose mass limitations in addition to these concentration-based limits.

### General Prohibitions (§ 14.16.090.A)

| Parameter | Limit |
|---|---|
| Flashpoint (closed-cup) | ≥ 140°F (60°C) — wastestreams below this threshold prohibited |
| Temperature | < 150°F influent; ≤ 104°F (40°C) at introduction to WWTF |
| pH | 6.0 minimum — 8.5 maximum |
| FOG (Fats, Oils, and Grease) | ≤ 100 mg/L |
| Total Petroleum Hydrocarbons (TPH — diesel, motor oil, hydraulic oil) | ≤ 100 mg/L |
| 96-hour median tolerance limit (TLM, Routine Fish Bioassay) | ≥ 25% |

### "Unpolluted Water" Quality Definition (§ 14.16.070.B)

(Used to define what may not enter sanitary sewers via storm or cooling water connections)

| Parameter | Limit |
|---|---|
| pH | 6.0 min – 8.5 max |
| BOD | 5 mg/L max |
| Dissolved matter | 1,000 mg/L max |
| Suspended matter | 5 mg/L max |
| Settleable matter | 0.1 mL/L/hr max |
| Grease or oil | 10 mg/L max (monitoring min. twice/year required if any known discharge) |

## Additional Specific Prohibitions (§ 14.16.090.A)

- Solid/viscous pollutants causing obstruction to flow or interference
- BOD or oxygen-demanding pollutants at flow rate/concentration causing interference
- Petroleum oil, nonbiodegradable cutting oil (mineral oil origin) in amounts causing interference
- Trucked/hauled substances except at District-designated discharge points (§ 14.40.090)
- Sludges, screenings, or residues from industrial waste pretreatment
- Medical wastes (except as authorized in individual permit)
- Gasoline, benzene, naphtha, fuel oil, or other flammable/explosive materials
- Garbage not shredded to particles < 2 inches in any dimension
- Noxious or malodorous gas or substance capable of creating a public nuisance
- Septic tank sludge
- Radioactive isotopes exceeding limits set by District Manager per state/federal regulations
- Algaecides, fungicides, antibiotics, insecticides, strong oxidizing or reducing agents
- Mercury or mercury compounds (absolute prohibition)

## FOG Control Program (§ 14.16.100)

- All **nonresidential food service establishments** connected to the sanitary sewer must install, operate, and maintain a grease interceptor (grease trap).
- Grease interceptors must meet Uniform Plumbing Code Chapter 10 requirements.
- **Trigger events** requiring installation within 60 days: change of ownership, change of title, change of use, building permit issued for work > $5,000, SSO event caused by grease, or written notice from District Manager.
- Grease interceptor sizing must be approved by the District Manager before a building permit is issued.

## Interceptors (§ 14.16.095)

Grease, oil, and sand interceptors required at the District Manager's direction (per Uniform Plumbing Code Chapter 10) for liquid wastes containing grease, flammable wastes, sand, or other harmful ingredients. Not required for residential buildings.

## Pretreatment / Industrial Wastewater Permit (IWP)

The ordinance grants the District Manager authority to issue **individual wastewater discharge permits** with more stringent standards than the general limits, and to impose mass limitations.

## Right of Revision

The District reserves the right to establish by ordinance, individual permits, or general permits more stringent standards or requirements (§ 14.16.090.F).

## Analytical Requirements

All pollutant analyses must be performed by an **ELAP-certified laboratory** per 40 CFR Part 136 techniques, unless otherwise specified in an applicable categorical pretreatment standard (§ 14.16.090.H).
