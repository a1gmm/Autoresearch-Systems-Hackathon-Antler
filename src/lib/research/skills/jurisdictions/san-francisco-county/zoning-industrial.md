## Industrial Zone Districts

### M Districts (Traditional Industrial) — Planning Code § 210.4

**M-1 — Light Industrial**
- Purpose: Accommodates smaller industrial operations dependent on truck transportation. Most industries are permitted; uses with particularly noxious characteristics are excluded.
- Locational context: Scattered throughout the city; buffer between residential and heavier industry.
- Key controls: Enclosed operations generally required; screening from Residential Districts; minimum setback distances from residential zones.
- Conditional uses: Uses with significant external effects (noise, odor, vibration) that are not principally permitted require CUA from Planning Commission.

**M-2 — Heavy Industrial**
- Purpose: Least-restricted industrial zone; located at the eastern edge of the city, separated from residential and commercial areas. Served by rail and water transportation, heavy utility lines.
- Locational context: Most land zoned M-2 is controlled by the Port of San Francisco. Also along the southeastern waterfront.
- Key controls: Fewer screening and enclosure requirements than M-1, but many heavier uses are conditional (require CUA) or must be sited at a minimum distance from Residential Districts.
- Permitted/conditional distinction: The heaviest process industries, waste handling, and extractive uses are conditional even in M-2.

### PDR Districts (Production, Distribution, Repair) — Planning Code § 210.3

SF's primary industrial land use classification. PDR districts are non-residential only — housing, large office, and large-scale retail are not permitted in any PDR district. Industrial Protection Zones (IPZs) overlay some PDR areas to provide additional displacement protection.

| District | Code Designation | Character |
|----------|-----------------|-----------|
| **PDR-1-D** | Production/Distribution/Repair, Design | Retains and encourages less-intensive PDR with emphasis on design-related clusters; uses generate less external noise/odor/vibration; generally conducted in fully enclosed structures. Small-scale retail and office permitted to buffer residential areas. |
| **PDR-1-G** | Production/Distribution/Repair, General | Retains existing PDR activities and promotes new business formation; less-intensive operations; generates less external noise/odor/vibration and fewer trucking activities than PDR-2. Generally conducted within enclosed structures. |
| **PDR-2** | Core Production, Distribution, and Repair | Broadest range of light and contemporary industrial activities; higher-intensity PDR uses; non-residential only. Core industrial uses, warehousing, distribution, repair, manufacturing, and compatible ancillary uses permitted. |

Additional PDR sub-sections:
- **§ 210.3A** — Non-accessory use size limits for certain retail and office uses in PDR-1-B and PDR-2 districts
- **§ 210.3C** — Allowance for uses that support development of new PDR space in PDR-1-D and PDR-1-G districts

## General Permitted vs. Conditional Framework

Under Article 2, use control tables (§ 202.1 — Zoning Control Tables) govern each district. The table structure identifies uses as:
- **P** — Principally permitted (by right, subject to applicable development standards)
- **C** — Conditional (requires Conditional Use Authorization from the Planning Commission)
- **NP** — Not permitted

In M and PDR districts, most PDR uses (manufacturing, industrial, warehouse, repair, distribution, wholesale) are principally permitted or conditionally permitted. Large office (>25,000 sq ft in PDR), large retail, and residential uses are NP.

## Conditional Use Authorization (CUA) Process — Planning Code § 303

- **Hearing body:** SF Planning Commission
- **Standard:** Use must be (1) necessary or desirable, (2) not detrimental to the neighborhood, and (3) consistent with the General Plan and the Planning Code's intent.
- **Filing:** CUA Supplemental Application + Project Application (PRJ) filed with the Planning Department.
- **Contact:** Planning Information Center, 628-652-7300 | pic@sfgov.org
- **Online permit / project portal:** SF Planning — https://www.sf.gov/information--zoning-businesses

## Industrial Protection Zones (IPZs)

Established by Board of Supervisors action (referenced in SF BOS records, Ordinance No. 105-17 and subsequent amendments). IPZs impose additional limitations on formula retail, office, and residential conversions within designated PDR and M areas to protect remaining industrial land base.
