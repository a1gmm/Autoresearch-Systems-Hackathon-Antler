## CUPA Structure

**City of Santa Barbara is NOT its own CUPA.** The City is served directly by the county-level CUPA:

- **CUPA Agency:** Santa Barbara County Public Health Department — Environmental Health Services (EHS), Hazardous Materials Unit
- **Certified by:** CalEPA as the Certified Unified Program Agency for all of Santa Barbara County (incorporated and unincorporated areas).
- **No City Participating Agency (PA) is documented.** Businesses within city limits submit permits and reports directly to the county CUPA; there is no city-layer intermediary agency.

## Programs Administered

The county CUPA administers all six state-mandated unified programs:

1. **HMBP — Hazardous Materials Business Plan** (Health & Safety Code § 25500 et seq.): Required for facilities handling hazardous materials above thresholds. Submitted via CERS.
2. **Hazardous Waste Generator Oversight** (Cal. Code Regs. Title 22): Annual permit based on waste generation tonnage.
3. **APSA — Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act** (H&SC § 25270 et seq.): Spill Prevention Control and Countermeasure (SPCC) plans; tiered by tank capacity.
4. **UST — Underground Storage Tanks** (H&SC § 25280 et seq.): Permit per tank; plan review for new/modified systems.
5. **CalARP — California Accidental Release Prevention** (H&SC § 25531 et seq.): Risk Management Plan (RMP) required above threshold quantities; three program levels.
6. **Hazardous Waste Facility Permit** (as applicable): For facilities requiring a HWFP under DTSC delegation.

## CERS Electronic Reporting

All regulated businesses must report to the **California Environmental Reporting System (CERS)** at https://cers.calepa.ca.gov. The county CUPA reviews and processes all submissions.

## Local Ordinance

The county administers the CUPA under **Santa Barbara County Code Chapter 18C** (Environmental Health Services — CUPA provisions). The chapter codifies the county's authority to issue permits, collect fees, and enforce unified program requirements within its jurisdiction, including city limits where the county is CUPA. No separate City of Santa Barbara hazardous-materials ordinance covering CUPA-tier programs has been identified.
