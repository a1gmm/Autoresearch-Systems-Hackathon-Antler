## Industrial User Classification System (§ 8-12.1005)

Chapter 8-12 establishes four user classes for wastewater discharge:

| Class | Description | Discharge Permit Required |
|-------|-------------|--------------------------|
| I | Single-family residential, multi-family, hotels/motels/rest homes | No — flat monthly fee |
| II | General commercial; volume-based charges | No — per-100-cubic-ft fee |
| III | Industrial/commercial users with SIC-classified discharges | **Yes** — before connecting |
| IV | Significant industrial users with major BOD/TSS/TDS/Na/Cl loadings | **Yes** — before connecting |

**Class I** (§ 8-12.1005.a): Flat monthly rate set by City Council; includes single-family, multi-family (tiered by number of rooms), and hotel/motel/rest home units.

**Class II** (§ 8-12.1005.b): Fee set by City Council per 100 cubic feet of water delivered or wastewater discharged.

**Class III** (§ 8-12.1005.c): Fees established per individual user, user groups by SIC activity, or major SIC divisions; per-100-cubic-ft volume charge plus constituent-based charges.

**Class IV** (§ 8-12.1005.d): Monthly user charge calculated by formula:

```
Cu = (Vc × Vu) + (Bc × Bu) + (Sc × Su) + X
```
Where:
- `Cu` = monthly user charge ($/month)
- `Vc` = cost of collecting and treating each 100 cubic feet of wastewater
- `Vu` = total monthly wastewater volume (hundreds of cubic feet/month)
- `Bc` = cost of treating one pound of BOD
- `Bu` = total monthly BOD discharged (pounds/month)
- `Sc` = cost of treating one pound of suspended solids
- `Su` = total monthly suspended solids discharged (pounds/month)
- `X` = special assessments for violations, including excess TDS, Na, Cl and other parameters

## Significant Industrial User Thresholds (Federal Pretreatment § 8-12.412 / EPA 40 CFR 403)

Under § 8-12, Significant Industrial Users (SIUs) include dischargers that:
- Discharge an average of ≥25,000 gallons per day of process wastewater, **or**
- Contribute ≥5% of the average dry-weather hydraulic or organic capacity of the POTW, **or**
- Are designated by the control authority as having reasonable potential to adversely affect POTW operations.

## Discharge Restrictions

### Prohibited Discharges (§ 8-12 Article 4)
The ordinance prohibits substances that create fire/explosion hazard, excessive corrosion, obstruction, interference with POTW operations, or pass-through to receiving waters. Specific prohibited categories include grease/oil/lint/sand trap effluents beyond ordinance limits, and stormwater illicit discharges (§ 8-12.413, Storm Water Runoff Pollution Prevention).

### Discharge Limits: pH and Temperature (§ 8-12.409)
- **pH**: Must be between **5.5 and 9.5**; wastewater shall not have corrosive properties capable of causing damage or hazard to structures, equipment, or personnel of the collection and treatment system.
- **Temperature**: Must not exceed **120°F (48.9°C)** at the point of discharge; heat in quantities shall not cause the temperature at the POTW treatment plant to exceed 40°C (104°F) unless alternate limits are approved by the approval authority.

### Metal and Phenol Concentrations (§ 8-12.408)
Section 8-12.408 prohibits discharges exceeding specified concentrations of metals and phenols. The specific numeric limits table is in the municipal code text (ecode360.com/43656017 — Cloudflare-protected; verify via City of Santa Maria Utilities Department directly). The section is titled "Metal and phenol concentrations" and includes limits for multiple metals.

### Federal Categorical Pretreatment Standards (§ 8-12.412)
Upon promulgation of federal categorical pretreatment standards for a particular industrial subcategory, the federal standard — if more stringent than local limits — immediately supersedes local limits. Dilution as a substitute for treatment is prohibited.

## Wastewater Discharge Permit Process

**Who must obtain a permit**: All Class III and Class IV users proposing to connect to or discharge into a community sewer must obtain a Wastewater Discharge Permit before connecting. Existing users as of the ordinance effective date had one year to obtain permits (§ 8-12 Article 10, § 8-12.1002).

**Application contents** (§ 8-12 Article 10): Submit a discharge report to the Director of Utilities including:
- Process description
- Flow rate (gallons/day)
- BOD concentration and loading
- TSS concentration and loading
- TDS concentration and loading
- Sodium (Na) and Chloride (Cl) concentrations
- SIC code
- Hours of operation
- Number of employees

**Permit terms**: Set by the Director of Utilities; may include self-monitoring requirements with specified sampling locations, frequency, analytical methods, and reporting schedules.

**Appeals**: Appeals from Director of Utilities rulings go to the City Manager (§ 8-1.07, as amended by Ordinance No. 2024-__); City Manager's action is final per § 8-12.1406.

## Local Rationale for Tight Controls
The City's groundwater contains high concentrations of minerals and solids; return flows to the wastewater treatment plant must be regulated to protect the facility. This is explicitly cited in the findings of Ordinance No. 2025-05.
