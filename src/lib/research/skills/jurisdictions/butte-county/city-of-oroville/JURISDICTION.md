> Local EHS authority reference for the **City of Oroville** (Butte County seat, pop. ~20,000). Verify the live page before quoting; the 2025 California Building Standards Code (Title 24) replaced the 2022 edition statewide on 2026-01-01, so confirm the adopted edition.

## EHS Local Rules by Family

### Air
- **District: Butte County Air Quality Management District (BCAQMD)** — the sole air permit authority inside Oroville city limits. The City has no independent air program. Stationary-source permits, CEQA air analysis, and burn-day declarations are all through BCAQMD.
- BCAQMD permit-to-operate applications, fees, and rule list: https://www.bcaqmd.org/
- [VERIFY] BCAQMD Rule 200 series governs permit requirements; Rule 400 series governs emission standards — confirm current adoption on BCAQMD live site.

### CUPA (Unified Hazardous Materials Program)
- **Not its own CUPA** — served by **Butte County Public Health Department, Environmental Health Division** (countywide CUPA). CUPA office is at 202 Mira Loma Drive, Oroville, CA 95965; (530) 552-3880.
- Programs administered: UST (Underground Storage Tanks), AST/APSA (Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act), Hazardous Waste generator/treatment, HMRRP/Business Plan (Hazardous Materials Release Response Plan), CalARP (Accidental Release Prevention), Site Assessment/Remediation Oversight.
- Fee schedule: Butte County Master Fee Schedule (eff. October 1, 2025) — CUPA section. See `cupa-local.md` for extracted fee table.
- Reporting portal: California Environmental Reporting System (CERS) — https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/
- Source: https://www.buttecounty.net/764/CUPA

### Fire
- **AHJ: City of Oroville Fire Department** (administers permits and inspections), but **operationally staffed by CAL FIRE / Butte County Fire** under a cooperative agreement effective July 2022. Fire Marshal contact: (530) 990-0541; station address: 2055 Lincoln Street.
- Adopted code: **California Fire Code (CFC), Title 24 Part 9** — incorporated by reference in Oroville Municipal Code **Chapter 15.60**. Most recent local amendment: **Ordinance No. 1888** (adopted March 18, 2025), which revised the local amendments to the CFC.
- Local amendments are made pursuant to H&S Code §§ 17958.7, 18941.5(c), 18942, based on local climatic, geological, and topographical conditions (fire-weather risk, wildland-urban interface).
- Permit fees set by City Council resolution. Burn permits require in-person pickup; fire safety/business permits through the Fire Marshal's office.
- Source: https://ecode360.com/44174116 (Ch. 15.60); https://www.orovilleca.gov/208/Fire-Department

### Wastewater
- **Collection system:** City of Oroville owns and operates ~66 miles of gravity mains, 1,200 manholes, 2.1 miles of force main, and 7 lift stations inside city limits. Two other districts (Thermalito Water and Sewer District, Lake Oroville Area Public Utility District) serve adjacent areas — check utility map.
- **Treatment / pretreatment:** **Sewerage Commission – Oroville Region (SC-OR / SCORCA)** is the regional POTW, a Joint Powers Agency formed 1973 by City of Oroville, LOAPUD, and Thermalito Water and Sewer District. Plant: 2880 S. 5th Ave, Oroville, CA 95965; (530) 534-0353.
- SC-OR administers an **Industrial Pretreatment Program** (federally/state mandated). Industrial users must obtain an Industrial User Permit from SC-OR.
- [VERIFY] SC-OR sewer use / pretreatment ordinance number and chapter. Contact SC-OR directly at (530) 534-0353 or https://www.scorca.gov/industrial-user-permits.
- City sewer regulations: Oroville Municipal Code includes stormwater/illicit-discharge prohibitions (Title 13 area — exact chapter [VERIFY] via ecode360.com/OR4980).
- Source: https://www.scorca.gov; https://www.orovilleca.gov/314/Sanitary-Sewers

### Stormwater
- **MS4 permit holder:** City of Oroville is a Phase II MS4 co-permittee under a Statewide General Permit or Regional Board order — [VERIFY] current permit number with Central Valley RWQCB (Region 5).

## Notes

- GOTCHA (recent consolidation): the city's fire service was transitioned to CAL FIRE / Butte County Fire under a cooperative agreement in July 2022; the "Oroville Fire Department" name persists but it is no longer city-operated.
- Despite being the county seat and hosting the county CUPA office, Oroville does **not** run its own CUPA — it is covered by Butte County Environmental Health.
- SCORCA (the regional POTW) is a separate JPA — industrial discharge permits come from SC-OR, not the city.
