## Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ)

**City of Oroville Fire Department** — issues permits, conducts fire inspections, and enforces the fire code within city limits.

> GOTCHA: Since July 2022, the city contracted with CAL FIRE / Butte County Fire under a cooperative agreement. Firefighters are state employees; stations are CAL FIRE-staffed. However, the **Fire Marshal** function for permitting and code enforcement remains with the city. For records **after July 1, 2022**, request fire incident reports from CAL FIRE directly.

## Local Amendments

Local amendments to the CFC are adopted pursuant to California Health and Safety Code §§ 17958.7, 18941.5(c), and 18942. The city council expressly finds that amendments are necessary for the protection of public health, safety, and welfare due to **local climatic, geological, or topographical conditions**, specifically:

- High fire-weather risk (hot, dry summers; Diablo/north wind events)
- Wildland-urban interface (WUI) conditions at city edges near Feather River canyon and foothill terrain
- Emergency response time delays due to topography
- Seismic considerations (geological)

## Permit Types

Based on the city's fire code framework:
- **Burn permits** — residential and agricultural; issued in-person at City Hall or Fire Department; valid 1 year from issuance; Ordinance 1861 / Chapter 13.08 governs open/dooryard burning.
- **Business fire safety permits** — operational permits for hazardous occupancies, high-piled storage, spray booths, compressed gas, welding, and similar uses covered by CFC Chapter 1 permit list.
- **Fire inspections** — pre-occupancy and periodic inspections through Fire Marshal.

Burn permit application: online through Civic Access Portal or in-person.

## Permit Fees

Permit fees set by **City Council resolution** (not by fixed ordinance dollar amounts). A permit shall not be issued until fees are paid; fees are set to cover cost of Fire Department review and inspection.

The city Master Fee Schedule (June 2025) contains current fire permit fees. Contact Fire Administration at (530) 538-2481 or request the fee schedule from City Hall.

## Fire Hazard Severity Zone

The city has published a 2025 Fire Hazard Severity Zone (FHSZ) map: https://www.orovilleca.gov/381/2025-Fire-Hazard-Severity-Zone-Map
Properties in the Very High FHSZ are subject to additional defensible-space and building-ignition-resistance requirements.

## Wildland-Urban Interface

Butte County Community Wildfire Protection Plan applies. The Fire Department references the Butte County CWPP for evacuation zone mapping and community wildfire risk assessment.
