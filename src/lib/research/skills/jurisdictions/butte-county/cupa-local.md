## CUPA Agency

**Butte County Public Health Department, Environmental Health Division** is the single Certified Unified Program Agency (CUPA) for all of Butte County, including all five incorporated cities (Chico, Oroville, Paradise, Gridley, Biggs). No city in Butte County operates its own CUPA or Participating Agency (PA).

- Walk-in / mailing address: 25 County Center Drive, Oroville, CA 95965
- Phone: (530) 552-3880 (Monday–Friday, business hours)
- Email / CERS contact: ccers@buttecounty.net
- After-hours environmental emergencies: contact local fire department
- CalEPA CERS regulator entry (type: CUPA): https://cersapps.calepa.ca.gov/Public/Directory/RegulatorDetails/1008

## Program Elements Administered

The CUPA consolidates all six state-mandated Unified Program elements under the California Health and Safety Code and California Code of Regulations:

1. **Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP) / HMRRP** — Cal HSC §25500 et seq.; community right-to-know inventory, release response plans
2. **CalARP** — California Accidental Release Prevention Program; Risk Management Plans for regulated substances
3. **Underground Storage Tank (UST)** — permitting, inspection, closure certification for tanks storing hazardous substances
4. **Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA)** — SPCC Plan review and inspection for above-threshold petroleum storage
5. **Hazardous Waste Generator + Tiered On-Site Treatment** — generator registration; tiered permitting (PBR, Conditionally Authorized, CA-Limited, CESQT, CESW)
6. **HMMP/HMIS (California Fire Code)** — Hazardous Materials Management Plan and Inventory Statement per California Fire Code Chapter 50

**Inventory size**: five inspectors + one manager; >1,200 regulated facilities countywide.

## Reporting Thresholds (State Standard — No Locally Stricter Thresholds Found)

- **HMBP trigger**: ≥55 gallons liquid, ≥500 lbs solid, or ≥200 cubic feet compressed gas of any hazardous material handled at any one time during the reporting year.
- **CalARP trigger**: any regulated substance at any quantity at a stationary source.
- **UST trigger**: any underground storage tank containing hazardous substances (including motor fuels).
- **APSA trigger**: single AST ≥1,320 gallons liquid petroleum OR cumulative ASTs ≥1,320 gallons (only tanks ≥55 gallons count toward cumulative). Farm/nursery/logging/construction site exemption applies if no single tank >20,000 gal and cumulative ≤100,000 gal.
- **Hazardous Waste Generator**: any quantity of ignitable, corrosive, reactive, or toxic waste triggers generator registration.

## Forms, Submission, and Reporting Portal

- **California Environmental Reporting System (CERS)**: cers.calepa.ca.gov — primary portal for HMBP, CalARP, and most program submissions. Facilities with covered processes must report in CERS.
- **Paper / email applications**: Environmental Health Division accepts applications by email; see https://www.buttecounty.net/764/CUPA for current forms for each program element (UST installation/closure permits, AST/APSA registration, hazardous waste generator registration, CalARP RMP).
- **File Review Request Form (Phase I/II ESA)**: available on the county document portal for environmental due diligence records requests.

## Local Hazmat Ordinance / County Code Chapter

No standalone local hazmat ordinance beyond the state HSC framework was identified in Butte County's Code of Ordinances. The CUPA operates under state authority (Cal HSC §25404 et seq.) as administered by the county. The California Fire Code Hazardous Materials chapters (CFC Chapter 50) apply countywide under the building/fire code adoption in Chapter 38A of the county code.

## Notes for Research Subagent

- The CUPA is in Oroville; however, the county serves all of Chico — the largest city — for all CUPA functions, even though Chico has its own building and fire department.
- Inspection compliance is measured against California Health and Safety Code, California Code of Regulations Title 27, and applicable Code of Federal Regulations.
- For spill reporting: business hours call EH at (530) 552-3880; off-hours call local fire department or 911.
