## Adopted CFC Edition

**2025 California Fire Code** (California Code of Regulations, Title 24, Part 9) and **2025 California Wildland-Urban Interface Code** (CCR Title 24, Part 7).

Adopted by **Ordinance No. 787.11** (introduced 2025-11-18, adopted by Riverside County Board of Supervisors 2025-12-09). Replaces 2022 CFC previously in force under Ord. 787.10.

Authority: Health and Safety Code §§ 17958, 17958.5, 17958.7, and 18941.5 (local climatic, geological, topographical modifications).

## Municipal Code Chapter

County Code **Title 8, Chapter 8.32** (Fire Code). The ordinance is codified there. Ord. 787 was first adopted as Chapter 8.32 and each amendment carries a new decimal suffix (787.1 through 787.11).

## AHJ

**Riverside County Fire Department** — operated under contract by **CAL FIRE** (Riverside Unit, RRU). The Fire Chief of the Riverside County Fire Department (or CAL FIRE Unit Chief) is the Fire Code Official for unincorporated Riverside County and approximately 20 contract cities.

## Key Local Amendments to the 2025 CFC (from Ord. 787.11, Section 5)

### Fees
- **Section 108.2** (CFC) deleted and replaced: Fees for services and permits are set in **Riverside County Ordinance No. 671** (Fees for Construction Inspection Services).
- **Section 108.7 added** (Cost recovery): All RCFD response costs — inspection, enforcement, fire suppression, hazmat containment, rescue — may be charged to the responsible party (HSC §§ 13009 et seq., 25514 et seq., 25540–25541; Gov't Code § 53150 et seq.).

### Automatic Fire Sprinklers (Section 903 amendment)
- **New buildings ≥ 3,600 sq ft** require an approved automatic sprinkler system regardless of occupancy classification (more restrictive than state baseline).
- **Existing buildings** (except 1- and 2-family dwellings) must be retrofitted when square footage exceeds 3,600 sq ft and there is a change of use/occupancy, removal of a fire wall, or additions ≥ 50% of original building area resulting in ≥ 5,000 sq ft total.
- **1- and 2-family dwellings** require sprinklers regardless of square footage per the California Residential Code.
- Detached accessory buildings ≥ 3,600 sq ft require sprinklers (§ 902.2; CRC § R317.5.1 design option allowed for private garages).

### High-Piled Storage (Section 3206.4.2 added)
- Client-leased or occupant-owned warehouses with **high pile storage** must base sprinkler system designs on **NFPA 13**.
- A **Professional Engineer licensed in California** must conduct an on-site survey determining commodity classification, storage configuration, building height, and seasonal fluctuations before system design.
- A technical report describing commodity classification, sprinkler design basis, and commodity isolation/separation methods must be provided at plan review.

### Fire Apparatus Access Roads (Section 503.2.1 amendment)
- Minimum width **24 feet** (exclusive of shoulders); unobstructed vertical clearance ≥ 13 ft 6 in.
- Where a passenger drop-off or pick-up area is provided, an additional 8 feet of width is required.
- Fire Code Official is the sole authority to designate fire apparatus access roads and fire lanes.

### Fire Hydrant Sizes (Section 507.5.7 added)
- Residential standard: one 4-inch outlet and one 2.5-inch outlet minimum.
- (Additional specifications determined by Fire Code Official based on project type.)

### Fire Command Centers (Section 508.1 amendment)
- Buildings > 300,000 sq ft or Group I-2 occupancies with occupied floors > 75 ft above lowest fire department access must provide a fire command center adjacent to the main lobby.
- Minimum size: total building area/500 or 200 sq ft, whichever is greater; minimum dimension 0.7 × √area or 10 ft, whichever is greater.

### WUI Code Application (Section 102.13.1 added)
- Where conflict exists between the Riverside County Fire Code and the California WUI Code, the more restrictive provision applies.

### Hazardous Fire Area Closure Authority (Section 104.12 added)
- Fire Chief is authorized to close hazardous fire areas; closures exceeding 15 days require Board of Supervisors approval within 15 days.

## Operational Permits Required (Section 105.2 — WUI area activities)

In wildland-urban interface areas the following operational permits are required:
automobile wrecking yards; candles/open flames in assembly areas; explosives or blasting agents; fireworks; flammable or combustible liquids; hazardous materials; liquefied petroleum gases; landscape and fuel modification zones; lumberyards; motor vehicle fuel-dispensing stations; open burning; pallet yards; pyrotechnical special effects material; tents, canopies and temporary membrane structures; tire storage; welding and cutting operations.

Violations punishable per **Ordinance No. 725** and Health & Safety Code §§ 17995–17995.5.
