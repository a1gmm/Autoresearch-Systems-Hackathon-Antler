## CUPA Status

**City of Riverside is NOT its own CUPA.**

The certified CUPA for Riverside is **Riverside County Department of Environmental Health (RCDEH) — Hazardous Materials Branch**, which oversees all six unified programs county-wide.

The City of Riverside Fire Department (RFD) Fire Prevention Bureau has been a **Participating Agency (PA)** since **1998**, administering the Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP) program within city limits only.

The only other participating agency under Riverside County DEH's CUPA umbrella is the City of Corona.

## Governing Local Ordinance

**RMC Chapter 9.48 — Unified Hazardous Materials Program**
(Title 9 — Peace, Safety and Morals, Riverside Municipal Code)

This chapter establishes the City's authority to implement two of the six unified programs: HMBP and CFC hazardous materials provisions (HMMP/HMIS). It implements California Health and Safety Code Chapter 6.95.

## Programs Administered by City (as PA)

Per the 2023 City Council memorandum (Legistar Item CUPA Fee Schedule), the six CUPA programs are:
1. Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA) Program
2. Area Plans for Hazardous Materials Emergencies
3. California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP) Program
4. Hazardous Materials Release Response Plans and Inventories (HMBP — Business Plans)
5. Hazardous Material Management Plan (HMMP) / Inventory Statements (HMIS) (California Fire Code)
6. Hazardous Waste Generator / Onsite Hazardous Waste Treatment (tiered permitting)
7. Underground Storage Tank (UST) Programs

The City as PA focuses on: **HMBP (program 4), CFC-based HMMP/HMIS (program 5), and APSA (program 1).**

## HMBP Thresholds (Reporting Requirements)

Businesses must file an HMBP in CERS when storing:
- **≥ 55 gallons** of liquid hazardous materials
- **≥ 500 pounds** of solid hazardous materials
- **≥ 200 cubic feet** of compressed gas

Lower thresholds apply for acutely or extremely hazardous materials.

## Filing System

**CERS (California Environmental Reporting System)** is the only approved filing method per AB 2286 (effective January 1, 2013): https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/

Plans are submitted and renewed **annually**; due dates align with Riverside County DEH permit expiration.

## Fee Schedule

Fees are established under **RMC Chapter 3.30, Section 3.30.030(VII)** (Schedule of Regulations, Products and Services). The 2023 city council action (November 28, 2023 hearing) adopted fees recovering 100% of actual costs per FY 2020/21 CUPA fee study. A single fee billing system is administered through Riverside County DEH.

| Program / Tier | Annual Fee |
|----------------|-----------|
| Hazardous Materials Usage/Permit I (HMBP) | $532.00 |
| Hazardous Materials Usage/Permit II (HMBP) | $610.00 |
| Hazardous Materials Usage/Permit III (HMBP) | $737.00 |
| Hazardous Materials Usage/Permit IV (HMBP) | $1,078.00 |
| Hazardous Materials Usage/Permit V (HMBP) | $1,195.00 |
| APSA — Tier I | $649.00 |
| APSA — Tier II | $805.00 |
| APSA — Tier III | $961.00 |

*(Source: City Council Memorandum, November 28, 2023; prepared by Fire Marshal Craig Kodat)*

Inspections are required on a **triennial basis** (once every three years) per the CA H&S Code. An April 2022 CUPA audit by RCDEH directed the City to implement a single fee billing system.

## Penalty Provisions

Late penalties: **20% after 60 days; additional 80% after 90 days** per the City fee resolution.
