## CUPA Agency

**Riverside County Department of Environmental Health (DEH)** is the CUPA for unincorporated Riverside County and the majority of incorporated cities within the county.

Exceptions (Participating Agencies under county CUPA for HMBP only):
- City of Riverside Fire Department
- City of Corona Fire Department
(Neither is an independent CUPA; they administer HMBP inspections within their city limits as Participating Agencies under the county CUPA umbrella.)

## Programs Administered by Riverside County DEH-CUPA

1. **Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP)** — HSC Ch. 6.95; Ord. 651.5; Ch. 8.64
2. **Hazardous Waste Generator** — HSC Ch. 6.5; Ord. 615; Ch. 8.60
3. **Underground Storage Tank (UST)** — HSC Ch. 6.7 §§ 25280 et seq.; Ord. 617; Ch. 8.140
4. **Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA)** — HSC Ch. 6.67 (SPCC Plan required)
5. **California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP) / Risk Management Plan (RMP)** — HSC Ch. 6.95
6. **Uniform Fire Code hazmat inspections** (coordinated with Riverside County Fire Dept.)

## Hazardous Materials Handling Classification (Ord. 651.5, Section 6)

All reporting thresholds default to HSC Ch. 6.95 definitions unless otherwise noted. Ord. 651.5 establishes three broad categories:

- **Category A** — routine commercial materials (motor vehicle fuels in USTs, lubricants/coolants, dielectric fluids, hydraulic fluids, used motor oil, welding gases, CO₂ at food facilities, dry cleaning chemicals, nitrogen at food/medical/automotive facilities, etc.) — subject to disclosure thresholds set in HSC Ch. 6.95.
- Categories B and C — additional hazardous materials not enumerated in Category A, subject to full HMBP disclosure and permit requirements.

A business below reportable quantities may still be required to obtain a permit if the DEH Director finds compliance necessary to protect health, safety, or the environment.

## Permit Requirements

- **Permit required** when hazardous materials handled ≥ quantities in Ord. 651.5 Section 6.
- **Business Plan submission:** Electronically through CalEHSA Statewide Information Management System (formerly CERS). Must be accepted by DEH before permit is issued.
- **Annual renewal:** Permits expire on the one-year anniversary of program qualification; renewal due within **30 calendar days** of expiration.
- **Plan updates required within 30 days** for: change of ownership, business name, address/phone, physical location, emergency contact, ≥ 100% increase in a disclosed hazardous material quantity, or addition of any previously undisclosed hazardous material.
- NFPA 704 hazard identification signs required at storage/handling areas and business entrances for quantities requiring a permit.
- Safety Data Sheets (Title 8 CCR) must be maintained on site.
- Initial employee training required before handling regulated materials.

Fees are set in **Ordinance No. 640** and may be adjusted annually (as of July 1) by CPI without further Board action (per Ord. 640.14, Section 14, effective 2016-07-01).
