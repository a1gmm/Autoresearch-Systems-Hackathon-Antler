## EHS Local Rules by Family

### Air

- **District: Great Basin Unified Air Pollution Control District (GBUAPCD)** — Alpine County joined Inyo and Mono Counties in 1974 under a joint powers agreement to form GBUAPCD. GBUAPCD is the sole air permitting authority for stationary sources in Alpine County; it issues Authorities to Construct and Permits to Operate. The county's own Chapter 16.04 (Air Pollution Control) predates the joint district and is superseded in practice by GBUAPCD rules.
- Permit application filing fee: $100; additional fees set by the GBUAPCD fee schedule determined during application review. Contact: 760-872-8211 / permits@gbuapcd.org.
- Source: https://www.gbuapcd.org/ | https://www.gbuapcd.org/permits/
- Alpine County Code Chapter 16.04 (legacy county Air Pollution Control District rules, Ord. 325, 1972) — superseded by GBUAPCD. Source: https://www.codepublishing.com/CA/AlpineCounty/html/AlpineCounty16/AlpineCounty1604.html

### CUPA

- **Agency: Alpine County Health and Human Services (HHS)** — CalEPA-certified CUPA; administers all six Unified Program elements: Hazardous Materials Business Plans (HMBP/CERS), Hazardous Waste Generator and On-Site Treatment, UST, APSA/SPCC, CalARP, and Cal Fire Code HMRRP. No participating agencies (zero incorporated cities).
- CERS mandatory since Jan 1, 2013 for all CUPA-regulated businesses.

### Wastewater

- **No countywide POTW.** Alpine County is rural with no centralized municipal sewer. Two principal wastewater service entities:
  - **Bear Valley Water District** — serves ~650 EDUs in Bear Valley community; secondary treatment via aerated lagoons with chlorine disinfection; disposal by spray irrigation and surface discharge to Bloods Creek; regulated under Central Valley RWQCB Waste Discharge Requirements Order No. R5-2022-0037 (design flow 0.5 MGD). No industrial pretreatment program published. Source: https://bvwd.ca.gov/treatment.aspx
  - Markleeville and other communities rely on on-site septic systems regulated by Alpine County Health Dept. under County Code Chapter 13.08 (Sewage Disposal Systems, Ord. 684, 2008).
- **County discharge prohibition (Chapter 16.12):** Unlawful to discharge sewage or industrial waste from outside-county basins into the county's unincorporated areas (Ord. 255, 1965). Facilities serving >3,000 inhabitants may not discharge into the West Fork Carson River, Markleeville Creek, or East Fork Carson River above specified reference points (Ord. 256, 1965). Industrial waste discharge requires prior report filing (Art. III).
- No local pretreatment/IWP program found — consistent with absence of a Phase I/II POTW pretreatment program; any significant industrial discharger would need Waste Discharge Requirements directly from Central Valley or Lahontan RWQCB.
- Source: https://www.codepublishing.com/CA/AlpineCounty/html/AlpineCounty16/AlpineCounty1612.html | https://www.codepublishing.com/CA/AlpineCounty/html/AlpineCounty13/AlpineCounty1308.html

### Stormwater

- Alpine County is not identified as a Phase I or Phase II MS4 permittee by Lahontan RWQCB or Central Valley RWQCB. Given the county's population (~1,200), it falls well below Phase II thresholds and has no formal MS4 permit.
- Construction stormwater in portions of Alpine County within the Lake Tahoe Hydrologic Unit is covered by the Lahontan RWQCB's NPDES Construction Stormwater Permit for the Lake Tahoe basin.
- General Construction / Industrial NPDES general permits (State Water Board) apply statewide; facilities must file NOIs where applicable.
- Source: https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/lahontan/water_issues/programs/storm_water/ [VERIFY Lahontan vs. Central Valley RWQCB split for Alpine County sub-basins]

### Zoning

- **Single industrial zone: IND (Industrial Zone), Chapter 18.48.** No M-1/M-2 split — only one industrial designation for the entire county.
- Permitted by-right: agriculture; all commercial uses permitted in C districts; manufacturing, warehousing, and commercial uses not requiring a use permit.
- Conditional use permit (CUP) required for: residences for owner/caretaker; commercial excavation; oil/gas/geothermal drilling; waste dumping/incineration; fat rendering; hog raising; junkyards; manufacturing of acid, cement, explosives, fireworks, fertilizer, gas, glue, gypsum, inflammable fluids; petroleum refining; smelting; stockyards/slaughterhouses; tanneries and uses with objectionable noise/odor/smoke/dust/vibration or explosive/dangerous materials; third-party hazardous waste recycling/handling/storage; airports/airstrips.
- Development standards: Lot area ≥1 acre; front yard ≥20 ft; side/rear yards: none unless abutting another zone (then ≥50 ft); max height 45 ft.
- Adopted by Ord. 453 (1985), amended by Ord. 480 (1988).
- Non-industrial use within 36 months may trigger rezoning proceedings (§18.48.060).
- Source: https://www.codepublishing.com/CA/AlpineCounty/html/AlpineCounty18/AlpineCounty1848.html

## Notes

- GOTCHA (no incorporated cities): Alpine County has **zero incorporated cities** — every community (Markleeville the county seat, Bear Valley, Kirkwood, Woodfords, etc.) is unincorporated. All local EHS authority is the county's; there are no municipal CUPAs or municipal fire departments. (Only Mariposa and Trinity counties share this trait in CA.)
- California's least populous county (~1,200 residents), heavily federal land (Humboldt-Toiyabe / Eldorado / Stanislaus National Forests) — expect federal-land carve-outs where USFS, not county, is the land manager.
- **Air district is GBUAPCD** (Inyo/Mono/Alpine joint district, formed 1974), not a local county APCD. The county's own Chapter 16.04 is legacy and superseded.
