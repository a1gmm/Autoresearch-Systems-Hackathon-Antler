## POTW Situation

Alpine County has **no centralized countywide POTW.** The county is entirely unincorporated and sparsely populated (~1,200 residents). Wastewater is handled through:
1. Two small community systems operated by special districts
2. On-site septic systems for the majority of residences and businesses

## Community Sewer Systems

### Bear Valley Water District (BVWD)
- **Service area:** Bear Valley community, Alpine County
- **System size:** ~650 residential and commercial equivalent dwelling units (EDUs)
- **Treatment process:** Secondary treatment — aerated lagoons, chlorine disinfection, polishing reservoir; disposal by spray irrigation and surface discharge to Bloods Creek
- **Design flow:** 0.5 million gallons per day (500,000 GPD); has received >1.5 MGD during hydraulic events
- **Regulating body:** Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB Region 5)
- **Permit type:** Waste Discharge Requirements (WDR) — not an NPDES POTW permit
- **Current WDR Order:** No. R5-2022-0037 (prior: Order No. 5-01-208)
- **Industrial pretreatment program:** None published. BVWD is not a POTW with a federally-approved pretreatment program. Industrial or commercial dischargers connecting to BVWD should contact the district directly to confirm discharge acceptance.
- Source: https://bvwd.ca.gov/treatment.aspx

## County Code: Sewage Disposal Systems (Chapter 13.08)

- **Ordinance:** Ord. 684 (2008), codified at Alpine County Code Chapter 13.08
- **Regulator:** Alpine County Health Officer (Environmental Health division)
- **Scope:** On-site sewage disposal systems (conventional septic, alternative systems, packaged sewage treatment plants) for private sewage; does not govern public/community sewer mains.
- **Permit requirement (§13.08.050):** A sewage permit from the Health Officer is required before constructing, altering, or repairing any on-site sewage disposal system.
- **Building permit linkage (§13.08.040):** No building permit issued without a valid sewage permit or connection to an approved community sewer.
- **Special design / nonresidential systems (§13.08.130):** Commercial and industrial on-site systems require special design review.
- **Alternative systems (§13.08.080):** Aerobic systems, mound absorption, ET beds, and State Water Board-approved alternative technologies permitted.
- **Minimum parcel size (§13.08.070):** Parcel minimums apply to new land divisions to ensure adequate area for sewage disposal.
- **Enforcement (§13.08.210):** Health Officer may issue correction notices, abatement orders, and impose penalties for violations.

## County Code: Discharge Prohibition (Chapter 16.12)

- **Municipal code chapter:** 16.12 (Disposal of Water and Sewage in Unincorporated Areas), Title 16 (Environment)
- **Ordinance:** Ord. 255 (1965) (Art. I), Ord. 256 (1965) (Art. II)

### Article I — General Prohibition (§16.12.010)
It is unlawful to discharge sewage or industrial waste (or its effluent) that originates in a water basin entirely outside Alpine County into the county's unincorporated areas.
- Penalty: misdemeanor, up to $500 fine and/or 6 months imprisonment per day of violation.

### Article II — Large Facility Discharge Prohibition (§16.12.050)
Facilities serving **more than 3,000 inhabitants** may not discharge sewage, industrial waste, or treated effluent into:
- West Fork of the Carson River and all tributaries upstream from ½ mile below the county highway bridge on Alpine County Road No. 3
- Markleeville Creek and all tributaries upstream from the bridge at State Routes 4 and 89 in Markleeville
- East Fork of the Carson River and all tributaries upstream from Hangman's Bridge (SR 4/89)
- Penalty: misdemeanor, up to $500 fine and/or 6 months imprisonment per day of violation.

### Article III — Industrial Discharge Reports (§§16.12.090–16.12.250)
Any person proposing to discharge or changing a discharge of sewage or industrial waste must file a report with the county. "Industrial waste" is defined broadly as any liquid or solid waste substance not sewage from producing, manufacturing, or processing operations.
- The county may establish waste assimilation capacity guidelines (§16.12.140).
- Failure to file a report is unlawful; county may seek injunction (§16.12.170).
- County may issue cease and desist orders for unlawful discharges (§16.12.200).

## Industrial Wastewater Discharge Permits (IWP)

No local IWP program exists — Alpine County has no POTW with a federally delegated pretreatment program. A significant industrial discharger would need to obtain Waste Discharge Requirements directly from:
- **Central Valley RWQCB** (Region 5) for non-Tahoe basin areas — https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/centralvalley/
- **Lahontan RWQCB** (Region 6) for Tahoe basin portions [VERIFY regional boundary for specific sites]

Contact Alpine County Environmental Health (530-694-2235 ext 235) for on-site sewage system permitting for commercial and industrial facilities.
