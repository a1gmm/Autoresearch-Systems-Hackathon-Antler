> Research reference for industrial wastewater pretreatment and sewer discharge into the City of San Rafael system.
> Two-tier structure: (1) San Rafael Sanitation District (SRSD) owns/operates the collection system; (2) Central Marin Sanitation Agency (CMSA) owns/operates the POTW and administers the pretreatment program. Industrial users must obtain a Wastewater Discharge Permit from CMSA. SRSD sewer connection fees are separate.

## Agency Structure

### Collection System — San Rafael Sanitation District (SRSD)

- **Agency:** San Rafael Sanitation District (SRSD) — operates ~132 miles of pipeline and 32 pump stations serving central San Rafael and adjacent unincorporated areas.
- **Address:** 111 Morphew Street, San Rafael, CA 94901
- **Phone:** (415) 454-4001
- **Email:** srsd@cityofsanrafael.org
- **Website:** https://www.cityofsanrafael.org/departments/sanitation-district/
- **Local sewer code:** SRMC Title 15 (Utilities), Chapter 15.06 (Sewer District — Standards and Minimum Requirements), including §15.06.030 (Local rules): https://library.municode.com/ca/san_rafael/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT15SU_CH15.06SUDESTMIRE_15.06.030LO
- SRSD handles collection system connections; industrial pretreatment permits flow through CMSA.

### Treatment Plant and Pretreatment Authority — Central Marin Sanitation Agency (CMSA)

- **Agency:** Central Marin Sanitation Agency (CMSA) — joint powers agency formed October 15, 1979; member agencies are SRSD, Ross Valley Sanitary District (RVSD), and Sanitary District No. 2 of Marin County (SD2).
- **NPDES Permit:** Order R2-2023-0006, Permit No. CA0038628 (San Francisco Bay Regional Water Quality Control Board).
- **Address:** 1301 Andersen Drive, San Rafael, CA 94901 (POTW location)
- **Website:** https://www.cmsa.us/
- **Permits page:** https://www.cmsa.us/services/permits

## Local Discharge Limits (CMSA Ordinance No. 2018-2, Section 3.D)

CMSA is authorized to establish Local Limits pursuant to 40 CFR 403.5(c). The following Daily Maximum Limits apply to all dischargers. Exceedance is a violation enforceable by penalty.

**pH:** No discharge shall have a pH < 6.0 or ≥ 12.5.

**Pollutant Daily Maximum Concentrations (mg/L unless noted):**

| Pollutant | Daily Maximum Concentration (mg/L) |
|---|---|
| Arsenic | 0.59 |
| Cadmium | 0.13 |
| Chromium (Total) | 2.0 |
| Copper | 1.5 |
| Lead | 1.6 |
| Mercury | 0.011 |
| Nickel | 1.0 |
| Silver | 1.0 |
| Zinc | 1.0 (or 2.5 for non-SIU users — see note) |
| Cyanide | 1.2 |
| Oil & Grease | 100 |

All concentrations for metallic substances are for total metal unless otherwise indicated. CMSA may impose mass limitations in addition to concentration-based limits.

**Zinc exception:** For Users who are NOT Significant Industrial Users (SIU), the maximum zinc concentration is **2.5 mg/L** (rather than 1.0 mg/L).

National Categorical Standards (40 CFR Chapter I, Parts 405–471) are incorporated by reference; Categorical Users must comply with whichever is more stringent — local limit or applicable Categorical Standard.

## User Classification (CMSA Ordinance No. 2018-2, Section 5.A)

| Class | Description | Typical Facilities |
|---|---|---|
| Class I | Significant Industrial User (SIU) — all Categorical Industrial Users and non-categorical Users discharging ≥25,000 gpd or with reasonable potential for pass-through or interference | Categorical industrial facilities |
| Class II | Users with potential impact on CMSA ability to meet objectives; not SIU/Class I/III/IV | Manufacturing, mass transit, other complex facilities |
| Class III | Business-activity categories designated by CMSA; standardized "short form" permits | Radiator repair shops, vehicle maintenance/repair facilities (designated categories) |
| Class IV | Contaminated groundwater and similar discharges | Groundwater dischargers, CIPP lining, construction runoff |

A facility in a Class III category may be reclassified as Class II if a standardized permit is inadequate. Class IV permits are issued separately alongside any required Class I/II/III permit.

## Industrial User Permit (IUP) — Process and Fees

### Permit Classes and Fees (CMSA Ordinance No. 2025-1, Section 9)

Fees are adjusted annually based on San Francisco Bay Area CPI.

| User/Permit Class | Description | New Permit Fee | Renewal Permit Fee | Expedited Permit Fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class I | Significant/Categorical Industrial User (SIU/CIU) | $1,464 | $732 | $2,197 |
| Class II | Manufacturing, mass transit, etc. | $732 | $367 | $1,099 |
| Class III | Business activity, radiator repair, auto service | $732 | $367 | $1,099 |
| Class IV | Groundwater discharger, CIPP lining, construction runoff | $488 | $244 | $732 |

- **Standard review timeline:** Approximately 30 calendar days for new and renewal permits. May be extended if additional information is required.
- **Expedited review timeline:** Targets 7 calendar days.

### Capacity Charges (CMSA Ordinance No. 2025-1, Section 3, effective July 1, 2025)

| Connection Type | Capacity Charge |
|---|---|
| Residential (per service unit / EDU) | $7,747.39 |
| Commercial / other non-industrial (per plumbing fixture unit, min $7,747.39) | $484.21 |
| High-strength user (restaurants, bakeries — per fixture unit) | $1,133.05 |
| Industrial / special volumes or strengths | Established by Board action per case |

### Application Process

1. Applicants submit permit application at least 30 calendar days prior to connecting or discharging.
2. Required application information: facility name/address, SIC codes, environmental permits held, process discharge times, average daily and peak flow rates, site/plumbing plans, description of all materials that could be discharged, pollutant concentrations, pretreatment facilities description, spill control plans, and signed certification.
3. CMSA reviews; may conduct site inspection; may require additional sampling.
4. Permit issued with discharge limits, monitoring, and reporting conditions.
5. All Class I, II, III, and IV Users must have an approved permit before discharging.

## Mercury / Dental Amalgam

- **Governing ordinance:** CMSA Ordinance No. 2009-2 (Mercury Reduction Ordinance, adopted December 8, 2009)
- Requires all dental facilities with amalgam removal/placement to install ISO 11143-certified amalgam separators.
- Dental facilities must submit certification of separator installation within 30 days. Separator must remove ≥95% of amalgam.
- Exemptions for limited-amalgam specialty practices (≤3 days/year): orthodontics, periodontics, oral surgery, radiology, oral pathology/medicine, endodontistry, prosthodontistry.
