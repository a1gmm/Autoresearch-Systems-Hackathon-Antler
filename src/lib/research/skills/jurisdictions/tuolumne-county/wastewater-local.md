## POTW / Sanitation District

**Tuolumne Utilities District (TUD)** is the primary wastewater utility serving the greater Sonora area and surrounding communities. TUD is an independent special district, not a county department.

**Sonora Regional Wastewater Treatment Facility (WWTF)**  
1400 Southgate Drive, Sonora, CA 95370  
APN: 056-590-005  
Permitted capacity: designed for ~2 MGD average dry weather flow  
RWQCB Order: R5-2024-0016 (adopted April 19, 2024; Central Valley Region)

**Service area communities (sewer):** Sonora, Columbia, East Sonora, Mono Village, Mono Vista, Ranchos Poquitos, Soulsbyville, Twain Harte, Willow Springs, and Apple Valley Estates. Approximately 6,140 connections; ~140 miles of pipeline.

**Unserved/on-site areas:** Large portions of rural unincorporated Tuolumne County rely on on-site sewage disposal systems (septic). Tuolumne County Ordinance Code Chapter 13.08 (On-Site Sewage Treatment and Disposal Code) governs septic design and permitting, administered by the Environmental Health Division.

## Sewer-Use Ordinance

**TUD Wastewater Discharge Ordinance** (originally adopted August 24, 1993; amended through at least November 13, 2018). This is TUD's own district ordinance — not a chapter of the Tuolumne County Ordinance Code.

The ordinance enables TUD to comply with Clean Water Grant Regulations, Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) Central Valley Region water quality requirements, national standards of performance, toxic and pretreatment effluent standards, and any other discharge criteria required by state or federal law.

## Local Discharge Limits (TUD Wastewater Ordinance, Section 2.08)

Numeric limits on industrial/non-domestic discharges to the TUD sewer system:

### Metals (mg/L, maximum):
| Parameter | Limit (mg/L) |
|---|---|
| Arsenic | 0.1 |
| Cadmium | 0.2 |
| Copper | 5.6 |
| Cyanide | 1.0 |
| Lead | 1.0 |
| Mercury | 0.01 |
| Nickel | 1.0 |
| Silver | 0.2 |
| Total Chromium | 0.5 |
| Zinc | 3.0 |

### Groundwater remediation projects:
| Parameter | Limit (mg/L) |
|---|---|
| Benzene, Toluene, Ethyl benzene, Xylene (BTEX) | 1.0 each |
| Total Petroleum Hydrocarbons (TPH) | 10.0 |

### General prohibitions (Section 2.08(c)):
- Temperature > 150°F (65°C)
- Oil or grease (animal/vegetable origin) > **300 mg/L**
- Oil or grease (mineral/petroleum origin) > **100 mg/L**
- pH < **6.0**
- Total identifiable chlorinated hydrocarbons > 0.02 mg/L
- Phenolic compounds > 1.0 mg/L

### Grease interceptors (Section 2.08(e)):
Required for restaurants, food-service, car washes, quick-lubes, and automotive repair shops. Gravity grease interceptors or hydromechanical grease interceptors required per District standard specifications and TUD Fats, Oils and Grease (FOG) Control Program.

## Industrial Pretreatment Program (IPP)

**No IPP is currently required at TUD.** Per RWQCB Order R5-2024-0016 (Finding 44, adopted April 2024):

> "Currently, there are reportedly no significant industrial wastes being discharged to the Facility... Consequently, an Industrial Pretreatment Program (IPP) will not be required at this time."

However, Order R5-2024-0016 requires TUD to report any proposed new industrial discharges to the Executive Officer. If directed, TUD must develop an IPP. The Order may be revised to require an approved IPP if industrial discharges arise. Biosolids metal concentrations (January 2018–March 2023 data) were confirmed well below 40 C.F.R. Part 503.13 Table 3 limits.

**Practical implication:** New industrial dischargers must notify TUD; TUD will route to RWQCB Central Valley Region if IPP triggers are approached. There is no self-contained local industrial waste permit process at this time — state oversight applies via RWQCB.

## Wastewater Discharge Permits (TUD Ordinance, Section 3.03)

Mandatory discharge permits required for "Critical Users" — defined as SIC Division A, B, D, E, or I facilities that meet any of:
1. Discharge flow ≥ 50,000 gallons/average work day, or
2. Discharge flow > 5% of the District's total system flow, or
3. Waste contains toxic pollutants in toxic amounts (per CWA Section 307(a))

Permit application, duration, transfer, and revocation provisions in TUD Ordinance Article 3.
