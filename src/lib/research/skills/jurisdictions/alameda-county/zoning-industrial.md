Note: most industrial development in Alameda County is within city jurisdictions (Oakland, Fremont, Hayward, etc.), which have their own zoning codes. This file covers only unincorporated Alameda County, administered by the Alameda County Community Development Agency (CDA) — Planning Department.

## Industrial Zone Districts

### M-1 — Light Industrial District
(Source: County of Alameda Zoning Regulations M-1 summary sheet, Alameda County Planning Department, last revised June 18, 2012)

**Intent:** To provide for light industrial, manufacturing and processing uses in suitable areas. To promote a desirable and attractive working environment with a minimum of detriment to surrounding properties. All uses must meet performance standards for noise, vibrations, odor, emissions, inflammable or explosive material. **All uses must be conducted within an enclosed building.**

**Where used:** Rarely used now; most industrial development is within city jurisdictions.

**Permitted Uses (by right):**
- Any manufacturing, processing, assembling, research, wholesale, storage, or utility use conducted entirely within an enclosed building — *other than those specified as conditional uses in the M-1 or M-2 Districts.*

**Conditional Uses (require CUP):**
- Restaurant or store oriented to district uses
- Enclosed outdoor storage yard
- Animal hospital / kennel
- Gasoline storage
- Indoor recreational facility
- Retail sales of industrial and building materials or equipment
- Concrete or asphalt batch plant
- Service station
- (Other uses may be conditionally permitted in any district: airport, disaster or fallout shelter, church, or publicly funded/licensed group rehabilitation living quarters)

**Development Standards:**

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Minimum building site area | 20,000 sq ft |
| Minimum lot width | 100 ft (median) |
| Front yard setback | 20 ft |
| Rear yard setback | 20 ft |
| Side yard setback | 10 ft (30 ft if adjacent to residential district) |
| Maximum height | 45 ft |
| Lot coverage | No requirement |
| Site Development Review | Required for new buildings ≥ 1,000 sq ft, or where aggregate additions since 1977 total ≥ 1,000 sq ft |

**Signs:** Business wall and freestanding or wall-and-projecting signs (25 sq ft guaranteed minimum); low-profile sign; non-illuminated identification signs. Others allowed under CUP. No moving, flashing or intermittent signs. No signs oriented to adjacent residential districts.

### M-2 — General (Heavy) Industrial District
(Source: County of Alameda Zoning Regulations M-2 summary sheet, Alameda County Planning Department, last revised June 18, 2012)

**Intent:** To provide for and protect general industrial uses. To protect adjacent areas from detrimental effects. All uses must meet performance standards for noise, vibrations, odor, emissions and inflammable or explosive material. (Note: M-2 does NOT require all uses to be conducted within an enclosed building, unlike M-1.)

**Where used:** Rarely used now; most industrial development is within city jurisdictions.

**Permitted Uses (by right):**
- Railroad or trucking terminal facility
- Public utility
- Wholesale establishment
- Research laboratory
- Manufacturing, processing or assembly plant or industrial operation not listed as conditional uses

**Conditional Uses (require CUP):**
- Restaurant or shop oriented to district uses
- Garbage/sewage disposal
- Salvage yard
- Manufacture or bulk storage of construction and hazardous materials
- Stockyard
- Kennel / animal hospital
- Drive-in theater
- Amusement park / race track
- Service station

**Development Standards:**

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Minimum building site area | 10,000 sq ft |
| Minimum setback | 50 ft if adjacent to agricultural or residential district |
| Maximum height | 45 ft if within 200 ft of a residential district |
| Lot coverage | No requirement |
| Site Development Review | Not required |

**Signs:** Same as M-1 (business wall and freestanding or wall-and-projecting; 25 sq ft guaranteed minimum; low-profile; non-illuminated identification; others via CUP; no moving/flashing/intermittent; none oriented to residential).

## Performance Standards (Both M-1 and M-2)

All uses in both M-1 and M-2 districts must meet performance standards for:
- Noise
- Vibrations
- Odor
- Emissions
- Inflammable or explosive materials

(Full performance standard text is in Title 17; contact Planning at (510) 670-5410 for details.)

## Conditional Use Permit (CUP) Process

(Source: Alameda County Planning Department "Guide Applying for a Conditional Use Permit," https://www.acgov.org/cda/planning/ordinance/documents/CUP.pdf)

### Required Findings (four findings, all required by law):
1. The use is required by the public need.
2. The use will be properly related to other land uses, transportation and service facilities in the vicinity.
3. The use, if permitted under all circumstances and conditions of the particular case, will not materially adversely affect the health or safety of persons in the vicinity, or be materially detrimental to the public welfare or injurious to property or improvements in the neighborhood.
4. The use will not be contrary to the character or performance standards established for the District.

### Process Steps:
1. **Pre-application:** Talk to a Planning Department staff member (contact Planning at (510) 670-5400) and relevant County agencies to determine required materials.
2. **Application filing:** Submit required applications and materials. Must set up appointment with a Senior Planner to review proposed application.
3. **Staff review:** Planning reviews materials against zoning requirements; refers to public agencies (school, park, fire districts); conducts site visit; writes staff report with recommendation. Public hearing date set; notices mailed and posted.
4. **CEQA review:** Generally exempt; exceptions may apply (additional cost if required).
5. **Public hearing:** Heard before West County Board of Zoning Adjustments OR East County Board of Zoning Adjustments, depending on location; larger projects (schools) go to Planning Commission.
6. **Decision:** Approve, conditionally approve, or deny. Written resolution mailed 2 days after hearing.
7. **Appeal period:** 10-day appeal period; appeal filed with Board of Supervisors. Board of Supervisors decision is final.

**Timeline:** Typically 2–3 months; appeal adds ~2 months.

### Application Fees (deposits):
| Application type | Deposit |
|---|---|
| Most districts (standard CUP) | $2,500 |
| Outdoor recreation facility, sanitary landfill, flight strip, heliport, cemetery, church, or school | $4,000 |
| Wireless Telecommunication Facilities | $6,000 |

> Processing is at actual cost; deposit may be adjusted up or down. Excess funds refunded.

### Required Submittal Materials:
- Complete Standard Application form (signed by property owner)
- Complete Application Supplement Conditional Use Permit form
- Deposit (see above)
- Project drawings to legible scale (plot plan, elevations, etc.)
- Written statement explaining how the proposed use satisfies all four required findings
- Any additional materials requested by Planning Department

### Application portal / forms:
- Contact Planning Department at (510) 670-5400 or visit https://www.acgov.org/cda/planning/ordinance/ordinance-links.htm
- [VERIFY] Online CUP application portal — not found on public website. Applications appear to be paper-based; contact Planning for current submission process.
