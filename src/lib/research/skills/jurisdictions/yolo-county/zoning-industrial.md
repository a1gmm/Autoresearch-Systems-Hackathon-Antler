> This file covers industrial zoning in the **unincorporated** Yolo County area only.
> The four incorporated cities (Davis, Woodland, West Sacramento, Winters) each maintain their own zoning codes — see city-level files.
> Yolo County is a predominantly agricultural county; large industrial zones are limited; most industrial activity is in or near the city limits of Woodland and West Sacramento.

## Industrial Zone Districts

Yolo County unincorporated area has **two industrial zoning designations** under Sec. 8-2.702:

### I-L — Light Industrial Zone

- **Purpose:** Accommodate a limited group of light manufacturing and service uses with little potential to generate noise, odor, vibrations, or similar impacts to adjacent neighbors.
- **Allowed uses include:** equipment sales and repair services; light manufacturing and processing involving non-toxic materials; warehousing and storage; wholesaling and distribution; limited retail, personal services, and food-related uses (to serve area workers or sell products manufactured onsite).
- **Maximum floor area ratio:** 0.5
- **Hazardous materials:** Light industrial designation; uses involving hazardous materials may require a use permit.

### I-H — Heavy Industrial Zone

- **Purpose:** Allow all heavy manufacturing and industrial uses that may create objectionable impacts — noise, odor, vibrations, and use of hazardous materials.
- **Allowed uses include:** processing, fabrication, manufacture, and storage of metals, cement, chemicals, agricultural products, animal carcasses, wood, grain, furniture, heavy equipment, automobiles and trucks, building materials, and similar.
- **Relationship to I-L:** All uses allowed in I-L are also allowed in I-H, except where Table 8-2.704 specifies otherwise.
- **Maximum floor area ratio:** 0.5

## Use Permit (CUP) Process

Yolo County uses a layered permit classification system in its zoning tables (Sec. 8-2.704):

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Allowed by right (no zoning permit required) |
| SP | Site Plan Review (non-discretionary) |
| UP(m) | Minor Use Permit — approved by Zoning Administrator |
| UP(M) | Major Use Permit — requires public hearing before Planning Commission |
| N | Not allowed |

- **Minor Use Permit (UP(m)):** Administrative approval by the Zoning Administrator; typically used for lower-impact conditional uses. No public hearing required.
- **Major Use Permit (UP(M)):** Discretionary approval requiring a noticed public hearing before the **Yolo County Planning Commission**. Used for higher-impact or larger-scale conditional uses, including most heavy industrial operations with significant environmental impact potential.
- **CEQA:** Discretionary approvals (use permits) require CEQA review; the Planning Division conducts or oversees the appropriate CEQA document (categorical exemption, initial study/MND, or EIR).

## Key Gotchas

- **Predominantly agricultural county:** Most unincorporated land is zoned agricultural. Industrial zoning in the unincorporated area is sparse; most EHS-relevant industrial activity will be in or adjacent to city limits (primarily Woodland's industrial parks, or West Sacramento which is governed by the City of West Sacramento).
- **Use permit process:** A Major Use Permit before the Planning Commission is the typical path for any EHS-significant industrial operation (chemical manufacturing, large hazmat storage, CalARP-triggering processes).
- **Incorporated cities have independent zoning codes:** Davis, Woodland, West Sacramento, and Winters each run their own planning and zoning processes — this file covers only the unincorporated county.
