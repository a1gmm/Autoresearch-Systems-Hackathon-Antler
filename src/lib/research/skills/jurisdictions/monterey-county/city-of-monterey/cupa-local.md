## CUPA Agency

**The City of Monterey is NOT its own CUPA.** All six Unified Program elements are administered by:

> **County of Monterey Health Department — Environmental Health Bureau**
> Hazardous Materials Management Services (HMMS)
> 1270 Natividad Road, Salinas, CA 93906
> Phone: (831) 755-4500 (main) / (831) 755-4511 Ext. 1 (Hazmat Specialist)

The HMMS is the state-certified CUPA for all unincorporated Monterey County and all incorporated cities within the county, including the City of Monterey.

## Programs Administered (Six Unified Program Elements)

The HMMS administers all six California Unified Program elements for facilities located within the City of Monterey:

1. **Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP)** — facilities storing or handling hazardous materials at or above state thresholds must submit an HMBP via CERS. Reporting deadline: annually by December 31.
2. **Underground Storage Tank (UST) Program** — annual compliance inspections required (HSC Chapter 6.7, §25288(a)). 221 UST facilities in the county as of the 2021 CalEPA evaluation.
3. **Hazardous Waste Generator Program (HWG)** — includes Tiered Permitting (TP) for on-site treatment. Facilities inspected once every three years (CCR Title 27, §15200(a)(3)(A)).
4. **Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA)** — aboveground petroleum storage tanks ≥ 1,320 gallons aggregate require Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure (SPCC) plan.
5. **California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP)** — administered by HMMS for facilities with regulated substances above threshold quantities. Central Coast RWQCB is co-regulator for water-affecting releases.
6. **Tiered Permitting (TP) / OncoTrak** — on-site hazardous waste treatment under Conditionally Authorized (CA) or Conditionally Exempt (CE) tiers.

## Local Ordinance / Municipal Code

The county's hazardous materials registration authority is codified in:
- **Monterey County Code Title 10 (Health and Safety), Chapter 10.65 — Hazardous Materials Registration**
- Chapter 10.65 covers UST permits (§10.65.040), hazardous materials registration requirements, maximum fee authority, and enforcement.
- Fee cross-reference: Chapter 10.04 (Health Permits — Registration — Fees), §10.04.020 (permit fees schedule by program element).

The City of Monterey has no separate local hazardous materials ordinance. All CUPA-regulated activities within city limits are governed by Monterey County Code Title 10 and state law (Health & Safety Code Chapters 6.5, 6.7, 6.95).

## CERS Electronic Reporting

All regulated facilities must submit program data electronically through the California Environmental Reporting System (CERS):
- Portal: https://cers.calepa.ca.gov
- Monterey County CERS information: https://www.countyofmonterey.gov/government/departments-a-h/health/environmental-health/hazardous-material-management/cers-california-electronic-reporting-system

## Fee Schedule

Environmental Health fees are approved by the Board of Supervisors and take effect July 1 of each fiscal year. Current fees (effective July 1, 2025) are posted at:
https://www.countyofmonterey.gov/government/departments-a-h/health/environmental-health/general/fees

Specific fee amounts for CUPA programs (HMBP, UST, CalARP, etc.) are set by county resolution and indexed annually. The public fee page is the authoritative source; contact HMMS at (831) 755-4511 Ext. 1 for the current fee schedule document.

## CalARP Program

- Administered by HMMS in coordination with CalEPA.
- Facilities with regulated substances above threshold quantities must prepare a Risk Management Plan (RMP) per HSC §25531 et seq.
- Program page: https://www.countyofmonterey.gov/government/departments-a-h/health/environmental-health/hazardous-materials-management/california-accidental-release-prevention-program

## CalEPA Performance Evaluation Note

In the 2021 CalEPA Unified Program Performance Evaluation (evaluation period May 2021 – February 2022), Monterey County HMMS was rated **Unsatisfactory**. Key deficiencies included:
- 33% of HWG facilities not inspected within required 3-year cycle (478 of 1,438 facilities).
- 82% of cited HWG violations lacked documented return-to-compliance (RTC) in CERS.
- Tiered Permitting element not fully developed and implemented.
- 15–24% of UST facilities not inspected annually across evaluation years.

Corrective action progress reports were required beginning February 1, 2023. Verify current compliance status with HMMS before relying on inspection frequency assumptions.
