## Adopted Edition and Adopting Ordinance

- **Adopted code:** 2025 California Fire Code (CFC), which is based on the 2024 International Fire Code (IFC 2024).
- **Adopting ordinance:** Ordinance No. 3712, codified April 2026.
- **Municipal Code chapter:** City of Monterey Municipal Code **Chapter 13** (Fire Protection), current through Ordinance 3713 (November 4, 2025).
- **All appendices** to the 2025 CFC are adopted in their entirety and shall apply (§13-0.2 amendment to §101.2.1).

## AHJ Scope

The City of Monterey Fire Department is the fire Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ) not only for the City of Monterey but also for Pacific Grove, Carmel-by-the-Sea, and Sand City under mutual-aid / contract arrangements, and for federal/special-district installations including the Presidio of Monterey and Naval Postgraduate School.

## Local Amendments (Chapter 13-0.2)

The following local amendments to the 2025 CFC were codified via Ordinance 3712 (April 2026):

### §101.1 — Jurisdiction Title
Fire Code title amended to read "the Fire Code for the City of Monterey."

### §101.2.1 — Appendices
All appendices to the 2025 CFC are adopted in their entirety and shall apply.

### Open Burning Prohibition
Open burning is prohibited within city limits unless:
- The fire is confined to an approved container (as defined by the Uniform Mechanical Code) AND authorized by the Monterey Bay Air Pollution Control District; or
- Written authorization is obtained from the Fire Chief for special conditions.

### Aboveground Flammable Liquid Storage
- Storage of Class I and Class II liquids in aboveground tanks **outside of buildings is prohibited** within city limits except as permitted by the Zoning Ordinance.
- **Exception:** A maximum of 2,000 gallons of diesel fuel may be stored and dispensed from an aboveground tank for **marine fueling operations**, subject to Fire Chief approval.
- **Emergency generators:** Aboveground tanks of maximum 500-gallon capacity may be used only in conjunction with emergency generators, subject to Fire Chief approval.

### Automatic Fire Sprinkler Systems
- One- and two-family dwellings, Group R-3 and R-4 congregate living facilities, and townhouses: permitted to install NFPA 13D-compliant systems throughout.

### Private Bridges
- Engineering requirements imposed for private bridge access serving fire apparatus.

## High-Piled Combustible Storage
- The Fire Prevention Division publishes a **"High-Piled Combustible Storage Owners Statement of Intended Use Form"** (available in the Document Center on the fire department website). CFC Chapter 32 (High-Piled Combustible Storage) applies; local threshold is governed by the 2025 CFC default (> 12 ft storage height triggers Chapter 32 requirements for most commodities).

## Plan Review and Permit Process

- **Plans submittal:** Email all plans, calculations, cut sheets, and the Deferred Submittal Permit application to [email protected].
- **Turnaround:** 10–14 business days (in-house or third-party). Allow additional time during high-volume periods.
- **Payment:** Notified by Fire Prevention after plan approval; do not call to pay until notified.
- **Inspections:** Call (831) 646-3908 or email [email protected] to schedule fire sprinkler, fire alarm, fire suppression, or defensible space inspections. Inspector has a 2-hour arrival window (effective January 1, 2026).
