## POTW — Lake County Sanitation District (LACOSAN), Southeast Regional System

The City of Clearlake does not operate its own POTW. Wastewater from the City is collected and treated by:

> **Lake County Sanitation District (LACOSAN)**
> Southeast Regional Wastewater Treatment Plant (SERWTP)
> 2485 Old Highway 53, Clearlake, CA 95422
> Administrative Office: 255 N Forbes Street, Lakeport, CA 95453
> Website: https://www.lakecountyca.gov/985/Southeast-Regional-Wastewater-System

## Service Area

The Southeast Regional collection system extends from Pirates Cove to Lower Lake and includes the full City of Clearlake. As of 2010 data: ~6,113 active residential connections in Clearlake; system total of ~6,277 service connections, 8,167 SFD equivalents, population served ~13,312.

## Treatment and Effluent Disposal

- Treatment technology: Aerated Lagoons with Blue Frog System (secondary treatment).
- **100% of treated effluent is recycled** — conveyed by pipeline to The Geysers geothermal steam field in Sonoma/Lake County for injection. No surface discharge to Clear Lake or waterways.
- Design capacity: 1.9 mgd dry-weather average flow / 6.1 mgd wet-weather monthly average flow / 8.5 mgd hydraulic capacity.

## Regulatory Permit

  - Hydraulic capacity cap: 8.5 mgd.

## Sewer Use Ordinance

- **LACOSAN Sewer Use Ordinance** — administered by Lake County Sanitation District. Ordinance 2924 is a known amendment to Section 706B of the Sewer Use Ordinance (pertaining to inflationary rate adjustments using the Engineering News Record-Construction Cost Index, adopted April 13, 2010).
- The full Sewer Use Ordinance text is available in the Lake County Code (Appendix A per Municode library search result). [VERIFY at library.municode.com/ca/lake_county — search "Sewer Use Ordinance".]
- **City of Clearlake Zoning Code performance standard (CMC § 18-22.050):** Liquid waste shall not be discharged into a public sewer system unless it has been pretreated to the level required by the jurisdiction managing the wastewater treatment system. Direct discharge of liquid waste to rivers, creeks, ditches, or Clear Lake is prohibited.
