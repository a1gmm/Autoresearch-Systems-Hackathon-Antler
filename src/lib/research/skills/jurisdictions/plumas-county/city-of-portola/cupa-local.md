# CUPA (Certified Unified Program Agency) — City of Portola, CA

## Controlling Authority

The City of Portola is **not** its own CUPA. All Unified Program activities for facilities located within Portola are administered countywide by:

**Plumas County Public Health Agency — Environmental Health Division**

## Programs Administered

The Plumas County Environmental Health Division administers the following Unified Program elements for all facilities in Plumas County (including Portola):

| Program | Authority |
|---|---|
| Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP) | Cal H&SC § 25500 et seq. |
| Underground Storage Tanks (UST) | Cal H&SC § 25280 et seq. |
| Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA) | Cal H&SC § 25270 et seq. |
| Hazardous Waste Generator (HWG) | Cal H&SC § 25100 et seq. |
| California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP) | Cal H&SC § 25531 et seq. |

*Note: CERS (California Environmental Reporting System) is the electronic portal for all submittal types.*

## Electronic Reporting (CERS)

All facility submittals (HMBP, UST, APSA, HWG, CalARP) must be filed through the **California Environmental Reporting System (CERS)** at https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/. Annual HMBP certification is due on or before **March 1** each year (Cal H&SC § 25508(a)(1)). Facilities must update CERS within 30 days of any change (ownership, operator, new materials, site layout, contacts).

Additional CERS guidance: https://www.plumascounty.us/2424/Electronic-Reporting-Information-CERS

## Notes

- Portola is the only incorporated city in Plumas County; it has no independent CUPA designation.
- Plumas County is a rural CUPA — facility density is low, with notable concentrations of lumber-processing, bulk-petroleum, and light manufacturing in Portola, Quincy, and Chester/Lake Almanor.
- For hazardous materials emergency response, the county maintains a **Hazardous Materials Response Plan** (updated copy available through the county).
