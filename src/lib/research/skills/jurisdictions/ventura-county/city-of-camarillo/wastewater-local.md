> Industrial wastewater discharge in Camarillo is regulated by the **Camarillo Sanitary District (CSD)**, a subsidiary district of the City of Camarillo administered through the Public Works Department. CSD has its own pretreatment authority under **CSD Ordinance No. 96** (effective July 14, 2023). Do NOT route to Ventura County or any county program for IWP matters.

## Industrial Wastewater Discharge Permit (IWP)

**Who must obtain a permit (§ 13018):**
No person shall discharge industrial wastewater directly or indirectly into CSD's sewerage facilities without first obtaining a District Permit for Industrial Wastewater Discharge. This includes extra-jurisdictional dischargers (Camrosa Water District and Ventura Regional Sanitation District service areas that connect to CSD). The District Manager may also require permits for facilities that store or use materials capable of causing pass-through or interference, even without an active discharge.

**Application process (§ 13020):**
1. Obtain application form from the CSD office at 601 Carmen Drive or contact Source Control Inspector Kayla Corcoran — (805) 383-5663
2. Complete form including all pollutant analyses per 40 CFR Part 136 methods
3. Submit to District; District Manager reviews for capacity adequacy and compliance with Supplement
4. Upon approval, Permit for Industrial Wastewater Discharge is issued
5. Permits are not transferable without prior written consent of the District Manager

**Permit conditions include (§ 13018):**
- User charges and fee schedule
- Average and maximum wastewater constituent limits
- Flow rate limits or flow equalization requirements
- Inspection and sampling facility requirements
- Monitoring and reporting requirements
- Daily average and daily maximum discharge rates for regulated pollutants
- Compliance schedules

## Discharge Limits (Chapter XIV, § 14001)

**pH:** 5.5–10.5 (discharges outside this range prohibited)

**Suspended Solids (TSS):**
- > 15,000 gpd monthly average daily discharge: 350 mg/L maximum
- ≤ 15,000 gpd monthly average daily discharge: 750 mg/L maximum

**Dissolved Solids (TDS):**
- > 15,000 gpd: 1,000 mg/L maximum
- ≤ 15,000 gpd: 2,000 mg/L maximum

**BOD:**
- > 15,000 gpd: 300 mg/L maximum
- ≤ 15,000 gpd: 1,200 mg/L maximum

**Oil and Grease:**
- Non-biodegradable petroleum oil: 50 mg/L maximum (§ 14001.10)
- Dispersed biodegradable oils and fats (lard, tallow, vegetable oil): 175 mg/L maximum (§ 14001.11)
- Grease trap/interceptor required for restaurants and food service establishments

**Temperature:** No discharge shall cause treatment plant influent to exceed 40°C (104°F)

**Metals — daily maximum concentrations (§ 14001.22, total recoverable form):**

| Pollutant | Limit (mg/L) |
|---|---|
| Arsenic | 0.1 |
| Boron | 3.0 |
| Cadmium | 0.19 |
| Chromium (total) | 2.8 |
| Copper | 1.0 |
| Lead | 1.5 |
| Mercury | 0.070 |
| Molybdenum | 1.5 |

| Selenium | 0.67 |
| Silver | 0.75 |

**Categorical industrial users** must also comply with applicable EPA categorical pretreatment standards (40 CFR categorical standards) which may be more stringent than the local limits above.

**Other prohibited discharges (§ 14001):**
- Flammable materials (closed cup flashpoint < 140°F / 60°C)
- Radioactive wastes
- Substances causing pass-through or interference
- Solid matter > 1/2 inch in any dimension
- Rainwater, stormwater, groundwater, or uncontaminated water (§ 14001.7; § 14005)
- Water added for dilution purposes
- Non-biodegradable cutting oils
- Deionized, steam condensate, or distilled water (except laboratory quantities)
- Strongly odorous wastes
- Cooling tower blowdown exceeding one-third of make-up water (except during off-peak hours with available capacity)
- Trucked or hauled pollutants (except at designated discharge points)

## FOG Program

- CSD operates a Fats, Oils and Grease (FOG) Source Control Program — codified as Chapter XVI of the CSD Operations Code
- Food service establishments must install and maintain grease traps/interceptors per Uniform Plumbing Code Appendix H specifications
- Contact Source Control Inspector for FOG compliance requirements

## Fees and Charges (Chapter XV)

- **Wastewater treatment and monitoring charge (§ 15004):** Charged periodically to industrial dischargers whose wastewater requires treatment and/or monitoring/sampling. Charge covers treatment plant costs (proportional) and monitoring costs.
- **Analytical/monitoring fees (§ 15006):** Commercial laboratory rates for monitoring and analytical work
- **Treatability charge (§ 15007):** $25–$500 per unit for dischargers whose waste character imposes special treatment costs on the District
- [VERIFY] Obtain current fee schedule from CSD office — specific dollar amounts for IWP application and annual permit fees not published online

## Sampling and Monitoring (§ 14002, § 15001)

- Periodic measurements of flow rates, BOD, suspended solids, and other pollutants as determined by the District Manager
- All sampling and analyses must use a District-approved laboratory or be performed by the District
- If performed by the District, actual costs charged to the discharger
- Methods: 40 CFR Part 136; standard industry procedures if no 40 CFR method exists
- Instantaneous maximum vs. daily average: For surcharge calculations, concentration exceeded when 1.4× baseline for BOD/TSS/fats-oils-grease, or 1.2× baseline for all other pollutants (§ 13002 reference)

## Approval Authority

- The California Regional Water Quality Control Board, **Los Angeles Region (Region 4)** is the Approval Authority for CSD's pretreatment program (as defined in § 12001)
- NPDES permit for CSD: Order R4-2019-0136 — WDR and NPDES Permit for Camarillo Sanitary District Camarillo Water Reclamation Plant
