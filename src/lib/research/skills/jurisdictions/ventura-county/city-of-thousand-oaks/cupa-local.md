> City of Thousand Oaks is NOT its own CUPA. All six CalEPA unified programs are administered by the **Ventura County RMA Environmental Health Division — CUPA**. This is confirmed by the Ventura County Ordinance Code, Division 4, Chapter 5 (Hazardous Substances), which defines CUPA jurisdiction as "all of the unincorporated and incorporated areas of Ventura County, except for the incorporated area of the City of Oxnard." (Sec. 4501(e)). Participating agencies in the county system are the City of Ventura Fire Department and City of Santa Paula Fire Department — Thousand Oaks is not a participating agency; it falls under the county CUPA directly.

## HMBP Program Details

- **Submission portal:** California Environmental Reporting System (CERS) — https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/
- **Who must comply:** Any business storing, using, or handling hazardous materials at or above threshold quantities (generally: ≥55 gallons liquid, ≥500 lbs solid, ≥200 cu ft gas — per H&S Code §25507)
- **Initial filing:** Within 30 days of bringing threshold materials onsite
- **Annual update:** Required every year; update also triggered within 30 days if:
  - Quantities increase by 100% or more
  - New previously-undisclosed materials are added
  - Ownership, name, or address changes
  - Significant operational changes affecting emergency response
- **Site map instructions:** https://rma.venturacounty.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/hazardous-materials-business-plan-site-map-instructions.pdf

## What Thousand Oaks Facilities Should Do

1. File HMBP and all required unified program documents via CERS at https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/
2. Direct all CUPA program questions (HMBP, UST, CalARP, APSA, HWG) to Ventura County RMA Environmental Health — NOT to the City of Thousand Oaks or VCFD.
3. Fire Code operational permits for hazmat operations (CFC Chapter 50+) are separately obtained from VCFD Fire Prevention Bureau (see fire-code-local.md).
