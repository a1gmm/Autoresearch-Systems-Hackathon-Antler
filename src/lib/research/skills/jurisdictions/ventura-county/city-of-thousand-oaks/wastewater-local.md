> City of Thousand Oaks owns and operates its own POTW (Hill Canyon Treatment Plant). It is the direct pretreatment authority — NOT Ventura County. Route all industrial wastewater discharge inquiries to the City's Public Works, Water and Wastewater Division.

## Permit Tiers / Industrial Pretreatment Program

The city issues tiered industrial wastewater pretreatment (IWP) permits based on discharge risk level. Confirmed tiers include:

| Tier | Trigger |
|---|---|
| **Significant Industrial User (SIU)** | Non-categorical dischargers meeting federal SIU thresholds (>25,000 gpd, or pass-through/interference potential) |
| **Categorical Industrial User (CIU)** | Facilities subject to EPA categorical pretreatment standards (40 CFR — e.g., metal finishing, electroplating, food processing) |
| **Standard / Risk-Based IWP** | Other industrial dischargers below SIU/CIU thresholds but with elevated discharge characteristics |

[VERIFY] Confirm current tier names and any lower-volume/lower-risk categories with the Water and Wastewater Division — specific tier structure and application forms are not available on the public city website.

## Application Process

1. Contact Water and Wastewater Division at (805) 449-2100 to determine applicable permit tier and required documentation.
2. Request the IWP permit application form (not available for download from the public web pages reviewed — contact city directly).
3. Submit completed application with supporting documents (SIC code, process flow description, discharge monitoring data).
4. City reviews application, may conduct a site inspection.
5. Permit issued; compliance monitoring and reporting required throughout permit term.

## Discharge Prohibitions (Confirmed Categories)

Under TOMC Title 7, Chapter 1, the following are generally prohibited (standard pretreatment prohibitions per 40 CFR 403.5):

- Substances that cause fire or explosion hazard at the POTW
- Substances causing corrosive structural damage (pH typically must be within 6.0–12.0 s.u. — [VERIFY exact Thousand Oaks pH range])
- Solid or viscous pollutants that obstruct flow
- Pollutants at concentrations or flow rates that inhibit biological treatment (pass-through or interference)
- Heat sufficient to inhibit biological activity at the POTW (typically <40°C / 104°F at POTW — [VERIFY local limit])
- Petroleum oil, nonbiodegradable cutting oil, or products containing mineral oil in amounts causing interference or pass-through
- Trucked or hauled pollutants without prior written approval
- Radioactive wastes (except per applicable law)
- Stormwater and unpolluted groundwater to the sanitary sewer

## Reporting and Compliance

- Baseline Monitoring Reports required at permit issuance for CIUs
- Periodic Compliance Reports required for CIUs per 40 CFR 403.12 (typically semi-annual)
- Self-monitoring reports required; frequency varies by permit tier
- Slug discharge notification: immediate verbal notification to city; written follow-up per ordinance requirements
- NPDES/pretreatment reporting requirements are contained in Attachment I of Order R4-2019-0137

## Connection to Regional Framework

- HCTP is classified as a **major NPDES discharger** under Order R4-2019-0137 (RWQCB Region 4)
- Federal pretreatment program requirements flow from the NPDES permit (40 CFR Part 403)
- Industrial users must not cause pass-through or interference at HCTP
- Stormwater (separate from sewer pretreatment) is managed under TOMC Title 7, Chapter 8 — contact Public Works Engineering at (805) 449-2100
