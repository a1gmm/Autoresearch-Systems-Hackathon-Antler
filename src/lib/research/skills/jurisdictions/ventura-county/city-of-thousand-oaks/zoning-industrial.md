> Local zoning reference for industrial land use in City of Thousand Oaks. Zoning authority is held by the City (not the county). Thousand Oaks is a primarily residential/commercial suburb with limited industrial land; the two industrial zone districts are M-1 and M-2. A comprehensive Title 9 zoning code update was underway as of 2025 to implement the Thousand Oaks 2045 General Plan — confirm current article/section numbers if the update has been adopted.

## Industrial Zone Districts

### M-1 — Industrial Park Zone (Article 16 of Title 9, Chapter 4)

- **Character:** Light industrial, research and development, warehousing, office/flex — generally cleaner, lower-impact industrial uses.
- **Code reference:** Article 16, TOMC Title 9, Chapter 4 — https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/thousandoaks/latest/thousandoaks_ca/0-0-0-15116
- **Approval requirement:**
  - If use is located **>400 feet from any existing residentially zoned property:** Development Permit (DP) required — administrative review.
  - If use is located **≤400 feet from any existing residentially zoned property:** Special Use Permit (SUP) with Planning Commission review required.
  - Notation in use matrix: "DP/SUP1" (per Sec. 9-4.2105)
- **2045 General Plan alignment:** The General Plan designates Industrial areas allowing manufacturing, distribution, light industrial/flex, R&D, offices, breweries, distilleries, and supportive retail/services.

### M-2 — Light Manufacturing Zone (Article 17 of Title 9, Chapter 4)

- **Character:** Broader manufacturing uses than M-1; heavier industrial activities permitted with appropriate conditions.
- **Code reference:** Article 17, TOMC Title 9, Chapter 4
- **Approval requirement:** Same 400-foot proximity rule applies — DP if >400 ft from residential; SUP with Planning Commission review if ≤400 ft.
  - Administrative special use permit (SUPA) may apply for lower-impact uses in M-2 if >400 ft from residential; SUP1 (Planning Commission) if ≤400 ft. (Per Sec. 9-4.2105 notation "SUPA/SUP1".)

## Conditional / Special Use Permit (SUP) Process

Thousand Oaks uses a "Special Use Permit" (SUP) framework (functionally equivalent to a CUP in many California jurisdictions):

**Approval findings required (Planning Commission):**
1. Project is consistent with the Thousand Oaks General Plan.
2. Project complies with applicable laws and regulations.
3. Project will not be detrimental to public health, safety, or general welfare.
4. Project has been reviewed in conformance with CEQA.
5. Proposed use will be compatible with land uses in the vicinity.

**SUP review body:** Planning Commission (for uses ≤400 ft from residential or as required by Sec. 9-4.2105).

**Administrative Special Use Permit (SUPA):** For certain uses where proximity to residential is not a factor — staff-level review, no Planning Commission hearing required.

**Development Permit (DP):** For uses >400 ft from residential in M-1/M-2 that do not require SUP — administrative review.

**Applications:** Via TO/24 Virtual Land Use Service portal — https://toaks.gov/licensesandpermits

**SUP/CUP action:** TOMC Sec. 9-4.2803 — Review and action on applications — https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/thousandoaks/latest/thousandoaks_ca/0-0-0-16980

## Notes on Industrial Land in Thousand Oaks

- Thousand Oaks is predominantly residential and commercial; M-1 and M-2 zoned areas are limited and concentrated primarily in the Conejo Business Park and adjacent areas.
- Higher-impact industrial or hazardous operations in M-1/M-2 will likely also trigger CUPA/HMBP review (Ventura County RMA) and VCFD fire code operational permits — see cupa-local.md and fire-code-local.md.
- Environmental review under CEQA is required for discretionary approvals (SUP/SUPA) for industrial projects — typically an Initial Study / Mitigated Negative Declaration or EIR.
