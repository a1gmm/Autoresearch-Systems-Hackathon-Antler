> Local fire code reference for City of Thousand Oaks. Thousand Oaks is NOT its own fire AHJ — fire code adoption and enforcement are administered county-wide by the **Ventura County Fire Department (VCFD)**. The City Council adopts a parallel local fire ordinance that mirrors the VCFD code; both are currently aligned on the 2025 CFC cycle.

## Key Local Amendments / Thousand Oaks-Specific Provisions (Ord. 34 / Dec. 2025 adoption)

1. **Wildland-Urban Interface — Zone Zero (5-ft perimeter rule):** Properties in High or Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zones must maintain a non-combustible 5-foot perimeter (Zone Zero) around all structures. Prohibited materials within Zone Zero include vegetation, mulch, wood chips, vines, synthetic turf, planting boxes, firewood, and combustible landscaping.
2. **New construction in HFHSZ:** New trees may not be planted within Zone Zero.
3. **Automatic fire sprinkler systems:** Provisions adopted as part of the 2025 CFC update.
4. **Hazardous material permits:** Updated permit thresholds aligned with 2025 CFC Chapter 50+.
5. **Open burning:** Recreational and agricultural burning regulations aligned with VCFD Chapter 49 (wildland/nuisance vegetation).

## Fire Code Permit Types (VCFD)

Operational permits required by the Ventura County Fire Code include (not exhaustive):

- Fire Code Permits (general — annual)
- High-Pile Combustible Storage (CFC Chapter 32)
- Hazardous Materials — storage, use, or handling above CFC threshold quantities (CFC Chapter 50)
- Compressed gases (CFC Chapter 53)
- Flammable/combustible liquids (CFC Chapter 57)
- Special Events
- Recreational Fire

Apply via Ventura County One-Stop Permitting: https://onestoppermits.venturacounty.gov/departments/fire-department

## Relationship to County

The City of Thousand Oaks does not operate its own independent fire prevention bureau for code enforcement purposes. The VCFD serves as the AHJ for fire code compliance, plan checks, inspections, and operational permits throughout the city. The city's municipal code chapter (Title 5, Chapter 6) mirrors the county ordinance by local adoption.
