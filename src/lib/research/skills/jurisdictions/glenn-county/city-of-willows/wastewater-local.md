## Prohibited Discharges (WMC 13.10.160)

No person shall discharge any of the following into the city sewer system:

1. **Stormwater and uncontaminated water**: rainwater, groundwater, street drainage, subsurface drainage, yard fountain/lawn spray water (sanitary sewers only).
2. **Explosive or flammable substances**: gasoline, benzene, naphtha, fuel oil, or any flammable/explosive solid, liquid, or solvent with **flashpoint < 190°F**.
3. **Corrosive materials**: waste with **pH < 5.5** or other corrosive property capable of damaging structures, equipment, or personnel.
4. **Obstructions and interferences**: solids or viscous substances capable of blocking flow or interfering with operations — ashes, cinders, sand, mud, straw, shavings, sawdust, oil, grease, metal, glass, rags, hair, hides, wool, feathers, tar, plastics, wood, garbage containers, etc.
5. **Garbage**: except properly shredded garbage from dwellings or restaurants (< 5% dry weight > 3/8-inch screen).
6. **Petroleum products**: mineral oils, petroleum greases, cutting oils (soluble oil) forming persistent emulsions; non-biodegradable petroleum products.
7. **Suspended solids**: industrial process water with SS of character/quantity requiring unusual treatment expense; discharge SS must pass a **1/4-inch mesh** screen.
8. **Cooling water / pool drains**: uncontaminated cooling water, evaporative cooler water, or swimming pool drains without special written permission from the Director of Public Works.
9. **Toxic substances**: waters containing toxic or poisonous substances in quantities sufficient to injure or interfere with the sewage treatment process, effluent quality, or receiving water.
10. **Chemical pesticides**: chlorinated hydrocarbons, organo-phosphates, or similar compounds used as algaecides, bactericides, fungicides, insecticides, or pesticides — except as specifically approved by written permit.
11. **Oxidizing and reducing agents**: strong oxidizers and reducers.
12. **Nondegradable wastes**: substances not amenable to treatment or causing effluent failure to meet state/federal discharge requirements.
13. **Unusual waste concentrations**: unusual concentrations of inert SS; excessive discoloration; unusual BOD/COD/chlorine demand; slug discharges exceeding **5× average 24-hour concentration or flow for > 15 minutes**.
14. **Noxious/malodorous substances**: substances that by themselves or in interaction may create a public nuisance or make sewer entry extraordinarily hazardous.
15. **Extreme temperatures**: wastewater > **150°F** or < **33°F**.
16. **Effluent limitation threats**: any wastewater causing a violation of treatment plant effluent limitations or precluding cost-effective sludge disposal alternatives.

## Industrial Wastewater Controls

### Grease Traps (WMC 13.10.050)
Required when Public Works Director determines waste pretreatment is necessary. Mandatory for: restaurants, cafes, lunch counters, cafeterias, bars, clubs, hotels, hospitals, rest homes, sanitariums, factories/school kitchens, or any establishment where grease may enter drainage in quantities affecting sewer flow or lift pump action. Must comply with Uniform Plumbing Code and be approved by building official. Annual inspection fees may be set by council resolution.

### Screening (WMC 13.10.040)
Director of Public Works may require screening of commercial or industrial flows; screen mesh equivalent to 20 meshes per linear inch in both directions (unless otherwise specified by council resolution). Discharge of any "deleterious substances" harmful to the sewage system is unlawful.

### Sampling Manholes (WMC 13.10.170)
Industrial dischargers required by Director may install monitoring manholes and metering/monitoring equipment at their own expense within 90 days of notice. Sampling facilities must be accessible at all times and maintained in good condition by the discharger.

### Waste Discharge Report (WMC 13.10.170)
Director may require a written waste discharge report within 30 days of notice, including: nature of the process, volume, flow rates, substances and concentrations. Director may extend by an additional 30 days on written request. Failure to file: authority to disconnect service.

### Interceptors (WMC 13.10.170)
Grease, oil, and sand interceptors required when Director determines necessary for liquid wastes with excessive grease, flammable wastes, sand, or other harmful ingredients (not required for residential). Type and capacity approved by Director.

### Special Agreements (WMC 13.10.170)
Unusual-strength or unusual-character industrial waste may be accepted under a special written agreement with the city, subject to payment and terms set by the city.

### Right of Entry (WMC 13.10.170)
Director of Public Works and authorized city employees may enter all properties served by the city for inspection, observation, measurement, sampling, and testing.

- The city must comply with the **Federal Clean Water Act and the 1987 Amendments** (stated in city Public Works page).
- **Pretreatment program threshold**: Federal pretreatment regulations (40 CFR Part 403) require a formally approved local pretreatment program only for POTWs with design flow > 5 mgd that receive industrial process wastewater. Willows WWTP is small; [VERIFY] whether it exceeds the 5 mgd threshold.
- If below the threshold: categorical industrial users (CIUs) are still subject to federal categorical pretreatment standards (40 CFR Parts 405–471) and must self-report to the RWQCB.
