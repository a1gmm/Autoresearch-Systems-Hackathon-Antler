## POTW / Sanitation Authority

Wastewater collection and treatment in the City of Mammoth Lakes is operated by an independent special district — not by the Town government:

**Mammoth Community Water District (MCWD)**

- Website: https://www.mcwd.dst.ca.us/
- Address: 1315 Meridian Blvd, Mammoth Lakes, CA 93546
- Phone: (760) 934-2596 (M–F 7:30 am – 4:30 pm)
- After-hours emergency: (760) 914-0935
- Billing: billing@mcwd.dst.ca.us

MCWD provides water, wastewater, and recycled water services. Infrastructure: ~75 miles of sewer lines; one wastewater treatment plant (constructed 1965, upgraded over time; design capacity ~5.48 MGD); treatment to advanced secondary level (biological treatment, filtration, chlorine disinfection); treated effluent discharged to Laurel Pond (~5 miles from plant). Recycled water system produces Title 22 quality water.

## Sewer Use Ordinance / Code

The controlling sewer-use instrument is **MCWD Sewer Code — Chapter 11** ("Sanitary Sewer Code"), adopted and maintained by the MCWD Board of Directors, updated through 2025.

- Direct PDF: https://www.mcwd.dst.ca.us/files/53cc9a926/MCWD+Code+-+Chapter+11+Sewer+(with+TOC+and+Attachments).pdf
- Code index page: https://www.mcwd.dst.ca.us/district-code-policies-regulations

Chapter 11 covers: discharge permits, prohibited waste, interceptors, fees, design standards, inspection, maintenance, out-of-district service, and the FOG Control Program.

## Industrial / Non-Domestic Discharge

Chapter 11 does not establish an EPA-approved **Approved Pretreatment Program (APP)** under 40 CFR Part 403. Mammoth Lakes is a small resort community with no Significant Industrial Users (SIUs) in the categorical sense; no formal IWP process was found online.

Industrial discharge is governed by case-by-case requirements within Chapter 11:

- **Division X, § 10.01**: Any person discharging waste to the public sewer must obtain a **Waste Disposal Permit** from MCWD prior to discharge.
- **§ 10.07 (Limitations on Use)**: No solid waste, garbage, oils, tars, chemicals, or other matter that could clog, obstruct, increase maintenance costs, or interfere with treatment may be discharged.
- **§ 10.10 (Temperature)**: Effluent temperature shall not exceed **140°F** (60°C).
- **§ 10.11 (pH)**: Acids or alkalis must have pH controlled to levels the District finds adequate before discharge.
- **§ 10.12 (Toxic Substances)**: All toxic chemicals must be retained or rendered acceptable before discharge.
- **§ 10.15 (Pretreatment)**: MCWD may require installation of pretreatment plants or interceptors whenever necessary to protect the collection system, treatment plant, or public health.
- **§ 10.06** (implied by surrounding context): Industrial users seeking a permit may be required to submit a pretreatment plan for MCWD review and approval before a permit is issued.

Industrial users also pay a **monthly waste quality surcharge** based on flow metering (§ fee provisions in Division VI / Appendix A, FY26 Master Fee Schedule). MCWD may require installation of District-approved recording and sampling devices at the industrial user's expense.

## FOG Control Program (Division XVI)

Food Service Establishments (FSEs) are subject to the MCWD FOG Control Program:

- All FSEs must obtain a **FOG Discharge Permit** before operations.
- FSEs must install and maintain a **Grease Control Device** (grease interceptor or grease trap) sized per the California Plumbing Code.
- Each restaurant must have a sand and grease interceptor installed at owner's expense.
- Notification to MCWD required before substantial changes to operations, menu, or property.
- FOG program contact: (760) 934-2596 ext. 232.

## Industrial Pretreatment Program Status

No formal EPA/RWQCB **Approved Pretreatment Program** found for MCWD. Given the facility's small size and predominantly resort/residential user base, a formal categorical pretreatment program is likely not required.
