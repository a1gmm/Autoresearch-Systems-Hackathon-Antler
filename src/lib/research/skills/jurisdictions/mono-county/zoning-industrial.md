## Zoning Authority

**Mono County Community Development Department — Planning Division** administers zoning for all **unincorporated** areas of Mono County under **Title 19 (Zoning)** of the Mono County Code (also published as the Land Development Regulations).

- The **Town of Mammoth Lakes** has its own zoning code administered by the Town's Planning Department — see city-of-mammoth-lakes file.
- All other communities (Bridgeport, Lee Vining, June Lake, Walker/Coleville, Benton, Crowley Lake, etc.) fall under county zoning.

## Industrial Zoning Districts

Mono County General Plan / Land Development Regulations establish two industrial land use designations in unincorporated areas:

### Industrial Park (IP)

**Purpose:** Accommodate light- and moderate-intensity industrial uses that do not create environmental nuisances or hazards.

**Permitted uses (by right):** Changes of use in existing conforming structures; adult-oriented businesses complying with Chapter 19 locational requirements.

**Director Review uses (administrative, no public hearing):** Agricultural operations, offices, laboratories, commercial laundries, wholesale/warehousing, vehicle repair, light manufacturing (clothing, jewelry, electronics assembly), printing/publishing, equipment rental, lumber yards, recreational vehicle storage, waste transfer facilities, recycling operations.

**Use Permit required (Planning Commission hearing):**
- Industrial condominiums
- Waste transfer and management facilities
- Tank farms
- Freight terminals

**Development Standards:**

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Minimum lot area | 10,000 sq ft |
| Minimum district area | 10 acres (5 acres if abutting compatible uses) |
| Minimum lot width | 75 ft |
| Minimum lot depth | 100 ft |
| Maximum lot coverage | 80% |
| Maximum building height | 40 ft |
| Front setback (Director Review) | 20 ft |
| Rear setback (Director Review) | 5 ft |
| Side setback (Director Review) | 0 ft |
| Front setback (Use Permit) | 20 ft |
| Rear setback (Use Permit) | 10 ft |
| Side setback (Use Permit) | 10 ft |
| Building spacing | 10 ft minimum |

Source: https://www.monocounty.ca.gov/generalplan/industrial-park-ip

### Industrial (I)

**Purpose:** Accommodate heavy industrial uses that may cause moderate to higher degrees of environmental nuisances or hazards. Must be located in areas relatively remote from residential and commercial development.

**Permitted uses (by right):** All IP uses; single caretaker unit per district; heavy-vehicle storage and maintenance; adult-oriented businesses complying with Chapter 19.

**Director Review uses:** All IP Director Review uses.

**Use Permit required (Planning Commission hearing):**
- General manufacturing: batch plants, concrete/asphalt plants, textile mills, lumber mills
- Alternative energy generation facilities
- Petroleum refining and metal smelting
- Waste processing and hazardous waste management
- Junkyards and auto salvage yards
- Mining and commercial excavation
- Food processing operations
- Heavy equipment storage, firewood processing, impound yards

**Development Standards:**

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Minimum lot area | 10,000 sq ft |
| Minimum district area | 30 acres (exceptions for resource-dependent uses, e.g., gravel pit) |
| Minimum lot width | 75 ft |
| Minimum lot depth | 100 ft |
| Maximum lot coverage | 80% |
| Maximum building height | 40 ft |

**Additional requirement:** Proof of conformance with Section 04.250 (Nuisances and Hazards) required before siting any proposed Industrial district. Septic system lots subject to Lahontan RWQCB minimums.

Source: https://www.monocounty.ca.gov/generalplan/industrial-i

## Conditional Use Permit / Use Permit Process

Mono County uses a **Use Permit** (not "CUP") as the primary discretionary approval for industrial uses requiring Planning Commission review. The general permit process has five phases:

| Phase | Description |
|---|---|
| Pre-application conference | Optional but recommended; staff reviews preliminary issues |
| Phase 1 — Application submission | Completeness review within 30 days |
| Phase 2 — CEQA review | Environmental determination (categorical exemption, negative declaration, or EIR) |
| Phase 3 — Staff analysis | Planning Commission report prepared |
| Phase 4 — Permit decision | Public hearing; Planning Commission action |
| Phase 5 — Project completion | Final documentation and fee processing |

**Fees:** Per Community Development Fee Schedule (effective 07-01-23). Contact Planning Division for current fee matrix.

**Applications:** Submit to Planning Division at either the Mammoth Lakes or Bridgeport office; email commdev@mono.ca.gov.

Source: https://monocounty.ca.gov/planning/page/planning-permit-process · https://monocounty.ca.gov/planning

## Notes on Industrial Development in Mono County

- **Very limited industrial land in practice.** Mono County is ~99% federal land (National Forest, BLM, Inyo National Forest, Yosemite). Industrial designations are rare; most existing industrial activity relates to mining, aggregate, utility operations, or resort support.
- **Nuisance/hazard review mandatory:** Section 04.250 conformance required for all I-district siting — proof of no significant nuisance to adjacent uses required.
- **CEQA is common for industrial uses:** Most Use Permit-level industrial uses require at minimum an Initial Study/Negative Declaration.
- **Air quality:** Any new stationary source must obtain Authority to Construct (ATC) and Permit to Operate (PTO) from the **Great Basin Unified Air Pollution Control District (GBUAPCD)** before equipment installation. GBUAPCD covers Mono County (and Inyo and eastern Alpine counties). Contact: 157 Short Street, Bishop, CA 93514 · (760) 872-8211 · https://www.gbuapcd.org/
