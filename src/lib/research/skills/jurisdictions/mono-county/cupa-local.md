## CUPA Agency

**Mono County Environmental Health Division — Hazardous Materials Business Unit (CUPA)**

Mono County is the **sole CUPA** for the entire county. There is no separate city-level CUPA — even within the incorporated **Town of Mammoth Lakes**, the county Environmental Health Division administers all Unified Program elements.

## Programs Administered

The Mono County CUPA consolidates all applicable Unified Program elements into a single consolidated inspection:

| Program | State Authority |
|---|---|
| Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP) | CalEPA / HSC Ch. 6.95 |
| Hazardous Waste Generator (HWG) | DTSC / HSC Ch. 6.5 |
| Underground Storage Tanks (UST) | State Water Board / H&SC Ch. 6.7 |
| Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA) | CUPA / H&SC Ch. 6.67 |
| California Accidental Release Program (CalARP) | CalEPA / H&SC Ch. 6.95 Art. 2 |

Note: Hazardous Waste Tiered Treatment (on-site treatment) is also within scope; confirm with CUPA whether this element is currently active for facilities in Mono County.

## CERS Reporting

All regulated businesses must report electronically via the **California Environmental Reporting System (CERS)** at https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/. Paper submittals are no longer accepted (mandatory electronic filing as of January 1, 2012).

**HMBP submission deadlines (HSC § 25503.5):**
- Within 60 days of commencing operations with regulated quantities of hazardous materials.
- Within 30 days of any major change in operations.

Source: https://monohealth.com/environmental-health/page/electronic-reporting-and-hazardous-materials-business-plan-requirements

## CalARP Program

The CalARP program applies to facilities that handle regulated substances above threshold quantities. Facilities must submit a Risk Management Plan (RMP) to the CUPA. Three program tiers apply:

- **Program 1:** No offsite impact potential; no release history — minimal requirements.
- **Program 2:** Intermediate requirements including hazard assessment and accidental release prevention steps.
- **Program 3:** Facilities subject to OSHA PSM (29 CFR 1910.119) — full compliance program required.

RMPs received by Mono County CUPA are available for public review. A list of received RMPs is published at:
https://monohealth.com/environmental-health/page/risk-management-plan-received

## Key Notes for Rural/Small Facilities

Mono County is sparsely populated (approximately 14,000 residents county-wide) and predominantly rural. Many facilities are seasonal (ski resort operations, tourism-related). The CUPA may require HMBP filings for seasonal fuel storage, propane systems at resorts, and vehicle maintenance operations at remote sites. Confirm threshold applicability with the Bridgeport or Mammoth Lakes office based on facility location.
