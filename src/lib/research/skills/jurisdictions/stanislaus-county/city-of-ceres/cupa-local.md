## Controlling CUPA

City of Ceres is **not its own CUPA**. All Unified Program elements within Ceres city limits are administered by the **Stanislaus County Department of Environmental Resources (DER), Hazardous Materials Division** — the sole countywide CUPA for Stanislaus County.

## Unified Program Elements Administered

The County DER CUPA administers all six Unified Program elements for facilities in Ceres:

1. Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP) — CalEPA CERS submissions
2. California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP) program
3. Underground Storage Tank (UST) permits and inspections
4. Aboveground Storage Tank (AST) program
5. Hazardous Waste Generator program (tiered permitting)
6. Facility Response Plans (where applicable)

The County also administers:
- Medical Waste Management Program
- E-Waste Recycling
- Household Hazardous Waste (HHW)
- Methamphetamine-Contaminated Property cleanup

## Local Hazmat Ordinance

No Ceres-specific local hazmat ordinance was found online. Hazmat program authority flows from the Stanislaus County Code (available at ecode360.com/ST5017) and state law. Businesses with hazardous materials above threshold quantities must register through CalEPA's CERS portal; the County DER is the local implementing agency.

## Note on Fire/Hazmat Interface

Under the 2021 Modesto-Ceres Fire JPA, Modesto Fire provides hazardous materials mitigation (emergency response) within Ceres. However, regulatory CUPA authority (permitting, inspection, enforcement) remains entirely with the County DER — the JPA is an operational fire-services arrangement only.
