## Industrial District Classifications

Title 21 defines five industrial zoning districts for unincorporated Stanislaus County:

| District | Chapter | Description |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| M — Industrial District | 21.60 | Heavy/general industrial; broadest use set |
| IBP — Industrial Business Park | 21.61 | Mixed industrial/business park |
| LI — Light Industrial | 21.62 | Light manufacturing, warehousing, assembly; no objectionable impacts |
| LM — Limited Industrial District | 21.64 | Light manufacturing from previously prepared materials; food processing |
| PI — Planned Industrial District | 21.42 | Planned development overlay for industrial uses |

## Chapter 21.60 — Industrial District (M)

The M district is the heaviest industrial classification. **Permitted by right:**
- All retail, wholesale, warehouses, service establishments, public/quasi-public buildings
- Junkyards, wrecking yards, auto dismantling yards
- All C-district (commercial) uses except dwelling units
- **All industrial uses** except those listed in § 21.60.030
- Crop farming; ballrooms, clubs, dance halls, stadiums (if not within 200 ft of R district)
- Single-family dwelling or one apartment accessory to a permitted industrial/commercial use

**Requires a Use Permit (§ 21.60.030):**
- Distillation of bones; disposal/dumping/sanitary landfill; incineration of dead animals, garbage, offal, refuse, or sewage; fat rendering
- Manufacturing of acid, cement, compressed gases, fertilizer, fungicides, glue, gypsum, hides, insecticides, lime, paper pulp, pesticides, plaster of paris, or poison gas
- Manufacture or storage of explosives or fireworks
- Feed lots, stockyards, slaughter of animals or poultry
- Refining of petroleum products; smelting of copper, iron, tin, zinc, or other metals
- Drilling for or removal of gas, oil, or commercial mining
- Racetracks, rifle ranges, motorcycle tracks
- Retail/wholesale stores with gross building/sales area ≥ 65,000 sf (Ord. CS 896)
- Emergency shelters (Ord. CS 1169)
- Commercial cannabis (retail, manufacturing, testing, distribution, cultivation) — subject to § 21.08.020(D) (Ord. CS 1205)

**Fire code cross-reference:** New bulk plants for flammable/combustible liquid storage are restricted to M-zoned (Heavy Industrial) areas per County Code § 16.55.040.

**Height limit:** Buildings/appurtenant structures — 75 ft. Fireproof structures not used for human occupancy — no limit. (§ 21.60.040)

## Chapter 21.62 — Light Industrial (LI)

Purpose: areas appropriate for light industrial/manufacturing, warehousing, offices, and assembly. Explicitly prohibits uses that create objectionable noise, smoke, odor, dust, noxious gases, glare, heat, vibration, or industrial wastes. Consistent with the Planned Industrial land use designation of the General Plan.

**Permitted by right (representative list):** Crop farming, assembly of products, bakery/warehouse/distribution, bottling plants, call centers, computer/communications R&D, furniture manufacturing, food packaging, printing/publishing, software development, warehouses, wholesale distribution, parcel delivery, public safety facilities, electronic repair/assembly, manufacturing and technology support.

**Expansion rule (§ 21.62.020.C):**
- Expansion < 25% of existing building floor area → **Director administrative approval**
- Expansion > 25% of existing floor area → **Conditional Use Permit** required

**Uses requiring Use Permit (§ 21.62.030):** See the full chapter for restricted uses.

**Prohibited uses (§ 21.62.060):** Junk/wrecking yards; hazardous material storage or manufacturing at intensities inconsistent with LI intent; uses generating the objectionable impacts listed in the purpose section.

## Chapter 21.64 — Limited Industrial District (LM)

**Permitted by right (representative list):**
- Crop farming and pasturing (no feedlots, hog/poultry farming)
- Assembly of business machines
- Bottling plants
- Compounding/packaging of cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, toiletries (no soap manufacture)
- Food processing and packaging (fruits, nuts, vegetables, blended foods, dairy, candies, non-alcoholic beverages, preserves, baked goods, frozen foods) — excludes dehydrating aromatic vegetables, olive processing, vinegar fermentation, slaughtering, fat rendering
- Manufacturing from previously prepared materials: jewelry, watches, clocks, precision instruments, appliances, musical instruments, glass products, electric/electronic equipment, motors, toys, TV/radio
- Leather goods, paper products, pens/pencils, professional/scientific instruments, photographic/optical equipment
- Containers from previously prepared materials (no enameling, lacquering, rubber coating, or electroplating)
- Printing, publishing, bookbinding

**Requires Use Permit (§ 21.64.030):**
- Industrial operations involving enameling, lacquering, rubber coating, or electroplating (vapors, dust, odors, smoke, fumes)
- Outside storage of materials, merchandise, wastes, or > 200 gal of petroleum products above ground (must be enclosed within a ≥ 6 ft solid ornamental or painted wood fence)
- Dray/freight operations (see full chapter for additional uses)

## Use Permit (CUP) Process — Chapter 21.96

**Authority:** Planning Commission issues Use Permits. Director may approve administrative permits for minor expansions per § 21.62.020.C.

**Application (§ 21.96.030):**
- Filed by property owner or authorized agent with Planning & Community Development Department
- Form prescribed by Planning Commission; must be signed by owner/agent
- Must include plot plan and pertinent data as required by Planning Director
- Filing fee: set by Board of Supervisors resolution (contact Planning for current amount)

**Hearing (§ 21.96.040):**
- Public hearing required for all use permits
- Notice: mailed postcards to property owners within 300 ft at least 10 days before hearing, OR publication in newspaper of general circulation + posting on property at least 10 days before hearing

**Findings required for approval (§ 21.96.050):**
The Planning Commission must find that:
1. The proposed use is consistent with the General Plan; and
2. The use will not be detrimental to the health, safety, and general welfare of persons residing or working in the neighborhood; and
3. The use will not be detrimental or injurious to property and improvements in the neighborhood or to the general welfare of the county.

Conditions of approval and bonding/undertaking requirements may be imposed as the Commission deems necessary.

**Appeals:** Chapter 21.116.
