## CUPA Agency

**Santa Cruz County Environmental Health Services (SCCEH)** — the single county-wide Certified Unified Program Agency (CUPA), certified by CalEPA in **1996**. Covers all four incorporated cities (Capitola, Santa Cruz, Scotts Valley, Watsonville) plus unincorporated areas.

The City of Capitola has **no municipal CUPA authority**. All six unified programs are administered by the County.

## Programs Administered

SCCEH administers the full six-program CUPA umbrella:

1. **Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP)** — chemical inventory, site maps, emergency response/training plans, via CERS.
2. **Hazardous Waste Generators** — inspection, compliance, and manifest requirements.
3. **Underground Storage Tanks (UST)** — installation, monitoring, and closure permits.
4. **Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA)** — for petroleum storage ≥ 1,320 gal aggregate or single tank ≥ 660 gal.
5. **California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP)** — RMP equivalent for regulated substances.
6. **Onsite Hazardous Waste Treatment (Tiered Permits)** — for on-site treatment operations.

Additionally: Medical Waste, Incident Response, and Hazardous Materials Advisory Commission functions.

## Local Ordinance / Municipal Code Chapter

**Santa Cruz County Code Chapter 7.100** — Hazardous Materials, Hazardous Waste, Underground Storage Tanks.

Key local threshold provision: **County Code § 7.100 requires HMBPs at quantities BELOW the state thresholds.** State law triggers a HMBP at ≥ 55 gallons liquid, ≥ 200 cubic feet compressed gas, or ≥ 500 pounds solid. The County's local ordinance imposes HMBP requirements on quantities smaller than these state minimums. Facilities with chemicals below state thresholds must contact SCCEH to determine if a county permit is required.

Ordinance text: https://www.codepublishing.com/CA/SantaCruzCounty/html/SantaCruzCounty07/SantaCruzCounty07100.html

## HMBP Submission

As of January 1, 2013, HMBPs must be submitted electronically via the **California Environmental Reporting System (CERS)**:
- Portal: https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/
- Select "Business Portal Sign In"
- Locally required supplemental forms (Santa Cruz County Additional Locally Collected Information; Small Quantity Chemical Inventory Form if applicable) must be uploaded within CERS.

## Fee Schedule

Fees are based on:
- Quantities of chemicals and number of hazard classes at the facility.
- Number of regulatory programs applicable (HMBP only vs. HMBP + UST + CalARP, etc.).

Fee schedule published as the **Unified Fee Schedule**:
- URL: http://unifiedfeeschedule.co.santa-cruz.ca.us/index.html

After SCCEH accepts the HMBP, an invoice for the hazardous materials permit is mailed to the facility.

## Area Plan

**Santa Cruz County Hazardous Materials Area Plan** (2017 Update) — governs emergency response coordination for hazmat incidents in Capitola and throughout the county.

## Gotchas

- Lower county thresholds mean businesses that are **exempt from state HMBP** may still need a county permit under § 7.100.
- Even if a CERS submittal shows < 200 cu ft / 55 gal / 500 lb, you must check "yes" on the CERS Business Activities screen and upload documentation if County Code 7.100 applies, or SCCEH cannot process the submission.
- All inspections, notices of violation, and enforcement actions are taken by County SCCEH staff — there is no city-level hazmat enforcement.
