## Zoning Code Authority

- **Municipal Code title/chapter:** SCCC **Chapter 13.10** (Zoning Regulations), Part III — Districts, **Article IV — Industrial Districts** (§§13.10.341–346)
- **Discretionary permit procedures:** SCCC **Chapter 18.10** (Discretionary Permit and Approval Procedures)
- **Coastal Zone overlay:** SCCC Chapter 13.20 (Coastal Zone permits) — uses other than principal permitted uses in the Coastal Zone are appealable to the Coastal Commission
- **Planning authority:** Community Development & Infrastructure (CDI), Unified Permit Center (UPC)
- **Code status:** The industrial district sections (13.10.341–346) were substantially revised in a November 2022 Board of Supervisors Public Hearing Draft; confirm current adopted version via UPC

## Industrial Zone Districts

Three M Industrial districts (SCCC §13.10.341):

### M-1 — Light Industrial District

**Purpose (§13.10.341(B)):** Areas for light industrial facilities — assembly and other manufacturing with no potential for major pollution, adverse visual impacts, or nuisance or hazard factors.

**Allowed uses (general description from Zone District Summary and §13.10.342):**
- Agricultural service establishments, poultry hatcheries
- Light manufacturing, assembly, or processing
- Wet laboratories, dry laboratories (new line item added 2022 draft)
- Handmade product fabrication and sale, including industrial art studios and cooperative maker spaces (new 2022 draft)
- Warehouses (approval level reduced to **AUP** — administrative use permit with public notice, no hearing — per 2022 draft)
- Commercial uses, infrastructure and utility uses (per updated chart)
- Commercial cannabis (non-retail, Class 3 manufacturing, Class 1 distribution) — subject to SCCC §13.10.650 and local license
- Live/work situations: **not currently allowed** (except manager's unit; under study per GP strategy BE-3.2b)

**Prohibited:** Uses from M-2 or M-3 requiring heavier industrial activity; uses emitting smoke prohibited (§13.10.343 performance standard for M-1).

**Approval levels:** Uses are permitted by right (Level IV or lower) or require discretionary use approval (higher levels). Specific level for each use shown in the Industrial Uses Chart in §13.10.342(D). M-2 uses may be allowed in M-1 as discretionary uses if approving body makes additional findings.

---

### M-2 — Heavy Industrial District

**Purpose (§13.10.341(C)):** Areas for heavy industrial facilities — lumber mills, major manufacturing or processing plants; ensures compatibility with environment and adjacent uses.

**Allowed uses (general description):**
- Large factories involving hazardous chemicals (Zone District Summary): manufacture of construction materials, household goods, glass, carpets, pharmaceuticals, petroleum processing, wood processing
- Transit stations for freight use only (clarified in 2022 draft; passenger transit not allowed)

**Performance standard:** In M-2 district, visible gray smoke of a shade equal to or darker than Ringelmann No. [number not fully captured in available documents — confirm at §13.10.343] is prohibited.

---

### M-3 — Mineral Extraction Industrial District

**Purpose (§13.10.341(D)):** Identifies and preserves rock, sand, gravel, and mineral resources; allows orderly economic extraction with mining permits.

**Allowed uses:** Quarries and mining, mineral extraction, reclamation. Mining permit (designated "M" in uses chart) required. Uses other than mineral extraction require special findings.

---

## Performance Standards (§13.10.3445 / §13.10.343)

- **Smoke prohibition (M-1):** No use permitted that emits smoke.
- **Hours of Operation:** No business open 10pm–6am except per CUP. Within 150 ft of residentially zoned property: no non-emergency outdoor activity (loading, sweeping, landscaping, maintenance) 10pm–6am except per CUP; no business open 8pm–10pm or 6am–8am except per MUP.
- **Outdoor storage:** Allowed only with a Minor Use Permit (MUP) and adequate screening from view of adjacent parcels.
- **Building height:** Max 40 feet (updated from prior 35 ft to accommodate taller first floors; per 2022 draft).
- **Setbacks:** Increased setbacks required when adjacent to sensitive uses (residential, non-commercial agriculture); fire-safe setbacks apply in State Responsibility Areas (SRAs).

## Discretionary Permit Process (CUP / Use Permits)

Discretionary approvals for industrial uses are processed under SCCC Chapter 18.10. Approval levels range from ministerial (Level I) through discretionary (Levels II–V) to mining permits.

### Zoning Administrator (CUP/Use Permit at ZA level)

1. **Application:** Filed with CDI, UPC, Discretionary Permits section. Review "Guidelines for Neighborhood Notification" and Lists of Required Information before submitting.
2. **Assigned planner site visit** and review against zoning/General Plan requirements.
3. **Agency coordination:** Planner receives comments from reviewing agencies (Fire, Public Works, Environmental Health, etc.).
4. **Staff report:** Includes project description, location, and approval/denial recommendation.
5. **Public hearing before Zoning Administrator** based on planner findings and public testimony.
6. **Required findings (SCCC §18.10.230(a)):** Project must not be detrimental to health, safety, or welfare; comply with county ordinances and zoning; align with General Plan and specific plans; meet CEQA; not overload utilities or create excessive traffic; harmonize with existing and proposed land uses.
7. **Notice requirements:** Project description sign posted on-site before application completion; postcards/letters mailed ≥10 calendar days before hearing to property owners within 500 ft and occupants within 100 ft; Coastal Commission notified for coastal zone projects.
8. **Decision effective** 10 business days after Zoning Administrator decision if not appealed.
9. **Appeal:** Appealable to Planning Commission per SCCC Chapter 18.10.

### AUP (Administrative Use Permit — e.g., Warehouses in M-1)

- Public notice but no hearing.
- Processed administratively by CDI Planning staff.

## Coastal Zone Note

In the Coastal Zone, **principal permitted uses** in industrial districts are:
- M-1: small light industrial facilities
- M-2: light industrial facilities in general
- M-3: mining, agriculture, and timber harvesting (including accessory uses)

Actions approving non-principal-permitted uses in the Coastal Zone are appealable to the California Coastal Commission per SCCC Chapter 13.20.
