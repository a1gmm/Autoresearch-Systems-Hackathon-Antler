## Agency and Designation

- **CUPA:** Santa Cruz County Environmental Health Division (SCCEH) — designated county-wide CUPA by CalEPA in 1996.
- **Own-CUPA or county?** County-wide CUPA. The City of Santa Cruz is NOT its own CUPA. All six Unified Program elements for facilities within city limits route to the county agency.
- **Geographic scope:** County-wide, covering the City of Santa Cruz and all other incorporated cities in Santa Cruz County.

## Local Ordinance

- **Santa Cruz County Code Chapter 7.100** — Hazardous Materials Storage, Use, and Disposal.
- Chapter 7.100 supplements state law (California Health & Safety Code §25500 et seq.) and sets local permit and reporting requirements.
- Note: The City of Santa Cruz does not have its own separate hazardous-materials municipal ordinance; county code Chapter 7.100 controls within city limits.

## Programs Administered (Unified Program Elements)

1. Hazardous Materials Management Plans (HMBP) / Business Plans
2. Hazardous Material Release Response Plans & Inventories
3. California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP)
4. Underground Storage Tanks (UST)
5. Aboveground Petroleum Storage Tanks (APSA)
6. Hazardous Waste Generators (including Tiered Permitting)
7. Onsite Hazardous Waste Treatment
8. Medical Waste (administered under same umbrella)

## Reporting Thresholds and Permit Trigger

**State thresholds (trigger HMBP and CUPA permit):**
- ≥ 55 gallons of a liquid hazardous material
- ≥ 200 cubic feet of a compressed gas
- ≥ 500 pounds of a solid hazardous material

**Local lower thresholds (Santa Cruz County Code §7.100):**
County Code §7.100 requires HMBPs for quantities **below** state thresholds. The specific local sub-threshold quantities are determined by a hazardous materials inspector on a case-by-case basis. Facilities that store chemicals below state thresholds should contact SCCEH to determine if local permit is required.

## Permit Fee Structure

- Permit fees are based on: (1) quantities of chemicals stored, (2) number of hazard classes present, and (3) number of regulatory programs that apply (UST, CalARP, APSA, etc.).
- More complex facilities with larger quantities pay higher fees than smaller facilities.
- **Fee schedule URL:** http://unifiedfeeschedule.co.santa-cruz.ca.us/index.html (the unified fee schedule portal — note: this URL uses http; may redirect).
- Invoiced after HMBP is received and accepted by SCCEH.

## Reporting Portal

- **CERS (California Environmental Reporting System):** https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/
- Mandatory electronic submission required since January 1, 2013 (California Health & Safety Code §25404(e)(2–5); AB 2286).
- Annual updates submitted via CERS; emergency responders access chemical inventories electronically.
- Walk-in computer assistance available at SCCEH lobby for businesses with limited computer access.

## Application Process

1. Determine chemicals/quantities and obtain Safety Data Sheets (SDS).
2. Create CERS account at https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/ → "Business Portal Sign In" → "Create New Account."
3. Complete HMBP in CERS: Facility Information, Business Activities, Hazardous Materials Inventory, Site Map (California Annotated Map), Emergency Response/Contingency Plan, Employee Training Plan.
4. Attach Santa Cruz County Additional Locally Collected Information form and (if applicable) Small Quantity Chemical Inventory form.
5. Submit via CERS green submit button; print confirmation for records.
6. SCCEH will issue invoice for permit fee after accepting HMBP.
