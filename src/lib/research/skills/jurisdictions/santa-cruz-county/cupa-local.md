## Agency

**County of Santa Cruz Health Services Agency — Environmental Health Division (EHS)**
Designated CUPA for all of Santa Cruz County since **1996**. This is the **sole CUPA** county-wide — no city-run CUPAs and no Participating Agencies (PAs). All four incorporated cities (Santa Cruz, Watsonville, Capitola, Scotts Valley) are served by the county CUPA for all Unified Program elements.

## Local Ordinance

- **Municipal Code chapter:** Santa Cruz County Code (SCCC) **Chapter 7.100** — governs hazardous materials storage, use, and disposal, including quantities **below** State reporting thresholds.
- **Key local provision:** SCCC §7.100 requires a Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP) for quantities less than State thresholds. Businesses storing any regulated chemical must contact EHS to determine applicability. Local lower thresholds apply — confirmed amounts as of permit information sheet (rev. 04/2018):
  - State thresholds that trigger HMBP statewide: ≥55 gallons liquid / ≥200 cf compressed gas / ≥500 lb solid.
  - SCCC 7.100 can require HMBPs below these quantities — contact EHS to determine if applicable.

## Programs Administered (Six Unified Program Elements)

1. **Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP) / Hazardous Materials Inventories** — chemical inventories, site maps, emergency response plans, employee training plans
2. **California Accidental Release Prevention (CalARP) / Risk Management Plans (RMP)**
3. **Underground Storage Tanks (UST)**
4. **Aboveground Petroleum Storage Act (APSA)** — non-SPCC and SPCC facilities
5. **Hazardous Waste Generators** (including tiered permits and non-RCRA generators)
6. **On-site Hazardous Waste Treatment** (tiered permits)

Additional programs administered by EHS (not part of core Unified Program but co-located):
- Medical Waste generators
- Incident Response / emergency releases

## HMBP Electronic Submittal Portal

Mandatory electronic submittal via **California Environmental Reporting System (CERS)** since January 1, 2013 (HSC §25404(e)(2-5), AB 2286).
- CERS portal: https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/
- Locally-required supplemental documentation (locally-collected information form, annotated site map, small-quantity chemical inventory form if applicable) uploaded via CERS.

## Permit Process

1. Determine applicability (contact EHS if below State thresholds to check SCCC 7.100 requirements).
2. Establish CERS account at https://cers.calepa.ca.gov/.
3. Complete and submit HMBP electronically via CERS including: Facility Information, Business Owner/Operator ID, Hazardous Materials Inventory (all chemicals with CAS#, SDS data, Federal Hazard Class), Site Map, Emergency Response/Contingency Plan, Employee Training Plan, and supplemental locally-required documents.
4. Submit any additional program modules (UST, APSA, CalARP) as applicable.
5. EHS reviews and accepts HMBP; invoice issued for permit fee.
6. Permit renewed annually; annual updates required via CERS.

## Significant Industrial Users / EHS Inspection Program

EHS inspects HMBP-regulated facilities; inspection frequency based on complexity and compliance history. Enforcement under SCCC Chapter 7.100 and California HSC Chapter 6.95.
