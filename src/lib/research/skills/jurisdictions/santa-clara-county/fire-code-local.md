## Adopted Code Edition

Santa Clara County has adopted the **2022 California Fire Code (CFC)** with local
amendments. The county code chapter is **Title B, Division B7 — Fire Protection**
of the Santa Clara County Ordinance Code. The adopting ordinance is catalogued by
Municode as **Ordinance No. NS-300.903**; the county uses the "NS-300" series for
Title B (Regulations) amendments. The 2025 CFC (based on the 2024 IFC) took effect
statewide on January 1, 2026 — contact the Fire Marshal's Office to confirm whether
the county has enacted a parallel adopting ordinance before advising on the current
enforceable edition.

## AHJ Scope

The **Santa Clara County Fire Department (SCCFD)** is the Authority Having
Jurisdiction (AHJ) for:
- Unincorporated county areas
- Contract cities: **Campbell, Cupertino, Los Altos, Los Altos Hills, Los Gatos,
  Monte Sereno, Saratoga**

**San José Fire Department** is the separate AHJ within City of San José limits —
(408) 535-7770. The **Santa Clara County Department of Planning and Development —
Fire Marshal's Office** handles fire code enforcement in the unincorporated county.

## Local Amendments — Key Provisions

The county participates in the Santa Clara County Fire Code Work Group which
develops coordinated local amendments to each CFC edition. Confirmed amendment
categories include:

- **§B7-12 — Wildland-Urban Interface / Defensible Space**: Localized WUI rules
  for hillside unincorporated areas (Los Gatos foothills, Saratoga, Los Altos Hills).
- **Administrative amendments**: Enforcement procedures, permit appeal processes.
- **Geological/Seismic**: Amendments justified by proximity to San Andreas, Hayward,
  and Calaveras faults; liquefaction hazard zones in northern Santa Clara Valley.
- **Climatic**: Late-summer diurnal dryness, Diablo winds.

Specific amended CFC sections (e.g., §5704 flammable liquid quantities, §6004 hazmat
permit thresholds) are **not published on public-facing web pages**. The Fire Marshal's
Office releases adopted amendment tables case-by-case.

## Hazardous Materials Permit Thresholds

The CFC establishes baseline Maximum Allowable Quantities (MAQ) per control area
for hazardous materials (Table 5003.1.1(1)). Local amendments may modify these
thresholds downward. **No published local quantity deviations from the state CFC MAQ
table were findable online as of 2026-06-03.** Verify current thresholds directly with:

- SCCFD Fire Prevention: (408) 378-4010 | info@sccfd.org
- Fire Marshal's Office (unincorporated): (408) 299-5770

## Standards & Specifications

SCCFD publishes 25+ technical standards documents organized by category:
- **A-series**: Apparatus access, speed humps, fire lane marking
- **C-series**: Two-way communications, ERRC systems, fire alarm
- **SP-series**: Commercial/residential sprinklers, underground piping, standpipes
- **W-series**: Water supply tanks, private hydrants, marking
- **SI-series**: Special occupancies (day care, carnivals, hazmat, construction)

## Fee Information

Fee schedules are jurisdiction-specific. The SCCFD page at
https://www.sccfd.org/fire-prevention/municipal-codes-fees/ links to each
contract city's code and fee schedule. No fee amounts are published on public pages.
Call (408) 378-4010 for current permit fees.

## Verification Note

The 2025 CFC took effect January 1, 2026. The county adopted the 2022 CFC via
NS-300.903; a 2025 CFC adoption ordinance may be pending. Always confirm the
current enforceable edition with the AHJ before advising clients.
