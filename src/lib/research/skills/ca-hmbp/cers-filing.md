## Filing Portal
**CERS** (California Environmental Reporting System): https://cers.calepa.ca.gov
All HMBP submissions must go through CERS — paper submission is no longer accepted.

## What to Submit in CERS
1. **Business Activities** — NAICS code, SIC code, employee count, operating hours
2. **Business Owner/Operator Information**
3. **Hazardous Materials Inventory** (HMI) — for each material above threshold:
   - Chemical name, CAS number, trade name
   - Maximum daily quantity, average daily quantity
   - State of matter, physical state
   - Health/fire/reactivity hazard categories
   - Storage location(s) with coordinates or description
4. **Site Map** — facility layout showing storage locations
5. **Emergency Response/Contingency Plan** — written plan with:
   - Emergency coordinator name/24-hr phone
   - Evacuation procedures
   - Emergency response procedures by chemical class
   - Notification list (CUPA, DTSC, fire, RWQCB)

## Timeline / Triggers
| Event | Deadline |
|---|---|
| New business with threshold quantities | Within 30 days of commencing business |
| New chemical added above threshold | Within 30 days of first use/storage |
| Annual update (all businesses) | March 1 each year |
| Significant change (quantity, location) | Within 30 days of change |
| Business closure | Within 30 days |

## Fees
Fees set by local CUPA — vary significantly by jurisdiction and inventory size. Examples:
- Los Angeles County: ~$350–$2,500/yr depending on inventory complexity
- Orange County CUPA: tiered by chemical count
[VERIFY with local CUPA — fees change annually]

## Inspection
CUPAs inspect on 1–3 year cycles. Inspectors verify: CERS data matches on-site inventory, site map accurate, employees trained, emergency response plan current.
