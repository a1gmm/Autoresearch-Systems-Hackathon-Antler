## Purpose

**Section 440** incorporates federal New Source Performance Standards (NSPS, 40 CFR Part 60) by reference for new stationary sources. **Section 470** provides a local ATCM (Air Toxics Control Measure) alternative to the CARB statewide program for agricultural compression-ignition (CI) diesel engines, which are common in Lake County's agricultural and geothermal industries.

---

## Section 440 — New Source Performance Standards

### Key Provision
> "Section 440: New Source Performance Standards (NSPS) All new sources of emissions shall comply with the applicable standards of performance for new stationary sources promulgated under Section 111 of the Federal Clean Air Act."

### NSPS Subparts Adopted by Reference
The rulebook incorporates the following NSPS subparts (as of July 2001 revision), among others relevant to common industrial operations:
- Subpart EE — Surface Coating of Metal Furniture
- Subpart GG — Stationary Gas Turbines
- Subpart KK — Auto and Light-Duty Truck Surface Coating
- Subpart LL — Graphic Arts: Publication Rotogravure Printing
- Subpart MM — Pressure Sensitive Tape and Label Surface Coating
- Subpart NN — Industrial Surface Coating, Large Appliances
- Subpart TT — Metal Coil Surface Coating
- Subpart WW — Beverage Can Surface Coating Industry
- Subpart VVV — Polymeric Coating Facilities
- Subpart XX — Magnetic Tape Coating Facilities
- Subpart ZZ — Flexible Vinyl and Urethane Coating and Printing

These are incorporated by reference to 40 CFR Part 60 — the district does **not** have standalone local coating/solvent VOC rules beyond NSPS adoption.

## Section 470 — Agricultural CI Engine ATCM

### Applicability
> "Except as provided in District Rule Section 470 (D), this Rule applies to any person who either sells a stationary CI engine, offers a stationary CI engine for sale or lease, purchases a stationary CI engine, or owns or operates a stationary CI engine with a maximum rated horsepower of greater than 50 brake horsepower used in an agricultural operation."

### Section 470(D) — Full Exemptions (No Permit Required)
> "(1) Engines with a maximum rating of 50 brake horsepower or less.
> (2) Engines powering an agricultural wind machine.
> (3) Engines used exclusively to power Mobile Agricultural Equipment.
> (4) Engines operating with a valid statewide Portable Equipment Registration Program (PERP) certificate, provided the engine meets the same emissions control standard as would be required of the stationary source and is operated for no more than one growing season."

### Section 470(E) — Control Technology Exemptions (Permit Still Required)
> "(1) Emergency engine use substantiated by a public official that is necessary to respond to a fire, flood, or police action.
> (2) A low use engine. [≤200 hours/calendar year per non-resettable hour meter, not grid-connected, not load-shifting, no significant health risk]
> (3) Remotely located engine. [In attainment area; >0.5 mile from residence, school, or hospital; <1,000 hours/year]
> (4) Engines used exclusively as emergency backup for electrical line power service at an agricultural operation, operated for testing and maintenance no more than 20 hours [per year]."

### Key Definitions
- **Low Use Engine**: "An engine that is operated no more than 200 hours per calendar year as determined by an operational non-resettable hour meter, provided the engine: A) Is not used to generate electrical power that is fed into the electrical utility power grid; B) Is not used to reduce electrical power purchased by the stationary source; and C) Does not cause a significant health risk due to location in close proximity to residents, schools, commercial or public areas."
- **Remotely Located Engine**: "A stationary diesel-fueled CI engine: A) Used in agriculture that is located in a Federal Ambient Air Quality area that is designated as unclassifiable or attainment for all Particulate Matter and Ozone [NAAQS]; B) Is located more than one-half mile from any residential area, school, or hospital; and C) Is operated less than 1,000 hours per year."
- **Registration**: "For the purposes of this Rule, Registration is equivalent to a Permit."

## Determination Notes

- Lake County AQMD does **not** have independent local VOC coating or graphic arts rules — coating operations are regulated through NSPS adoption (Section 440) and the general ATC/PTO permit requirement.
- For agricultural diesel engines: the 50 BHP cutoff is the bright line — engines ≤50 BHP are fully exempt from Section 470 (Section 470(D)(1)).
- Low-use engines (≤200 hr/yr) still require a registration/permit but qualify for a control technology exemption pathway under Section 470(E)(2).
- PERP-certificated engines used for one growing season or less are fully exempt (Section 470(D)(4)).
- The agricultural CI engine rule (Section 470) was adopted from the CARB statewide ATCM as a local equivalent program.
