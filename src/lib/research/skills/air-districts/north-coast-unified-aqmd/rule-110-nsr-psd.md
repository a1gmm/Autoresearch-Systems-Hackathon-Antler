## Purpose

Rule 110 establishes pre-construction review requirements for new and modified stationary sources of air pollution and provides mechanisms, including emission offsets, by which authorities to construct may be granted without interfering with attainment or maintenance of ambient air quality standards.

> "This Rule shall provide for no net increase in emissions, pursuant to Section 40918 of the H&SC, from new or modified stationary sources which emit, or have the potential to emit, 25 tons per year or more of any non-attainment pollutant or its precursors."

Effective date: December 9, 2010. Current version adopted July 9, 2015 (Resolution 2015-9).

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### B. Applicability

> "This Rule shall apply to all new stationary sources and emission units and all modifications to existing stationary sources and emissions units that, after construction, emit or may emit any affected pollutant within the District."

Permanent shutdown presumption: Any facility that has ceased operation for two or more continuous years (excluding minimal maintenance) is presumed permanently shut down; five or more continuous years is conclusively permanently shut down.

### E.1 – Table 1.0 Significance Thresholds (BACT trigger)

> "An applicant shall apply BACT to any new emissions unit or modification of an existing emissions unit, if the change would result in an increase in the potential to emit from the new unit or modification of existing equipment. BACT shall be applied to each new unit or modification only for the pollutant(s) emitted in excess of the threshold(s) listed in Table 1.0 Significance Thresholds below."

**Table 1.0 Significance Thresholds** (verbatim from Rule 110, p. 7):

| Pollutant | Daily (pounds/day) | Annual (tons/year) |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon monoxide | 500.0 | 100 |
| Fluorides | 15.0 | 3.0 |
| Hydrogen sulfide | 50.0 | 10.0 |
| Lead | 3.2 | 0.6 |
| Nitrogen oxides | 50.0 | 40.0 |
| Particulate matter (PM10) | 80.0 | 15.0 |
| Particulate matter (PM2.5) | 50.0 | 10.0 |
| Reactive organic compounds | 50.0 | 40.0 |
| Reduced sulfur compounds | 50.0 | 10.0 |
| Sulfur oxides | 80.0 | 40.0 |
| Sulfuric acid mist | 35.0 | 7.0 |
| Total reduced sulfur compounds | 50.0 | 10.0 |

### E.2 – Offset Requirements

> "Offsets shall be required for a new stationary source with a potential to emit, calculated pursuant to Section (F)(5) of this Rule, non-attainment pollutants or their precursors equal to or exceeding 25 tons per year. The amount of offsets required shall be at least equal to that portion of the potential to emit which exceeds 25 tons per year."

For modified sources: offset required for the portion exceeding 25 tons/year once that threshold is crossed. After exceeding the threshold, all future increases from new or modified emissions units shall be offset.

CO offset exception: Offsets shall not be required for CO if the applicant demonstrates through an impact analysis that ambient air quality standards are not violated.

### J. Exclusions

> "New Source Review Procedures in accordance with Rule 110 shall not be required for temporary stationary sources which will be in operation for less than 90 days duration provided BACT is applied in accordance with Section 5.1 and such operations will not interfere with the control strategy of the SIP."

### K. Prevention of Significant Deterioration (PSD)

> "The provisions of this section shall only apply to new or modified sources which have the potential to emit greater than the applicable annual significance threshold of the NSR Regulated Pollutant(s) listed in Table 1.0 Significance Thresholds of Section (E)(1)."

PSD requires air quality analysis (ambient monitoring data plus dispersion modeling) for major sources to ensure no violation of PSD increments for Class I, II, or III areas.

### Key Definitions

- **BACT**: "The most effective emission control device, emission limit, or technique which has been required or used for the type of equipment comprising such emissions unit unless the applicant demonstrates to the satisfaction of the APCO that such limitations are not achievable."
- **Modification**: "Any physical change or operational change to an existing emissions unit, including changing hours of operation or production rate, which would necessitate a change in permit conditions." Routine maintenance or repair and changes in ownership are NOT modifications.
- **Potential to Emit**: "The maximum daily and/or annual capacity of an emission unit to emit a pollutant under its physical and operational design. Any physical or operational limitation on the capacity of the unit to emit a pollutant, including pollution control equipment and restrictions in hours of operation on the type or amount of material combusted, stored, or processed, shall be treated as..." [capped at the enforceable limit].

## Determination Notes

- Table 1.0 thresholds function as a two-way gate: (1) they trigger the BACT requirement under Rule 110 E.1, and (2) under Rule 102(D), any source whose emissions exceed these thresholds loses eligibility for the Rule 102 permit exemptions.
- The offset trigger (25 tons/year) for non-attainment pollutants is a separate, higher threshold than the BACT/significance thresholds.
- The 90-day temporary source exclusion (Section J) still requires BACT — it only waives the full NSR process.
- The North Coast Air Basin covers Del Norte, Humboldt, and Trinity Counties. Attainment status affects which pollutants have mandatory offset requirements.
- LAER (Lowest Achievable Emission Rate) applies to non-attainment pollutants for major sources; BACT applies to all affected pollutants at significance thresholds.
- PSD applies only above the annual significance thresholds in Table 1.0 — a separate track from basic NSR.
