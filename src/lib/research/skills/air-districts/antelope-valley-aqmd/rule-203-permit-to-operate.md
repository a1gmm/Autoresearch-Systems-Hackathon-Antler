## Purpose

Rule 203 (Permit to Operate) is the companion to Rule 201. It prohibits operation or use of any equipment that may cause the issuance of air contaminants, or may reduce or control such issuance, without a written Permit to Operate from the APCO. Equipment may not operate contrary to the conditions stated in the permit.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

Verbatim from Rule 203 (Adopted: 1/9/76; Amended: 1/5/90; Amended: 8/19/97):

> "A person shall not operate or use any equipment, the use of which may cause the issuance of air contaminants, or the use of which may reduce or control the issuance of air contaminants, without first obtaining a written permit to operate from the Air Pollution Control Officer or except as provided in Rule 202. The equipment shall not be operated contrary to the conditions specified in the permit to operate."

Key operational notes:
- Applies to operation of equipment, not just construction — a separate Permit to Operate must be obtained after the Authority to Construct (Rule 201).
- Exception provided in Rule 202 (specific conditional exemptions).
- Equipment otherwise exempt from permitting is defined in Rule 219.

SIP status: Submitted as amended 8/19/97 on 3/10/98; Approved 11/9/78, 43 FR 52237, 40 CFR 52.220(c)(39)(iii)(B); 40 CFR 52.220(c)(31)(vi)(C).

## Determination Notes

- Rule 203 is triggered at the operation stage. A project that obtained an ATC under Rule 201 must then obtain a Permit to Operate before commencing operation.
- No standalone numeric emission threshold in Rule 203; thresholds for exemption are in Rule 219.
- Equipment operating without a Permit to Operate where one is required violates Rule 203 — separate and additional from a Rule 201 violation.
- Title V facilities are governed by Regulation XXX (Title V Permits) layered on top of Rules 201/203.
