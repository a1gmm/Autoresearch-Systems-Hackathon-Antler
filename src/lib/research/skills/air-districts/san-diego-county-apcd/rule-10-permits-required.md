## Purpose / Applicability

Rule 10 is the foundational permit requirement rule for San Diego County APCD (Regulation II). It requires anyone who builds, erects, alters, or replaces equipment that may emit air contaminants — or equipment used to eliminate, reduce, or control air contaminant emissions — to first obtain an Authority to Construct (ATC) and subsequently a Permit to Operate (PTO) from the Air Pollution Control Officer. Adopted 1/1/69; last revised effective 5/15/96.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

**Authority to Construct (Section a):**

> "Any person building, erecting, altering or replacing any article, machine, equipment or other contrivance, the use of which may cause the issuance of air contaminants or the use of which may eliminate or reduce or control the issuance of air contaminant, shall first obtain written authorization for such construction from the Air Pollution Control Officer. A separate Authority to Construct will be required for each piece of equipment, product line, system, process line or process that produces a product or performs a service independently of other equipment, product lines, systems, process lines or processes. An Authority to Construct shall remain in effect until the Permit to Operate the equipment for which the application was filed is granted or denied or the application is cancelled."

**Permit to Operate (Section b):**

> "Before a person operates or uses, or causes to be operated or used, any article, machine, equipment or other contrivance described in Rule 10(a) (Authority to Construct) that person shall obtain a written Permit to Operate from the Air Pollution Control Officer. No Permit to Operate or use shall be granted either by the Air Pollution Control Officer or the Hearing Board for any article, machine, equipment or contrivance described in Rule 10(a) which is constructed or installed without authorization as required by Rule 10(a) until all information required for the Authority to Construct of Rule 10(a) is presented to the Air Pollution Control Officer and such article, machine, equipment or contrivance is altered, if necessary, and made to conform to the standards set forth in Rule 20 and elsewhere in these Rules and Regulations."

**Posting of Permit to Operate (Section c):**
The current Permit to Operate must be affixed to the equipment in a clearly visible and accessible manner, or posted within 25 feet of the equipment if attachment is impractical.

**Annual Renewal (Section h):**
Permits to Operate are renewable annually on a staggered schedule. Expired permits may be reinstated within the first six months after expiration upon application and payment of fees plus penalty.

**Change of Location (Section i):**
> "Any person who possesses a Permit to Operate any article, machine, equipment or other contrivance and desires to change the location of such article, machine, equipment or other contrivance shall first apply to the Air Pollution Control Officer for an Authority to Construct and Permit to Operate."
Note: does not apply to portable equipment moving between work locations.

**Transfer of Ownership (Section j):**
Equipment with a valid PTO being transferred to a new owner cannot operate until a transfer application is filed. If the PTO was valid within the preceding 18 months and the application is submitted with required fees, the application is deemed a temporary PTO until a formal PTO is issued or denied.

## Determination Notes

- Rule 10 is the trigger rule: if equipment "may cause the issuance of air contaminants," an ATC is required before construction begins, and a PTO is required before operation begins.
- A separate ATC/PTO is required for each independently operating piece of equipment, product line, or process line.
- Exemptions from Rule 10 are found in **Rule 11** (Exemptions from Rule 10 Permit Requirements). If a unit is listed in Rule 11 and meets the stated conditions, no ATC/PTO is required (but all other applicable rules still apply).
- New Source Review obligations (BACT, offsets, etc.) are governed by Rules 20.1–20.4 and are triggered on top of the Rule 10 permit requirement.
- The NSPS/NESHAP corollary requirement is in Rule 10.1: any source subject to a federal NSPS or NESHAP that has been delegated to SDAPCD must also comply with Regulation X or XI.
