## Purpose / Applicability

Rule 20.1 is the general provisions rule for New Source Review (NSR) in San Diego County APCD, establishing definitions, emission calculation methods, and the BACT/LAER/offset framework that governs new and modified emission units. Revised Adopted 10/14/21, Effective 10/28/22. It applies to any new or modified emission unit, replacement emission unit, relocated emission unit, or portable emission unit for which an ATC or PTO is required under Rule 10, unless the unit is exempt under Rule 11 or specific subsections of Rule 20.1 itself.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

**Applicability (Section a):**
> "Except as provided in Rule 11 – Exemptions from Rule 10 Permit Requirements, Section (b) Exemptions of this rule, or Subsections (d)(1)(ii)(B) or (d)(4)(iii)(C) of this rule, this rule applies to any new or modified emission unit, any replacement emission unit, any relocated emission unit or any portable emission unit for which an Authority to Construct or Permit to Operate is required pursuant to Rule 10 – Permits Required."

**Exemptions from NSR (Section b):**
Rule 20.1 NSR requirements (and those of Rules 20.2, 20.3, 20.4) do not apply to:

> "(1) Any emission unit for which a permit is required solely due to a change in Rule 11, provided the unit was operated in San Diego County at any time within one year prior to the date of adoption of the applicable Rule 11 change and provided a District permit application for the unit is submitted within one year after the date upon which permit requirements became applicable to the unit."

> "(2) The following changes, provided such changes are not contrary to any permit condition, and the change does not result in an increase in the potential to emit of any air contaminant not previously emitted: (i) Repair or routine maintenance of an existing emission unit. (ii) A change of ownership. (iii) An increase in the hours of operation. (iv) Use of alternate fuel or raw material."

> "(3) Portable and stationary abrasive blasting equipment which comply with the requirements of 17 CCR Section 92000 et. seq. This exemption shall not apply if the abrasive blasting equipment would be, by itself, a major stationary source."

> "(5) Air compressors used exclusively to pressurize nuclear reactor containment domes, provided the compressors are not operated more than 50 hours over any two-year period."

> "(6) Applications for modified Authority to Construct or modified Permit to Operate which are for the sole purpose of reducing an emission unit's potential to emit and which will not result in a modified emission unit, a modified stationary source or an actual emission reduction calculated pursuant to Rule 20.1(d)(4)(ii) shall be exempt from the Best Available Control Technology (BACT), Lowest Achievable Emission Rate (LAER), AQIA and Emission Offset provisions of Rules 20.1, 20.2, 20.3 and 20.4."

**Major Stationary Source / Major Modification Thresholds:**
Tables 20.1-5a and 20.1-5b define Federal Major Modification and Federal Major Stationary Source levels (triggering federal PSD and NSR offsets requirements). Tables 20.1-6a and 20.1-6b define the District-level Major Modification and Major Stationary Source thresholds (triggering LAER and offsets under Rule 20.3).

**Table 20.1-8 — Non-Criteria Pollutant Emissions Significance Levels:**
Sets the significance levels for hazardous air pollutants and other non-criteria pollutants above which AQIA and BACT apply.

**Potential to Emit Calculation (Section d(1)):**
> "(i) Calculation of Pre-Project and Post-Project Potential to Emit" — the rule specifies how to calculate PTE for new and modified units including capacity, control equipment, and enforceable permit conditions.

**Emission Offsets (Section d(5)):**
Applies when a new or modified major stationary source or major modification results in a net emissions increase above significance levels — requires securing Emission Reduction Credits (ERCs) at a ratio specified in Rule 20.3.

**Standards for Granting Permits (Rule 20 cross-reference):**
The overarching Rule 20 (Adopted 4/25/89) states the Air Pollution Control Officer shall deny an ATC or PTO unless the applicant shows that equipment is designed, controlled, or equipped such that it will operate in compliance with Health and Safety Code Sections 41700, 41701, and 44300, and Rules 20.1–20.7 and other rules.

## Determination Notes

- Rule 20.1 is the analytical engine behind NSR: it defines PTE, major/minor thresholds, and what control technology is required.
- The NSR tier determines the review level: minor source review (BACT only, Rule 20.2) vs. major source review (LAER + offsets, Rule 20.3) vs. portable equipment (Rule 20.4).
- Section (b)(2) exemptions are critical for operational changes at existing permitted facilities: a change of ownership, increase in hours of operation, or use of alternate fuel does NOT require NSR review as long as no new pollutants are introduced and no permit conditions are violated.
- For a project that spans multiple emission units, emissions increases are aggregated across the entire project (Table 20.1-5a and 20.1-6a).
- Rule 20.5 governs power plants; Rule 20.6 and 20.7 govern additional specialized categories.
