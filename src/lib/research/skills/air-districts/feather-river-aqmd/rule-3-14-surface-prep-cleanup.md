## Purpose

Rule 3.14 limits volatile organic compound (VOC) emissions from surface preparation and clean-up operations, and from storage and disposal of cleaning materials. Adopted 6/1991; amended 8/1/2011, 8/1/2016; EPA-approved 4/26/2021 (86 FR 21941).

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Applicability (Section A.2)

> "The provisions of this Rule apply to any owner or operator of any facility that uses VOC containing materials for surface preparation and clean-up, or any person who sells or distributes any solvent subject to the provisions of this rule."

### General Exemptions (Section A.3)

The following operations are **exempt** from all provisions of Rule 3.14 except Section E.3 (Burden of Proof):

> "a. Cleaning operations using a solvent containing **no more than 25 grams of VOC per liter** of material;
>
> b. Cleaning with aerosol products provided that the facility uses **less than 160 fluid ounces of aerosol products per day**. The use of such products shall comply with CARB regulations.
>
> c. Dry cleaning operations;
>
> d. Janitorial cleaning;
>
> e. Stripping of cured coatings, cured adhesives, and cured inks;
>
> f. Degreasers with an open top surface area of **1.0 square foot or less** or with a capacity of **2.0 gallons or less**, using unheated non-halogenated solvent exclusively, and the reservoir is covered when not processing work;
>
> g. Any solvent degreasing operations that are subject to the National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants (NESHAP) requirements of 40 CFR Part 63 Subpart T — National Emission Standards for Halogenated Solvent Cleaning;
>
> h. Cleaning operations in printing pre-press or graphic arts pre-press areas, including the cleaning of film processors, color scanners, plate processors, film cleaning, and plate cleaning.
>
> i. Sanitizing products which are labeled and applied to food-contact surfaces that are used to process dry and low-moisture food products and are not rinsed prior to contact with food."

### Solvent Requirement Exemptions (Section A.4)

The VOC solvent limits of Section C.1 do not apply to:

> "a. Wipe cleaning of solar cells, laser hardware, high precision optics, or polycarbonate plastics;
>
> b. Wipe cleaning for performance laboratory tests on coatings, adhesives or inks, research and development programs, and laboratory tests in quality assurance laboratories;
>
> c. Cleaning of cotton swabs to remove cottonseed oil before cleaning of high precision optics;
>
> d. Cleaning of paper-based gaskets, and clutch assemblies where rubber is bonded to metal by means of an adhesive;
>
> e. Cleaning of sterilization ink indicating equipment provided that the **solvent usage is less than 1.5 gallons per day**;
>
> f. Coating and adhesive application processes utilized to manufacture transdermal drug delivery products using ethyl acetate;
>
> g. Cleaning applications using bug and tar removal, provided that the bug and tar remover is regulated under the Consumer Products Regulation (California Code of Regulations Section 94507 et seq.)."

### Exempt Compounds (Section B.13)

> "EXEMPT COMPOUNDS: As defined in District Rule 1.1."

Rule 1.1 (adopted 6/1991, amended 6/3/2024, amendment pending 6/1/2026) defines:

> "Exempt Compounds: Those compounds listed as excluded from the definition of Volatile Organic Compounds in 40 CFR 51.100(s)."

### Storage and Disposal (Section E in rule)

The rule also includes requirements for storage of VOC-containing materials and proper disposal practices to minimize evaporative emissions.

## Determination Notes

- The 25 g VOC/liter exemption threshold in section A.3(a) is a practical "de minimis" exemption for low-VOC cleaning products — water-based and low-VOC cleaning agents that meet this threshold are automatically excluded.
- Aerosol exemption (160 fl oz/day) applies per-facility per-day; facilities using multiple aerosol cans for large operations may not qualify.
- The small-degreaser exemption (≤1 sq ft surface / ≤2 gallon capacity, unheated, non-halogenated, covered) allows bench-top parts cleaners without permit or compliance obligations under this rule.
- Rule 3.14 does NOT require a permit by itself — it is an emission standards rule. However, equipment subject to this rule that also requires a permit under Reg IV (Rule 4.1) must comply with Rule 3.14 as a permit condition.
- Rule 3.19 (Vehicle and Mobile Equipment Coating Operations) and Rule 3.20 (Wood Products Coating Operations) impose separate, stricter VOC limits for their respective applications and should be consulted for those industries.
- Related rules: Rule 3.15 (Architectural Coatings), Rule 3.19 (Vehicle/Mobile Equipment Coating), Rule 3.20 (Wood Products Coating).
