## Purpose

Rule 10.1 establishes preconstruction review for new and modified stationary sources, requiring offsets, Best Available Control Technology (BACT), compliance with all District rules, and air quality impact analysis to ensure no net increase in emissions and no interference with ambient air quality standards attainment. Adopted 2/8/1993; last amended 10/6/2014; EPA-approved 10/5/2015 (80 FR 60047).

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Applicability (Section B.1)

> "This rule shall apply to all new and modified stationary sources which are subject to District permit requirements and which, after construction, emit or may emit any affected pollutants."

### Major Source / Major Modification Thresholds — Offset Requirements (Section E.2)

The district spans multiple nonattainment areas. Offset obligations are triggered when a project constitutes a "major source" or "major modification":

> Rule 10.1 Section E.1 (BACT) and Section E.2 (offsets) apply to new stationary sources or modifications resulting in emissions increases above significance thresholds defined in the rule definitions for "Major Source" and "Major Modification."

For the Feather River district's nonattainment areas, VOC/NOx major source thresholds vary by subarea:
- **Sacramento Federal Nonattainment Area (SFNA)** portions: 25 tons/year for VOC or NOx (extreme/severe ozone nonattainment thresholds per Clean Air Act § 182).
- **Yuba City-Marysville Nonattainment Area (YC-MNA)** (PM2.5): applicable major source threshold applies.

> "The Regulations in effect at the time any application for an Authority to Construct is deemed complete shall apply, except when a new federal requirement not yet incorporated into this rule applies to the new or modified source." (Section B.3)

### BACT Requirement (Section E.1)

> "The APCO shall require the applicant to utilize Best Available Control Technology (BACT) for any new stationary source or modification which results in a net increase in emissions of any affected pollutant."

### Offset Ratios (Section E.2)

Required offsets must be obtained within the same nonattainment area or its basin (with APCO and EPA approval for cross-district offsets):

> "Offsets which are used for Federal requirements in the SFNA and are obtained from a source located in another Air District within the SFNA may be used only if the provisions of Section E.2.e.3 above are met and EPA approval is obtained."

### Seasonal Offset Provision (Section E.2.g–h)

> "Emissions of reactive organic gases during the quarters starting April 1 and July 1 may be used to offset emission increases of reactive organic gases during the quarters starting October 1 and January 1. Emissions of nitrogen oxides during the quarters starting April 1 and July 1 may be used to offset emission increases of nitrogen oxides during the quarters starting October 1 and January 1."

### Ambient Air Quality Standards — Denial Authority (Section E.3–E.4)

> "In no case shall the emissions from the new or modified stationary source cause or make worse the violation of an ambient air quality standard."

> "The APCO shall deny any Authority to Construct or Permit to Operate if the APCO finds that the subject of the application would not comply with the standards set forth in this rule." (Section E.4)

### Interpollutant Offsets (Section E.2.f)

> "The APCO may approve the substitution of one air contaminant for another air contaminant to meet the requirement for offsetting an emission increase on a case-by-case basis, provided that the applicant demonstrates to the satisfaction of the APCO, through the use of an impact analysis, that the emission increases from the new or modified source and offsets provided will result in a net air quality benefit and will not cause or contribute to a violation of any air quality standard."

> "PM10 emissions shall not be allowed to offset nitrogen oxide or reactive organic compound emissions."

### Potential Visibility Impacts (Section E.8)

> "The APCO shall consult with the Federal Land Manager on a proposed major stationary source or major modification that may impact visibility in any Class I Area...if the net emissions increase from the new or modified source exceeds 10 tons/year of PM2.5, 15 tons/year of PM10, or 40 tons/year of NOx; and the location of the source, relative to the closest boundary of a specified federal Class I area is within 20 miles."

### Administrative Timelines (Section H)

> "Within 30 days after receipt of the application" — district must determine completeness. (H.1.a)

> "Within 180 days after acceptance of an application as complete, the APCO shall take final action on the application after considering all written comments." (H.5.a)

### PSD / Prevention of Significant Deterioration (Rule 10.10)

Rule 10.10 incorporates 40 CFR Part 52.21 by reference (adopted 8/1/2011; EPA-approved 11/12/2015) and applies to major stationary sources in PSD attainment areas. PSD review is separate from the nonattainment NSR process under Rule 10.1.

## Determination Notes

- Rule 10.1 applies **on top of** the basic Reg IV permit requirement whenever NSR thresholds are crossed. A project can require both an ATC (Reg IV) and NSR review (Rule 10.1) simultaneously.
- The 23 lb/day significance threshold in the legacy county permit rules (Reg IV Rule 4.5) is the general permit trigger; Rule 10.1 NSR/BACT/offset requirements kick in at the major-source thresholds (typically 25 tpy VOC/NOx in nonattainment areas).
- Both PM2.5 (YC-MNA) and ozone precursor (SFNA) nonattainment programs are active in different portions of the district. Projects must check which area designation applies to their site.
- Rule 10.9 (Rice Straw Emission Reduction Credits and Banking, adopted 10/6/2014) provides an emission credit banking mechanism specific to this agricultural district.
