## Purpose

Establishes emission prohibitions applicable within the Glenn County APCD boundaries, including visible emissions limits, open burning prohibitions, petroleum storage/dispensing vapor controls, and particulate/sulfur emission standards. These rules are separate from the permit framework (Article III) and apply regardless of permit status.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Section 76 – Visible Emissions

> "A person shall not discharge into the atmosphere from any single source of emission whatsoever, any air contaminant for a period or periods aggregating more than three minutes in any one hour which is: a. As dark or darker in shade as that designated as No. 2 on the Ringelmann Chart, as published by the United States Bureau of Mines, or b. Of such opacity as to obscure an observer's view to a degree equal to or greater than does smoke described in subsection 'a' above."

Exceptions (Section 77) include agricultural operations, orchard heaters producing less than 1 gram/minute of unconsumed solid carbonaceous matter, and fires permitted by public officers for fire prevention or training.

### Section 80 – Open Fires (Prohibition)

> "No person shall, after December 31, 1971, use open fires for the purpose of disposal of petroleum wastes, demolition debris, tires, tar, trees, wood waste, or other combustible or flammable solid or liquid waste; or for metal salvage or burning of automobile bodies."

### Section 81 – Open Fires Exceptions

Permitted exceptions include:
> "d. The burning for disposal of combustible waste, except garbage, of a single or two family dwelling on the premises of the dwelling."

> "f. Agricultural burning for which a permit has been issued pursuant to Section 10 of these Regulations."

> "g. Fires used to dispose of unusable wood waste from trees, vines, or shrubs only upon the property where grown may be authorized by the Air Pollution Control Officer under the following minimum conditions [...] 2. A permit for such burning shall be issued by the Air Pollution Control Officer prior to time of ignition. No such permit shall be issued unless satisfactory evidence has been submitted by the applicant to prove the following: a. That the proposed burn shall not create a nuisance. b. That the proposed burn is approved by the Fire Protection Agency having jurisdiction. c. That the moisture content of the wood waste is low enough to insure a clean burn."

> "h. Fires used only for the cooking of food for human consumption or set and used wholly for recreational purposes."

### Section 82 – Burning of Garbage

> "Notwithstanding any other provisions of these Regulations, the open burning of garbage is prohibited within the boundaries of the [Glenn County Air Pollution Control] District at any time."

### Section 83 – Petroleum Storage and Dispensing

> "No new gasoline storage tank with a capacity of 250 gallons or more shall be installed unless it is equipped with a permanent submerged fill pipe as described in Section 41950, Health and Safety Code[...]"

### Section 83.1 – Service Stations and Bulk Storage Plants

> "For bulk storage tanks at service stations and bulk plant facilities, a 90% (or greater) vapor balance system shall be utilized during filling. Service Stations with throughputs of less than 300,000 gallons per year are exempt when receiving shipments from local distributors with facilities not equipped to handle returning vapors."

### Section 83.2 – Petroleum Solvents

> "When it is determined by emission inventory that petroleum solvent degreasing operations amount to 1 ton per day, the District shall notify, in writing, each distributor in the county [that] the following statement is required on all such solvent containers holding 49 gallons or more: 'Keep containers in a closed container'."

Note: This is a labeling/notification trigger, not an operational permit requirement.

## Determination Notes

- No dedicated VOC coating rule, solvent usage rule, or graphic arts rule exists in the Glenn County APCD rulebook. The district relies on CARB/state emission limitations for these source categories.
- The open burning prohibition (Section 80) is absolute for demolition debris, tires, and petroleum wastes — no permit pathway exists.
- Wood waste burning on-site requires an advance permit from the APCO and fire agency approval.
- The 250-gallon threshold for gasoline storage submerged-fill-pipe requirement aligns with (but is distinct from) the Section 57 permit exemption for tanks under 250 gallons.
- Petroleum solvent degreasing at 1 ton/day triggers a district notification/labeling step — projects approaching this scale should track inventory.
