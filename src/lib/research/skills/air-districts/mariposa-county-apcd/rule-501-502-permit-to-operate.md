## Purpose

Regulation V (Rules 501–521) governs Permit to Operate (PTO) requirements. A PTO is required before operating any source that needed an Authority to Construct. This regulation covers application procedures, exemptions, conditional approvals, recordkeeping, and revocation.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Rule 501 – Permit Required (verbatim)

> "Before any source may be operated, a Permit to operate shall be obtained from the Air Pollution Control Officer. No Permit to Operate shall be granted either by the Air Pollution Control Officer or the Hearing Board for any source constructed or modified without authorization as required in Regulation IV until the information required is provided to the Air Pollution Control Officer and such source is altered, if necessary, and made to conform to the standards set forth in Regulation IV and elsewhere in these Rules and Regulations."

### Rule 502 – Exemptions to Rule 501 (verbatim)

> "The Air Pollution Control Officer may exempt from the requirements of Rule 501 any item of equipment specified in Rule 402, Exemptions to Rule 401."

This means all categories exempt from the ATC under Rule 402 are likewise exempt from the PTO requirement.

### Rule 504 – Action on Applications (verbatim)

> "The Air Pollution Control Officer shall act within 180 days after the filing date on a Permit to Operate application and shall notify the applicant in writing of his approval, conditional approval or denial."

### Rule 505 – Conditional Approval (verbatim)

> "The Air Pollution Control Officer may issue a Permit to operate subject to conditions which will insure the compliance of any equipment within the standards of these Rules and Regulations, in which case the conditions shall be specified in writing. Commencing work under an Authority to Construct, or observation under a Permit to Operate, shall be deemed acceptance of all the conditions so specified."

### Rule 508 – Posting of Permit to Operate (verbatim)

> "A person who has been granted a Permit to Operate under this Regulation shall firmly affix such Permit to Operate, an approved facsimile or other identification approved by the Air Pollution Control Officer upon the article, machine, equipment, or other contrivance in such a manner as to be clearly visible and accessible."

### Rule 513 – Source Recordkeeping (verbatim)

> "The owner or operator of any stationary source shall, upon notification from the Air Pollution Control Officer, maintains records of the nature and amounts of emissions from such source and/or any other information as may be deemed necessary by the Air Pollution Control Officer to determine whether such source is in compliance with applicable emission limitations or other control measures."

> "The information recorded shall be summarized and reported to the Air Pollution Control Officer … and shall be submitted within 45 days after the end of the reporting period. Reporting periods are January 1 – June 30 and July 1 – December 31…"

> "Information reported … shall be retained by the owner or operator for two years after the date on which the pertinent report is submitted."

### Rule 521 – Annual Renewal

PTO must be renewed annually. Fee schedule is governed by Regulation VI (Rule 601–606).

## Determination Notes

- **Sequence:** ATC (Regulation IV) must come first; PTO (Regulation V) follows after construction. Both are needed to legally build and operate a new emission source.
- **Exemptions mirror Rule 402:** All Rule 402 exempt categories (dwellings ≤2 family, comfort HVAC, lab equipment, welding, natural gas boilers <50 MMBtu/hr, small IC engines, implements of husbandry, <1 ton/yr sources) are also exempt from the PTO.
- **180-day clock:** APCO has 180 days from application filing to act on a PTO application.
- **Annual renewal required:** Facilities with PTOs must renew annually and pay applicable fees per Rule 601/602.
- **Posting required:** Permit must be physically affixed to the equipment or posted prominently on-site.
