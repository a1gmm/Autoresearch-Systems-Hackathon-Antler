## Purpose

Rule 422 restricts the use and sale of architectural coatings containing photochemically reactive solvents in Tuolumne County. Rule 423 limits the daily disposal or evaporation of photochemically reactive solvents. Both rules reduce VOC emissions that contribute to ozone formation (Tuolumne County is ozone nonattainment under the 2015 8-hour NAAQS).

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Rule 422 — Architectural Coatings

> "(a) A person shall not sell or offer for sale or use in Tuolumne County, in containers of one quart capacity or larger, any architectural coating containing photochemically reactive solvent, as defined in Rule 410 (k)."

> "(b) A person shall not employ, apply, evaporate or dry in Tuolumne County any architectural coating, purchased in containers of one quart capacity or larger, containing photochemically reactive solvent, as defined in Rule 410 (k)."

> "(c) A person shall not thin or dilute any architectural coating with a photochemically reactive solvent, as defined in Rule 410 (k)."

> "(d) For the purposes of this Rule, an architectural coating is defined as a coating used for residential or commercial buildings and their appurtenances; or industrial buildings."

**Applicability trigger:** Containers of one quart capacity or larger containing photochemically reactive solvent. Definition of "photochemically reactive solvent" is cross-referenced to Rule 410(k).

### Rule 423 — Disposal and Evaporation of Solvents

> "A person shall not during any one day dispose of a total of more than 1¼ gallons of any photochemically reactive solvent, as defined in Rule 410 (k), or of any material containing more than 1¼ gallons of any such photochemically reactive solvent by any means which will permit the evaporation of such solvent into the atmosphere."

**Threshold:** 1¼ gallons per day of photochemically reactive solvent disposed/evaporated.

> "This Rule shall become effective on January 1, 1974 for all sources which are either in operation, or under construction on June 1, 1972. This Rule shall be effective for all other sources on June 1, 1972."

## Determination Notes

- **Rule 422** is an outright prohibition on photochemically reactive solvents in architectural coatings (containers ≥1 quart). No permit pathway — compliance is achieved by using non-reactive solvent coatings.
- **Rule 423** imposes a hard daily cap of 1.25 gallons of photochemically reactive solvent that may be disposed of by evaporation. Industrial cleaning and degreasing operations that exceed this threshold need engineering controls (closed systems, carbon adsorption, etc.).
- The key defined term "photochemically reactive solvent" is in Rule 410(k) — not extracted here; consult the full rulebook PDF for that definition.
- These rules are older SIP-approved rules (1970s vintage) and do not include the more modern VOC content limits (g/L) found in CARB or larger district rules. For coating VOC content limits beyond the photochemical reactivity prohibition, check current CARB Suggested Control Measure (SCM) for Architectural Coatings.
- No permit-to-operate is triggered by Rules 422/423 alone; violations are operational/operational-conduct violations rather than permit violations.
