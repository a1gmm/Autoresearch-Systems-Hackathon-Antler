## One-Line Decision Guide

**Does the project need an ATC?**
- YES if: any new/modified stationary equipment that "may cause" air emissions — no de minimis tonnage threshold exists in Rules 2:1A or 2:5
- NO if: equipment falls squarely into a Rule 2:5 category (NG/LPG space heating [not boilers], food prep for same-day consumption, agricultural field equipment, self-propelled construction equipment, wood dryers, home appliances, recreational equipment, ICEs, government training programs) AND is not a boiler
- ALWAYS check: even exempt equipment must meet Rule 3 emission limits (PM, opacity, SOx, NOx, organic solvents)
- KEY GRAY AREAS: stationary diesel generators at construction sites (not self-propelled → likely need ATC); HVAC with boilers (explicitly excluded from exemption b); agricultural processing equipment (vs. field equipment)

## District Notes

Shasta County AQMD is a small district in the Sacramento Valley Air Basin. It has a relatively compact rulebook focused on:
1. Permit administration (Rules 2:x)
2. Direct emission standards for specific pollutants (Rules 3:x)
3. Sector-specific rules for gasoline vapors, organic solvents, dry cleaning, asphalt, and agricultural burning

The district does NOT appear to have adopted local coating VOC content rules (the CARB Suggested Control Measure framework) — facilities should check applicable CARB statewide regulations for coating VOC limits. The district relies on Rules 3:4.a/b (mass-based solvent limits) for VOC control from industrial solvent users.
