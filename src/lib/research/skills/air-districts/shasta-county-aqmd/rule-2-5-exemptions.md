## Purpose

Identifies categories of equipment and operations that are exempt from the Authority to Construct and Permit to Operate requirements in Rule 2:1A. Exemptions are discretionary — the Control Officer "may" exempt equipment in the listed categories. Vehicles (per California Vehicle Code) and aircraft are exempt by statute.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Statutory Exemption (first paragraph)

Verbatim from PDF (Rule 2:5, consolidated amended text):

> "There are hereby exempted from the permit requirements vehicles as defined by the Vehicle Code of the State of California and aircraft."

### Discretionary Exemptions (Control Officer "may" exempt)

> "The Control Officer may exempt any kind or type of machines or devices within the following categories:
>
> a. Internal combustion engines.
>
> b. Equipment used exclusively for space heating or air conditioning, other than boilers.
>
> c. Equipment used in the preparation of food products that are intended for human consumption within 24 hours.
>
> d. Steam generators, steam superheaters, water heaters, and closed heat transfer systems that are fired exclusively with one of the following:
>    1. Natural gas;
>    2. Liquefied petroleum gas;
>    3. A combination of natural gas and liquefied petroleum gas.
>
> e. Agricultural equipment used in the preparation of the land, and the planting, tillage and harvesting of field and orchard crops.
>
> f. Self-propelled construction equipment used in land grading, paving, leveling, digging, or other similar operations, other than pavement burners.
>
> g. Home use appliances.
>
> h. Recreational equipment.
>
> i. Dryers for wood and wood products.
>
> j. Any program operated by a governmental agency to train air pollution enforcement personnel. (Added 11/27/78)"

### Rule 3 Interaction

> "Except for subparagraph j, the exemptions set forth above do not supersede the provisions of Rule 3. (Amended 10/1/73 and 11/27/78)"

This means that even exempt equipment must still comply with Rule 3 emission standards (opacity, particulates, SOx, NOx, organic solvents, gasoline vapor recovery, etc.).

## Determination Notes

- "May exempt" language: the exemptions are not automatic rights — the Control Officer has discretion. In practice, the listed categories are routinely exempt but require no formal permit.
- Internal combustion engines (category a) are nominally exempt but must still comply with Rule 3:2 emission limits and may be regulated under CARB mobile source rules.
- Space heating / HVAC (category b) — boilers are explicitly carved out and require permits.
- Natural gas / LPG-fired equipment (category d) — a common exemption path for small combustion units. Only applies to pure NG, LPG, or blends of those two fuels; switching to other fuels would require an ATC.
- Food prep equipment (category c) — limited to items with 24-hour consumption window; longer-term processing equipment (smokehouses, dryers) likely requires a permit.
- Wood dryers (category i) — explicitly exempt; relevant for lumber industry in Shasta County.
- Agricultural field equipment (category e) — tractors, harvesters, irrigation pumps used in-field are exempt; stationary agricultural processing equipment (e.g., grain dryers, packing sheds) is not in this category and may require permits.
- Construction equipment (category f) — only self-propelled equipment; stationary generators and compressors at construction sites are NOT covered by this exemption.
- The Rule 3 override means an "exempt" piece of equipment that emits excessive particulates, opacity, SOx, NOx, or organic solvents can still be ordered to comply or shut down under Rule 3 enforcement.
