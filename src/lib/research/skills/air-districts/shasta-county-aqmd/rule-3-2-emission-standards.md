## Purpose

Sets quantitative emission limits for specific air contaminants from any single source within the district. These limits apply regardless of whether a source holds a permit or is exempt from permit requirements — Rule 2:5 exemptions do not override Rule 3. The rule establishes a two-tier system based on whether the source was constructed or modified before or after July 1, 1986.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### General Prohibition

Verbatim from PDF (Rule 3:2 amended text):

> "No person shall discharge from any single source into the atmosphere contaminants in amounts greater than those designated in Table I of this rule. All emissions are to be measured by methods approved for use by the Air Pollution Control Officer. Any method approved by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency and/or the California Air Resources Board is approved for use by the Air Pollution Control Officer."

### Table I — Maximum Emission Limits

| Pollutant | Constructed/Modified After 7-1-86 | Existing Before 7-1-86 |
|---|---|---|
| **Combustion Particulate Matter** (notes 1,2) | 0.10 gr/dscf | 0.15 gr/dscf |
| **Particulate Matter ≤ 10µ** (PM10) (notes 1,2) | 0.05 gr/dscf | 0.10 gr/dscf |
| **All Other Particulate Matter** (notes 1,2) | 0.15 gr/dscf | 0.15 gr/dscf |

**Process Weight — Particulate Matter (Maximum Hourly Emissions E as function of Process Weight Pt in tons/hour):**

> "a. Less than or Equal to 30 Tons/Hour: E lbs/hr = 4.1Pt^0.67"
>
> "b. Greater than 30 Tons/Hour: E lbs/hr = 55Pt^(1-40/Pt) [note: formula as transcribed from OCR scan of original rule text]"

| Pollutant | Constructed/Modified After 7-1-86 | Existing Before 7-1-86 |
|---|---|---|
| **Oxides of Sulfur (as SO₂)** (notes 1,2,3) | 200 ppm | 300 ppm |
| **Oxides of Nitrogen (as NO₂) — Solid/Liquid Fuels** (notes 1,2,3) | 300 ppm | 400 ppm |
| **Oxides of Nitrogen (as NO₂) — Gaseous Fuels / All Other Processes** (notes 1,2,3) | 250 ppm | 250 ppm |
| **Total Reduced Sulfur (TRS)** | See Table 1-1 (Kraft Pulp Mills) | See Table 1-1 |
| **Opacity** | Ringelmann 0 and/or 40% equivalent opacity per CHSC §41701 | Ringelmann 1 and/or 40% equivalent opacity per CHSC §41701 |

> "*Unless governed by EPA New Source Standards"

### Explanatory Notes (verbatim)

> "1. Calculated at standard conditions: 70°F., one atmosphere, dry gas basis."
>
> "2. When emissions are generated by a combustion process, the gas volume shall be corrected to 12% CO₂ at standard temperature and pressure."
>
> "3. The APCO may specify an appropriate correction and/or reporting factor depending upon the type of process involved."

### TRS Guidelines — Table 1-1 (Kraft Pulp Mills)

> Recovery Furnace — Old Design Furnaces: 20 ppm; New Design Furnaces: 5 ppm
> Cross Recovery Furnaces: 25 ppm
> Digester System: 5 ppm
> Multiple-Effect Evaporator System: 5 ppm
> Lime Kiln: 8 ppm
> Brown Stock Washer System: 5 ppm
> Black Liquor Oxidation System: No Control
> Condensate Stripper System: 5 ppm
> Smelt Dissolving Tank: 0.0084 g/kg BLS
> (Amended 7-15-86)

## Determination Notes

- Rule 3:2 is a backstop — it applies to ALL sources including those exempt from permitting under Rule 2:5.
- The 7-1-86 cutoff date creates a tiered standard: new/modified sources face tighter PM and SOx limits.
- The process weight formula for particulate matter provides a higher absolute tonnage allowance for larger sources but a declining emission rate per ton.
- Opacity standard for post-7-1-86 sources is Ringelmann 0 (no visible emissions) vs. Ringelmann 1 for pre-existing sources — a meaningful distinction for combustion equipment visible from property boundaries.
- The NOx standard for gaseous fuels is uniform at 250 ppm regardless of construction date — no grandfathering for NG/LPG combustors on NOx.
- Federal NSPS (40 CFR Part 60) standards override Table I where more stringent — the asterisk applies particularly to large boilers, cement kilns, and other NSPS-covered categories.
