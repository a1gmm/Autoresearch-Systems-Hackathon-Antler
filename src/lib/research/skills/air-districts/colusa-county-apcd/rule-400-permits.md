## Purpose

Rule 400 establishes the general permit requirements for any person who constructs, modifies, or operates a source of air contaminants in Colusa County. It defines the Authority to Construct (ATC) and Permit to Operate (PTO) requirements administered by the Air Pollution Control Officer (APCO).

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Authority to Construct (Section 2.1)
> "Any person building, erecting, altering or replacing any article, machine, equipment or other contrivance, the use of which may cause the issuance of air contaminants or the use of which may eliminate or reduce or control the issuance of air contaminants, shall first obtain written authorization for such construction from the Air Pollution Control Officer. An authority to construct shall remain in effect until the permit to operate the equipment for which the application was filed is granted or denied. An Authority to Construct shall be canceled in two years from date of issue if the Air Pollution Control Officer determines that substantial construction has not commenced."

### Short-Duration Sources (Section 2.1.1)
> "A stationary source which will be operated at a single location for a period of less than 45 days shall file a notification of operation with the Air Pollution Control Officer prior to the start of the operation."

### Permit to Operate (Section 2.2)
> "Before any article, machine, equipment or other contrivance described in subsection a., above, may be operated or used, or leased or rented for operation or use, a written permit shall be obtained from the Air Pollution Control Officer. No permit to operate or use shall be granted either by the Air Pollution Control Officer or the Hearing Board for any article, machine, equipment or contrivance described in Section 2.1, above, constructed or installed without authorization as required by Section 2.1."

### Title V Permit Sources (Section 2.3)
> "A source subject to Rule 1101 shall obtain a Title V permit to operate issued by the APCO under Title V of the Federal Clean Air Act as amended in 1990. The APCO will issue a Title V permit separately from, and in addition to, the permit Required pursuant to Section 2.2."

### Existing Sources (Section 3)
> "It is required that all owners or operators of existing equipment, machinery, contrivances or places of business that send emissions into the atmosphere, be registered or permitted with the Air Pollution Control District."

### Action Timeline (Section 8)
> "The applicant may, at his option, deem the Authorization to Construct or Permit to Operate, denied if the Air Pollution Control Officer fails to act on the application within thirty (30) days after applicant furnishes the further information, plans and specifications requested by the Air Pollution Control Officer."

### Appeals (Section 9)
> "Within fifteen (15) days after notice by the Air Pollution Control Officer of denial or conditional approval of an Authorization to Construct or a Permit to Operate, the applicant may petition the Hearing Board, in writing, for a public hearing."

### Permit Posting (Section 17)
> "A person who has been granted, under this regulation, a permit to operate any article, machine, equipment, or other contrivance, shall firmly affix such Permit to Operate, any approved facsimile, or other approved identification bearing the permit number upon the article, machine, equipment or other contrivance in such a manner as to be clearly visible and accessible."

## Determination Notes

- **Trigger:** Any construction, modification, or operation of equipment that may emit air contaminants requires an ATC before construction and a PTO before operation.
- **Exemptions:** See Rule 401 for the exhaustive list of exempt equipment categories.
- **NSR overlay:** New or modified sources with potential to emit above Rule 430 thresholds also trigger Best Available Control Technology (BACT) and offset requirements.
- **Title V overlay:** Major sources (Rule 1101) require a separate Title V operating permit in addition to the standard PTO.
- **Short-duration sources** (< 45 days at one location) file a notification rather than a full permit.
- APCO has 30 days to act on a complete application; applicant may appeal conditional approvals or denials to the Hearing Board within 15 days.
