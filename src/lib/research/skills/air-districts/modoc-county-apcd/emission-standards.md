## Purpose

Sets operational emission limits that apply to permitted and exempt sources alike. Covers particulate matter (process weight-based), visible emissions (Ringelmann), fuel sulfur content, open burning prohibitions, and gasoline storage tank vapor control. These are the substantive standards a permitted source must meet under Modoc County APCD's Regulation III and IV rules.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Rule 4.1 — Visible Emissions (Ringelmann Limit, Regulation IV)

> "Except as provided in these regulations, a person shall not discharge into the atmosphere from any single source of emission whatsoever, any air contaminant for a period or periods aggregating more than three minutes in any one hour which is:
> A. as dark or darker in shade as that designated as No. 2 on the Ringelmann chart as published by the United States Bureau of Mines.
> B. of such opacity as to obscure an observer's view to a degree equal to or greater than does smoke described in subsection A. of this rule."

Exceptions to Rule 4.1 include (among others):

> "9. smoke emissions from teepee burners operating in compliance with Section 4438 of the Public Resources Code during the disposal of forestry and agricultural residues or forestry and agricultural residues with supplementary fossil fuels when such emissions result from the startup or shutdown of the combustion process or from the malfunction of emission control equipment. This exception shall not apply to emissions which exceed a period or periods of time aggregating more than 30 minutes in any 24-hour period."

> "D. When the presence of uncombined water is the only reason for the failure of an emission to meet the visible emissions limitation (Rule 4.1), that rule shall not apply. The burden of proof which establishes the application of this exception shall be upon the person seeking to come within its provisions."

### Rule 3:10 — Sulfur Content of Fuels (Regulation III)

> "No person shall burn within the District any gaseous fuel containing sulfur compound in excess of 50 grains per 100 cubic feet of gaseous fuel, calculated as hydrogen sulfide at standard conditions, or any liquid fuel or solid fuel having sulfur content in excess of 0.5 percent by weight."

Exemptions from the sulfur rule include:

> "The provisions of this rule shall not apply to: [fuels used to incinerate waste gases where the fuel itself does not contain sulfur compounds in excess of the rule]; b. The use of solid fuels in any metallurgical process. c. The use of fuels where the gaseous products of combustion are used as raw materials for other processes. d. The use of liquid or solid fuels to propel or test any vehicle, aircraft, missiles, locomotive, boat or ship. e. The use of liquid fuel whenever the supply of gaseous fuel, the burning of which is permitted by this rule, is not physically available to the user due to accident, act of God, act of war, act of the public enemy, or failure of the supplier. f. The use of liquid fuel during a period [when] the supply of gaseous fuel, the burning of which is not prohibited by this Rule, interrupts the delivery of [such] fuel to the user."

### Rule 3:14 — Combination of Emissions (Regulation III)

> "If air contaminants from two or more source operations are combined prior to emission and there are adequate and reliable means reasonably susceptible to confirmation and use by the control officer for establishing a separation of the components of the emission arising from each such source operation, this Regulation shall apply to each such source operation separately."

> "If air contaminants from two or more source operations are combined prior to emission, and the combined emissions cannot [be separated per the requirements of Rule 3:14(a)], this [Regulation shall apply to the combined source operations] as if they are so [combined with respect to their] air contaminants [combined]."

### Rule 3:16 — Gasoline Storage (Vapor Control, Regulation III)

> "(Section 39068.2 — It is a misdemeanor to install any stationary storage tank having a capacity of 250 gallons or more for the storage of any petroleum distillate having a Reid vapor pressure of four psi or greater, unless the tank is equipped with one of the following emission control systems:
> 1) a permanent submerged fill pipe (Section 39068.2(e));
> 2) a vapor recovery system (Section 39068.4);
> 3) a floating roof (not acceptable if the vapor pressure of the contents exceeds 11 psi) (Section 39068.3); or
> 5) control equipment of efficiency equal to that specified above, which has been approved by the air pollution control officer."

> "These requirements are not applicable to stationary storage tanks which are used primarily for the fueling of implements of husbandry (Section 39068.2f)."

### Rule 3:7 / Rule 3:8 — Open Outdoor Fire Prohibition and Exceptions (Regulation III)

> "No person, firm, corporation, association or public agency shall ignite, cause to be ignited, permit to be ignited, or suffer, allow, or maintain any open outdoor fire within the District, except as provided in this Regulation."

Named exceptions (Rule 3:8) include: cooking/recreational fires; household rubbish fires at 1-2 family dwellings; fires set by public officers for weed abatement or hazard prevention; safety flares for waste gas combustion; agricultural fires permitted by the County Agricultural Commissioner for disease/pest prevention, crop residue, stubble, brush control, or watershed improvement; and fires where only CO2, NO2, or water vapor is emitted.

## Determination Notes

- Modoc County is primarily rural/agricultural. The operative emission standards are the classic 1970s-era California rules: Ringelmann No. 2 visible emissions and a process weight-rate table for particulate. No VOC-specific coating rule or solvent-use rule is present in the district rulebook.
- **Coating/surface-coating operations**: no local rule. Operators are subject to the general process weight rate particulate limit (Rule 3:12) and Ringelmann visible emissions (Rule 4.1). Large VOC emitters would need to comply with CARB statewide consumer products / coating regulations.
- **Fuel sulfur**: 0.5% by weight for liquid/solid fuels; 50 grains/100 scf for gaseous fuels. This is relevant to diesel generators and industrial boilers.
- **Gasoline tank vapor control** kicks in at **250 gallons** capacity with Reid vapor pressure ≥ 4 psi — submerged fill, vapor recovery, or floating roof required. Farm tanks for implements of husbandry are exempt.
- **Open burns** require a daily burn-day permit check through Cal Fire / CARB; the APCO can issue special permits for no-burn days in cases of imminent economic loss.
