## Purpose / Applicability

Rule 321 applies to any person who owns, operates, or uses solvent cleaning equipment or performs solvent cleaning outside a machine during production/repair/maintenance work at any stationary source.

> "This rule shall apply to any person who owns, operates, or uses any solvent cleaning machine or performs any solvent cleaning operation outside of a solvent cleaning machine during the production, repair, maintenance, or servicing of parts, products, tools, machinery, equipment, or in general work areas at any stationary source."

Last adopted/revised: **June 21, 2012**

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Exemptions (Section B)

**Low-VOC / low-TAC solvents (B.1):**

> "Any solvent cleaning machine equipped with and any solvent cleaning performed with a solvent (including emulsions) that contains two percent by weight or less of each of the following:
> a. Reactive organic compounds (as determined by Environmental Protection Agency method 24), and
> b. Toxic air contaminants (as determined by generic solvent data, solvent manufacturer's composition data or by a gas chromatography test and a mass spectrometry test)."

Recordkeeping required for this exemption.

**Small-quantity exemption (B.15):**

> "Provisions of Section M.1 shall not apply if the net aggregate amount of solvent used for all solvent cleaning subject to Rule 321 ... at a stationary source does not exceed 55 gallons per year. Solvents with a reactive organic compound content of 50 grams per liter of material or less do not count towards the 55 gallons per year aggregate limit."

**Aerosol products (B.9):**

> "Solvent cleaning with aerosol products shall not be subject to Section D.9 and Section M.1 provisions ... provided:
> a. 160 fluid ounces or less of aerosol products are used per day, per facility..."
and aerosol products comply with VOC limits for consumer products.

**Janitorial cleaning (B.7):** Janitorial cleaning, including graffiti removal, is fully exempt.

**Health care facilities (B.14):** Solvent cleaning to disinfect/decontaminate in hospitals and medical facilities is exempt.

**Rules that supersede Rule 321 (B.6):** Operations covered by the following rules are exempt from Rule 321 (those rules govern instead):
- Rule 325 (Crude Oil Production)
- Rule 326 (Storage of ROC Liquids)
- Rule 330 (Surface Coating of Metal Parts)
- Rule 337 (Surface Coating of Aerospace Vehicles)
- Rule 339 (Motor Vehicle/Mobile Equipment Coating)
- Rule 343 (Petroleum Storage Tank Degassing)
- Rule 344 (Petroleum Sumps, Pits, Well Cellars)
- Rule 349 (Polyester Resin Operations)
- Rule 351 (Surface Coating of Wood Products)
- Rule 353 (Adhesives and Sealants)
- Rule 354 (Graphic Arts)

**Specialty exemptions (B.8):** Cleaning of solar cells, laser hardware, scientific instruments, high-precision optics, telescopes, microscopes, avionic equipment, and aerospace/military fluid systems; laboratory testing; quality assurance applications.

**Low-ROC solvent cleaning (B.13):**

> "Solvent cleaning with a solvent containing 50 grams of reactive organic compounds per liter of material or less shall not be subject to the Section D.9 provision."

### Section M.1 — Equipment and Solvent Requirements Table

Section M.1 sets specific solvent VOC content limits and equipment requirements by type of solvent cleaning machine (batch vapor, in-line, cold cleaner, etc.). The 55-gallon/year threshold (B.15) provides a small-source safe harbor from M.1 requirements.

### Solvent Atomization Prohibition (Section D.9 / M.2.c)

Solvents may not be atomized during cleaning operations, with limited exceptions (aerosol products ≤ 160 fl oz/day, hand-held spray bottles ≤ 1 liter, nozzle tip cleaning for automated spray equipment, gas turbine/jet engine gas-path cleaners).

## Determination Notes

- **First check B.15 (55-gal/year):** If the facility uses ≤ 55 gal/year of ROC-containing solvents for cleaning, the M.1 equipment requirements do not apply — only basic recordkeeping is needed.
- **Low-VOC solvents (B.1):** Solvents with ≤ 2% ROC and ≤ 2% TAC by weight are fully exempt — no equipment standards apply.
- **Cross-rule check:** If the cleaning is incidental to a process governed by Rules 325, 330, 337, 339, 349, 351, 353, or 354, those rules control; Rule 321 does not apply unless those rules specifically invoke Rule 321.
- **Permit trigger:** Rule 321 is a prohibitory rule (emission standards); whether the equipment itself requires a Rule 201 permit depends on Rule 202 exemptions. Small cleaning operations using ≤ 55 gal/year of coatings and solvents combined may also qualify for the Rule 202 Section I coating/solvent exemption (55-gal/year, 10-ton aggregate limit).
- **Records:** All exemption claims require records of solvent use maintained ≥ 3 years, available to District on request.
