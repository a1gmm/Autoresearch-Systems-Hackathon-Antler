## Purpose / Applicability

Rule 202 lists all categories of equipment, operations, and activities that are exempt from the permitting requirement in Rule 201. From the rule text:

> "An Authority to Construct or Permit to Operate shall not be required for equipment, operations and activities described herein."

Last adopted/revised: **August 25, 2016**

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Exceptions That Override Exemptions (Section B)

Two categories are never exempt, regardless of other exemption language:

> "1. Equipment, activity or operations proposed by an applicant for use as an Emission Reduction Credit is not exempt."
> "2. Emission unit that functions for distributed electrical generation and is not certified under the regulations of the Air Resources Board is not exempt."

### Recordkeeping Requirement for All Exemptions (Section D.1)

> "The owner or operator shall maintain records which clearly demonstrate that the exemption threshold has not been exceeded. These records shall be made available to the District upon request and shall be maintained for a minimum of three calendar years. Failure to maintain records which meet the above requirements or exceedance of the emission exemption threshold or violation of any District rule may result in the immediate loss of the permit exemption."

### Agricultural Source Exemption (Section D.3)

> "A permit shall not be required for an agricultural operation at a stationary source that, in aggregate, produces actual emissions less than all of the following:
> a. 50 percent of any applicable emission threshold for a major source in the District,
> b. 5 tons per year of any single Hazardous Air Pollutant, and
> c. 12.5 tons per year of combined Hazardous Air Pollutants."

### Temporary Equipment Exemption (Section D.5)

> "A permit shall not be required for temporary equipment where the projected actual aggregate emissions of all affected pollutants do not exceed 1 ton (except carbon monoxide, which shall not exceed 5 tons) and the use of each individual piece of equipment does not exceed one 60 day period in any consecutive 12 month period."

Owner/operator must submit a written request to the Control Officer before use.

### De Minimis Exemption (Section D.6)

> "Any physical change in an existing stationary source that meets all of the requirements below is exempt."
>
> "a. The emission increase for any one emission unit shall not exceed 2.40 pounds per day of any affected pollutant, except carbon monoxide, which shall not exceed 19.20 pounds per day."
>
> "b. The aggregate emissions increase at the stationary source due to all de minimis physical changes at the stationary source shall not exceed 24.00 pounds per day, except carbon monoxide, which shall not exceed 60.00 pounds per day."

Additional conditions: no change to control equipment; not subject to ATCM, NSPS, NESHAP, or HAP requirements; recordkeeping required.

### Stationary Source Permit Exemption (Section D.7) — "1-ton rule"

> "A permit shall not be required for any new, modified or existing stationary source if the uncontrolled actual emissions of each individual affected pollutant from the entire stationary source are below 1.00 ton per calendar year, unless:"

Exceptions to this 1-ton exemption:
- Subject to EPA-promulgated NSPS or NESHAP, or federal operating permit program (40 CFR Part 70)
- Subject to CARB Airborne Toxic Control Measure
- Subject to public notification or risk reduction under CA H&S Code Section 44300 et seq.
- Control Officer determines permit is necessary to ensure emissions remain below one ton/year
- New or modified source emitting hazardous air emissions within 1,000 feet of a school site

Written request to Control Officer required; applicant pays applicable fee.

### Routine Repair and Maintenance (Section D.8)

> "A permit shall not be required for routine repair or maintenance of permitted equipment, not involving structural changes."

### Equivalent Routine Replacement (Section D.9)

> "A permit shall not be required for equivalent routine replacement in whole or in part of any article, machine, equipment or other contrivance where a Permit to Operate had previously been granted under Rule 201 ... providing emissions are not increased and there is no potential for violating any ambient air quality standard."

Owner must notify District within 30 days (unless replacement is identical make/model and routine).

### Overriding Thresholds — Toxic/Hazardous Pollutants (Section D.10)

Regardless of any other exemption, no source is exempt if potential to emit exceeds:

> "a. 3.28 pounds per day of lead
> b. 0.04 pounds per day of asbestos
> c. 0.0022 pounds per day of beryllium
> d. 0.55 pounds per day of mercury
> e. 5.48 pounds per day of vinyl chloride
> f. 16.44 pounds per day of fluorides
> g. 38.45 pounds per day of sulfuric acid mist, or
> h. 54.79 pounds per day of total reduced sulfur or reduced sulfur compounds.
> i. 0.0000035 tons per year municipal waste combustor organics.
> j. 15 tons per year municipal waste combustor metals.
> k. 40 tons per year municipal waste combustor acid gases."

### Internal Combustion Engines (Section F)

Key engine exemptions:
- **Compression ignition (diesel) engines < 50 BHP** (Section F.1.e)
- **Spark ignition engines < 50 BHP** — unless aggregate at source totals ≥ 400 BHP (Section F.1.f)
- **Gas turbines ≤ 3 MMBtu/hr heat input** (Section F.1.g)
- **Emergency SI engines ≤ 200 hrs/year** for emergency power or flood/fire pumping (Section F.1.d)
- **Portable engines registered in Statewide Registration Program** (Section F.2)

### Combustion Equipment — Non-Engine (Section G)

> "Combustion equipment with a maximum heat input of less than or equal to two (2) million British thermal units per hour is exempt from permit requirements if fired exclusively with [natural gas, LPG, or combination]."

> "Combustion equipment with a maximum heat input rate of 1 million British thermal units per hour or less is exempt and does not count towards the 25 tons per calendar year stationary source exemption threshold..."

Aggregate emissions from all equipment in the category must not exceed 25 tons per calendar year of any affected pollutant.

### Abrasive Blast Equipment (Section H)

Exempt items include blast cabinet/filter combinations ≤ 50 cubic feet and portable blast equipment. Aggregate limit: 10 tons/year per pollutant.

### Coating Application Equipment (Section I)

> "Equipment used in surface coating operations provided that the total amount of coatings and solvents used does not exceed 55 gallons per year."

Solvents with ROC content ≤ 50 g/L do not count toward the 55-gallon limit. Aggregate limit: 10 tons/year per pollutant.

Other exempt coating operations:
- Dipping with oils/waxes/greases (no organic solvents)
- Air brushing
- Powder coating (ROC content ≤ 5% by weight)
- Unheated non-conveyorized dip tanks ≤ 100 gallons

### Architectural Coating (Section D.14)

> "Application of architectural coating in the repair and maintenance of a stationary structure is exempt from permit requirements."

## Determination Notes

- **Work down the exemption hierarchy:** Check Section D.7 (1-ton source-wide rule) first — it is the broadest; if total source emissions < 1 ton/year of each pollutant, no permit needed (subject to carve-outs).
- **De minimis (D.6):** For project-level changes at an already-permitted source where the increase per unit < 2.40 lb/day and source aggregate increase < 24 lb/day.
- **Toxic overrides (D.10):** Always check regardless of which exemption is claimed.
- **Engine rules (F):** Diesel < 50 BHP and gas turbine ≤ 3 MMBtu/hr are the most common field exemptions.
- **Coating shops (I):** ≤ 55 gal/year of coatings+solvents (ROC-containing) is the operative bright line for small shops.
- Written requests to the Control Officer are required for several exemptions (D.5, D.7, F.5, F.7) — factor 30–90 day lead times into project schedules.
- All exemptions require recordkeeping for ≥ 3 years; loss of records = loss of exemption.
