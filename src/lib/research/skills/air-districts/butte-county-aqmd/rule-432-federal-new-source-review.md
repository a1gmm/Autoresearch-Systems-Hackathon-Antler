## Purpose

Rule 432 establishes nonattainment air pollutant pre-construction review requirements for new major stationary sources and major modifications. It requires Best Available Control Technology (BACT), offsets, and air quality impact analysis.

> "The purpose of this Rule is to establish nonattainment air pollutants preconstruction review requirements for new major stationary sources and major modifications for use of Best Available Control Technology (BACT), offsets, and analysis of air quality impacts, and to ensure that the operation of such sources does not interfere with the attainment or maintenance of ambient air quality standards, and complies with all other applicable requirements." (Section 1.1)

Adopted September 28, 2006; last amended April 22, 2021.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Major Stationary Source Definition
> "Any stationary source which emits, or has the potential to emit, 100 tpy or more of any nonattainment regulated NSR pollutant. A major stationary source for nitrogen oxides or VOCs shall also be considered a major source for ozone." (Section 4.21/4.20)

Emissions from units exempt under Rule 401 are not counted toward major source status unless the unit emits greater than 2 pounds per day of any pollutant.

### Major Modification Thresholds — Significant Net Emissions Increase
> - "Carbon monoxide: 100 tons per year (tpy)" (4.19.1)
> - "Nitrogen oxides: 40 tpy" (4.19.2)
> - "Sulfur dioxide: 40 tpy" (4.19.3)
> - "Ozone: 40 tpy of Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs) or 40 tpy of nitrogen oxides" (4.19.4)
> - "PM10: 15 tpy" (4.19.5)
> - "PM2.5: 10 tpy of direct PM2.5 emissions or 40 tpy of sulfur dioxide emissions or 40 tpy of nitrogen oxide emissions" (4.19.6)
> - "Lead: 0.6 tpy" (4.19.7)

### BACT / LAER
> "Best Available Control Technology (BACT): The most stringent emissions limitation or control technique of the following: Achieved in practice for such class and category of source; or Contained in any SIP approved by the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) for such class and category of source ... or Contained in an applicable New Source Performance Standard (NSPS); or Any other emission control device or technique, alternative basic equipment, different fuel or process, determined to be technologically feasible and cost-effective by the APCO for such a class or category of sources." (Section 4.7)

> "Lowest Achievable Emission Rate (LAER): The same as BACT as defined in this Rule." (Section 4.19/4.18)

### Applicability
> "This Rule shall apply to all new major stationary sources and major modifications which are subject to Butte County Air Quality Management District (DISTRICT) Rule 400-Permit Requirements." (Section 2.1)

Not applicable to portable, temporary, or replacement units unless they are major sources or cause a major modification at an existing major source (Section 2.3).

### Allowable Emissions Definition
> "The emissions rate of a stationary source calculated using the maximum rated capacity of the emission unit or source, unless the emission unit or source is limited by federally enforceable conditions which restrict operating rate or hours or both, and the most stringent of the following: Any applicable standards set forth in 40 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) Parts 60, 61, or 63; Any applicable current or future emission limitation in the State Implementation Plan (SIP); or A current or future emission rate specified in a federally enforceable permit condition." (Section 4.3)

## Determination Notes

- Rule 432 applies only to MAJOR sources (≥100 tpy any nonattainment NSR pollutant) or projects causing a MAJOR MODIFICATION (significant net emissions increase as defined above).
- Most small-to-mid industrial facilities in Butte County will be subject to Rule 400/401 for permits, but NOT Rule 432 (which is reserved for large sources).
- Butte County is in nonattainment for ozone (NOx, VOC) and PM2.5. These are the most commonly triggered pollutants under Rule 432.
- The 40 tpy NOx/VOC threshold for major modification means a new source or modification adding fewer than 40 tpy VOC/NOx at a major facility does not trigger FNSR.
- Offsets (Emission Reduction Credits) are required for major modifications; Rule 431 governs ERCs and banking.
