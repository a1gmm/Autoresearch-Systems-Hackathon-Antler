## Purpose

Rule 2.12(f) establishes VOC content limits for architectural coatings sold or applied in Butte County. This is the SIP-approved rule governing coatings applied to stationary structures, mobile homes, pavements, and curbs. It applies to manufacturers, sellers, and applicators.

Note: This is an older SIP rule adopted as part of Resolution 79-55. Modern architectural coating VOC limits in California are largely governed by CARB's Architectural Coatings Suggested Control Measure (SCM) and statewide regulations; however, this district rule remains in the EPA-approved SIP for Butte County.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Definitions (Section 1)

> "For the purpose of this rule, an architectural coating is defined as any coating applied to stationary structures and their appurtenances, to mobile homes, to pavements, or to curbs." (Section 1.a)

Key coating types defined include: Graphic Arts Coatings ("coatings which are marketed solely for application to indoor and outdoor signs and include lettering enamels, poster colors and bulletin colors"), Industrial Maintenance Finishes, Metallic Pigmented Paints, Primers, Sealers, Semitransparent Stains, Varnishes/Lacquers/Shellacs, Waterproofing Coatings, and Wood Preservatives.

### VOC Limits (Section 2)

> "No person shall sell, offer for sale, or apply any architectural coating manufactured after July 26, 1980 which:
> a. contains more than 250 grams of volatile organic material per liter of coating as applied, excluding water, except as provided in subsection b of this section.
> b. contains more than 350 grams of volatile organic material per liter of coating as applied, excluding water, and is recommended solely for use on interior surfaces. Interior coatings manufactured after July 26, 1982 may not contain more than 450 grams of volatile organic material per liter of coating as applied, excluding water.
> c. is recommended for use as a bituminous pavement sealer unless it is an emulsion type coating." (Section 2)

### Exemptions (Section 5)

The following coating types are exempt from Section 2 VOC limits (if manufactured prior to July 26, 1984):
> "a. architectural coatings supplied in containers having capacities of one liter or less;
> b. traffic coatings applied to public streets and highways; however, this exemption shall not extend to traffic coatings applied to other surfaces, including, but not limited to curbs, berms, driveways and parking lots.
> c. architectural coatings recommended by the manufacturer for use solely as a:
> 1) varnish, lacquer, or shellac
> 2) semitransparent stain
> 3) opaque stain on bare redwood, cedar, mahogany, and douglas fir
> 4) primer, sealer, or undercoater
> 5) wood preservative
> 6) fire retardant coating
> 7) tile-like glaze coating
> 8) waterproofing coating, except bituminous pavement sealers
> 9) industrial maintenance finish
> 10) metallic pigmented coatings
> 11) swimming pool coating
> 12) graphic arts coatings" (Section 5)

### Export and Small Business Exemptions
Coatings sold for shipment outside the district are exempt (Section 3). Small businesses (as defined by <200,000 gallons of coatings sold in 1976) had phased compliance exemptions for coatings manufactured prior to July 26, 1984 (Section 4).

### Labeling (Section 7)
If a container indicates a coating may be used for any purpose other than an exempt purpose listed in Section 5, the exemption does not apply. Where multiple standards could apply, the most restrictive applies.

## Determination Notes

- This rule governs VOC content of architectural coatings at point of sale and application — it is a product-content rule, not a permit-triggering rule. Applying architectural coatings to structures is also categorically exempt from permit requirements under Rule 401 Section 4.7.4.
- The 250 g/L (general) and 350/450 g/L (interior) limits are the baseline SIP limits. California has since moved to tighter limits under CARB's SCM; check whether CARB statewide limits supersede this for current compliance.
- Graphic arts coatings (signs, lettering enamels) are exempt from the architectural coatings VOC limits under Section 5.c.12 — but graphic arts operations are separately addressed under Rule 401 Section 4.12 (permit exemption at <15 lb VOC/day and <300 lb VOC/year).
- No permit from Butte County AQMD is required to sell or apply architectural coatings; compliance is demonstrated by meeting the g/L thresholds and labeling requirements.
