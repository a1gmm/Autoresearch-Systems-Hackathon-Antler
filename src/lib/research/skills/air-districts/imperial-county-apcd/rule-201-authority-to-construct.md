## Purpose

Rule 201 establishes the fundamental requirement that any person constructing, modifying, or operating equipment that emits or controls air contaminants must first obtain written authorization (Authority to Construct, ATC) and then a Permit to Operate (PTO) from the Imperial County APCO. This is the primary permit trigger rule under Regulation II.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

Verbatim from Rule 201 (Adopted prior to 10/15/79; Revised 9/14/99; 10/10/2006):

**Section A — Authority to Construct:**
> "Each Person constructing, erecting, installing, modifying, or replacing any article, machine, Equipment or contrivance, the use of which may emit or control Air Contaminants, shall first obtain written authorization for such Construction from the Air Pollution Control Officer, except as may be exempted herein."

**Section B — Permit to Operate:**
> "Subject to the exemptions contained in Rule 202 of this part, each Person who uses or operates any article, machine, Equipment, or other contrivance that emits or controls Air Contaminants is required to have a permit. A single Permit to Operate may be issued for all components of an integrated system or process."

**Section C — Other Permits:**
> "Agricultural Burning permits are provided for in Rule 701. Large Confined Animal Facilities are provided for in Rule 217 and Rule 420. Non-Agricultural Burning permits are provided for in Rule 421."

**Section D — Posting of Permit:**
> "A Person who has been granted a Permit to Operate any article, machine, Equipment, or other contrivance described in this Rule, shall firmly affix such permit, an approved facsimile, or other approved identification bearing the permit number upon the article, machine, Equipment, or other contrivance in such a manner as to be clearly visible and accessible…"

## Determination Notes

- ATC required BEFORE construction/installation/modification of any emission source or control device.
- PTO required to OPERATE any emission source (exemptions listed in Rule 202).
- Rule 201 cross-references Rule 202 for equipment exempt from permitting.
- The ATC and PTO are separate actions; an ATC is the preconstruction authorization while a PTO authorizes ongoing operation.
- New Source Review requirements (BACT, offsets) are layered on top via Rule 207 for larger sources.
- For a new project, confirm: (1) Does the equipment fall under a Rule 202 exemption? (2) If not, ATC required before any construction; PTO required before operation.
