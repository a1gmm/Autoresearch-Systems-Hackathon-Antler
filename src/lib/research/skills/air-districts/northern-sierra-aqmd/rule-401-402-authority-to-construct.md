## Purpose

Rule 401 requires any person building, altering, or replacing any source of air contaminants to first obtain an Authority to Construct (ATC) from the Air Pollution Control Officer (APCO) before beginning construction. Rule 402 lists categories of equipment exempt from that requirement.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Rule 401 — Permit Required (verbatim)

> "Any person building, altering, or replacing any source of air contaminants shall first obtain an Authority to Construct from the Air Pollution Control Officer. An Authority to Construct shall remain in effect until the Permit to Operate for that source for which the application was filed is either granted or denied or until termination pursuant to other provisions of this Regulation."

> "Major sources subject to Title V of the Clean Air Act of 1990, shall also comply with the requirements of Rule 522 Title V - Federal Operating Permits."

*Amended 05/11/94*

### Rule 402 — Exemptions to Rule 401 (verbatim)

> "Unless required or allowed elsewhere within the District rules, or by a ARB Airborne Toxic Control Measure, or any other State or Federal regulation, an Authority to Construct shall not be required for:"

**Section A — Categorical exemptions:**

> "A. (1) Any vehicle.
> (2) Any structure designed for and used exclusively as a dwelling for not more than four families.
> (3) An incinerator used exclusively in connection with a structure as described in A.(2).
> (4) Barbecue equipment that is not used for commercial purposes.
> (5) (a) Repairs or maintenance not involving structural changes to any equipment for which a Permit to Operate has been granted. (b) As used in this subdivision, maintenance does not include operation.
> (6) Nothing in this section shall affect any requirements imposed on a district or a source of air pollution, including, but not limited to, an agricultural source, pursuant to the federal Clean Air Act (42 U.S.C. Sec. 7401 et seq.)."

**Section B — Toxic prioritization / emissions threshold:**

> "B. Any facility or source with a toxic prioritization score less than 1 as determined by the District and that emits less than 1000 pounds per year of any criteria pollutant, precursor or toxic air contaminant."

**Section C — Small combustion equipment (natural gas / LPG):**

> "C. Steam generators, steam superheaters, water boilers, water heaters, and closed heat transfer systems that have a maximum heat input rate of less than 3,000,000 British Thermal Units (BTU) per hour gross, and are fired exclusively with one of the following:
> (1) Natural gas;
> (2) Liquefied petroleum gas;
> (3) A combination of natural gas and liquefied petroleum gas."

**Section D — Low-emission miscellaneous sources:**

> "D. Other sources that have not been deemed a nuisance or potential nuisance by the APCO and emit less than 1000 pounds per year of any one criteria pollutant or precursor or emit less than 2000 pounds of any combination thereof as determined by the APCO."

*Amended 06/27/05*

## Determination Notes

- **Key threshold for Section B and D exemptions:** < 1,000 lb/yr of any single criteria pollutant or TAC, AND toxic prioritization score < 1 (Section B), OR < 1,000 lb/yr single / < 2,000 lb/yr combined and not a nuisance (Section D).
- Small boilers/heaters under 3 MMBtu/hr on natural gas or LPG are categorically exempt (Section C) — this covers most commercial HVAC and process heating.
- Residential structures of ≤ 4 units are fully exempt (Section A.2).
- Maintenance (not operation) of already-permitted equipment is exempt (Section A.5).
- ARB ATCMs can override exemptions — always check applicable ATCM (Regulation IX) before concluding exempt.
- Major sources (Title V) face additional federal requirements under Rule 522.
