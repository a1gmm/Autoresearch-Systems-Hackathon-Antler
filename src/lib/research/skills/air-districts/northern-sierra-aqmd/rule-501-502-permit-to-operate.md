## Purpose

Rule 501 requires a Permit to Operate (PTO) before any source may be operated. Rule 502 cross-references the Authority to Construct exemptions in Rule 402, applying the same exemption list to the operating permit requirement.

## Key Provisions / Thresholds

### Rule 501 — Permit Required (verbatim)

> "Before any source may be operated, a Permit to Operate shall be obtained from the Air Pollution Control Officer. No Permit to Operate shall be granted either by the Air Pollution Control Officer or the Hearing Board for any source constructed or modified without authorization as required in Regulation IV until the information required is provided to the Air Pollution Control Officer and such source is altered, if necessary, and made to conform to the standards set forth in Regulation IV and elsewhere in these Rules and Regulations."

> "Major sources subject to Title V of the Clean Air Act of 1990, shall comply with the requirements of Rule 522 Title V - Federal Operating Permits."

*Amended 05/11/94*

### Rule 502 — Exemptions to Rule 501 (verbatim)

> "The Air Pollution Control Officer may exempt from the requirements of Rule 501 any item of equipment specified in Rule 402, Exemptions to Rule 401."

*Amended 05/11/94*

## Determination Notes

- Rule 502 is a single-sentence rule that entirely delegates its exemption list to Rule 402. See `rule-401-402-authority-to-construct.md` for the complete exemption thresholds.
- The two-step permit sequence (ATC → PTO) is sequential: a source cannot obtain a PTO if it was constructed without a required ATC.
- Rule 522 (Title V Federal Operating Permits) applies to major sources as defined under the Clean Air Act — typically ≥ 100 tpy for attainment areas, ≥ 50 tpy for some nonattainment pollutants.
- Rule 526 (Limiting Potential to Emit) allows sources to accept enforceable operating limitations to avoid major source thresholds.
