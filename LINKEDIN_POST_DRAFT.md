## Version A — story-led (recommended)

Ask any EHS team how they figure out which environmental, health & safety rules apply to a new manufacturing change, and you'll hear the same thing: weeks of cross-referencing regulations by hand, and a quiet fear of the one obligation you missed.

This weekend at the **Antler hackathon**, our team built the **EHS Permit-Navigator** — an AI agent swarm that turns a facility or project change into a *defensible regulatory applicability matrix*.

You describe a change (we focused on Southern California manufacturing). The swarm fans out, determines which EHS obligations apply, and — this is the part I'm most proud of — **proves every single row with current source evidence**. When the evidence isn't there, it doesn't guess. It **fails closed** and flags the gap instead of inventing a confident-sounding answer.

A few things made it real in 72 hours:
🔹 OpenAI Agents SDK for a code-first agent loop with structured outputs and guardrails
🔹 Modal for parallel research fan-out across many sources at once
🔹 An eval-driven approach — we built golden cases from real failure modes, not vibes

The lesson I'm taking home: in regulated domains, "looks right" isn't good enough. The bar is *traceable to a source, or it doesn't ship.* Designing for honest uncertainty turned out to be the feature, not the footnote.

Huge thanks to my teammates and to Antler for the weekend. We're keeping at it — if you work in EHS, regulatory compliance, or evidence-grounded AI agents, I'd genuinely love to compare notes. 👇

## Version C — technical / builder audience

How do you make an AI agent you'd actually trust in a regulated domain? Our answer from the **Antler hackathon**: make it *prove its work* and *refuse to bluff*.

We built the **EHS Permit-Navigator** — an agent swarm that converts a facility/project change into an EHS regulatory applicability matrix. Design principles:

1️⃣ Every determination is a row with a citation to current source evidence — no orphan claims.
2️⃣ Incomplete evidence → fail closed. The system surfaces the gap rather than hallucinating compliance.
3️⃣ Human-review research support, not autonomous filing. The agent does the legwork; people make the call.

Stack: OpenAI Agents SDK (agent loop, structured outputs, guardrails, traces) · Modal (parallel research fan-out + worker isolation) · trace replay + failure-driven evals to keep quality honest.

The hardest — and most interesting — engineering wasn't getting an answer. It was getting the system to know, and admit, when it *didn't* have one.

Grateful to my teammates and to Antler. More to come. 🚀
